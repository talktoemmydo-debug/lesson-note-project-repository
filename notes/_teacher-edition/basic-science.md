**Weeks:** 1–6, 8–9 (Week 7 Mid-Term, 10 Revision, 11 Examination — no notes).
**Depth floor:** NERDC 2025 · Nursery 3 · Basic Science and Technology · First Term, pp 491–497.

### WEEK 1 — OUR BODY

**Reference:** NERDC NURSERY 3 · Basic Science and Technology · First Term W1 (pp 491–494)
**Objectives.** By the end of the lesson pupils should be able to (1) point to the head, neck, trunk,
arms and legs on themselves; (2) name the parts of the head, of the trunk and of the limbs; (3) say
what two body parts do; (4) state that their body is special and different from every other body.
**Previous knowledge.** Pupils come to school each day using their eyes, legs and hands; they have
sung body-part rhymes at home.
**Materials.** Body chart, a doll, two hand mirrors, a cap, a wrist-watch, a toy telephone.

**Step 1 — Our body is everything about us.** Teacher holds up the doll: "This is a body. Everybody
has a body. Your body is all of you — from the top of your head to the tips of your toes." Pupils
touch their head, then their toes. Our body is what we use to see, hear, run, jump, eat, hold and play.

**Step 2 — The five big parts.** Teacher points on the chart and the class says after him: **head,
neck, trunk, arms, legs**. Head is on top and is round — inside it is the brain. The neck joins the
head to the body. The trunk is the middle. The arms are at the sides, the legs below.

**Step 3 — The parts we can count.** Head: **eyes, nose, ears, mouth, hair**. Trunk: **chest,
stomach, back, waist**. Limbs: **shoulders, elbows, wrists, hands, fingers, hips, knees, ankles,
feet, toes**. Pupils count ten fingers, then ten toes; teacher shows the wrist by bending the hand.

**Step 4 — What they do, and why you are special.** Eyes see, ears hear, nose smells, mouth eats and
talks, legs walk, hands hold. Then: "Look in the mirror. Nobody else in this class has your exactly
same face. Your body is special — it belongs only to you." Body-awareness game: teacher calls a part,
pupils touch it; the child who touches the right one claps.

**Board summary.** *I have a body. My body has a head, neck, trunk, arms and legs. My body is special.*

**Evaluation.** (1) Point to your neck. (2) Name the parts on your head. (3) What do we use to hear?
(4) Touch your elbow, then your ankle. (5) Say one thing only you do with your hands.
**Assignment.** Draw your own body in your notebook and label five parts you know.

**Worksheet — Week 1 (exam style).**
1. Which of these is NOT a part of the head? A) nose B) ear C) elbow D) hair
2. The part of the body between the head and the trunk is the A) leg B) neck C) waist D) finger
3. Which one is on the trunk? A) chest B) wrist C) ankle D) knee
4. We use our ____ to smell. A) ears B) feet C) hands D) nose
5. Fill in: I have ____ fingers on both hands.
6. Say two things your body helps you do every day.

**Teacher's key.** 1-C · 2-B · 3-A · 4-D · 5 ten · 6 any two of: see, hear, eat, walk, run, hold, play.

### WEEK 2 — CARE FOR OUR BODY

**Reference:** NERDC NURSERY 3 · Basic Science and Technology · First Term W2 (pp 491–494)
**Objectives.** (1) Say why we care for the body; (2) name the daily body-care practices in order;
(3) demonstrate correct hand-washing and nail care; (4) state how much sleep and rest the body needs.
**Previous knowledge.** Body parts (Week 1).
**Materials.** Soap, towel, toothbrush and cup, comb, nail cutter, water, a bowl, clean and dirty
clothes, a plate of fruit, a chart of the daily routine.

**Step 1 — Why we care for the body.** A body that is looked after stays strong, does not itch, does
not smell and does not fall sick often. Clean body, clean mouth, strong body.
**Step 2 — The daily practices.** Teacher holds each item as it is named: bathe every day with soap;
brush the teeth **twice a day** — morning and night; wash the hands before eating and after the
toilet. Pupils mime each action in a line: wash, brush, wash.
**Step 3 — Hair, nails, clothes.** Hair: wash it, comb it, keep it neat. Nails: cut them short and
keep them clean — long nails hide dirt and scratch the skin. Clothes: wear clean clothes every day;
the dirty ones go to the basin, not back on the body.
**Step 4 — Food, water, sleep, play, clinic.** Eat good food (foo-foo, rice, beans, fruit, vegetables)
and drink clean water; sleep early and rest well so the body grows; run and play to make the body
strong. Once in a while the nurse or the health worker checks us — teeth, eyes, body, how tall we are.
We are not afraid of the clinic; the nurse only looks and does not hurt.

**Board summary.** *We bathe daily. We brush twice a day. We wash our hands. We keep nails short and
clothes clean. We eat good food, sleep well, play and grow.*

**Evaluation.** (1) How many times a day do we brush? (2) Show me how you wash your hands. (3) Name
two things we do to the hair. (4) Why do we cut our nails short? (5) What do we do when we are tired?
**Assignment.** Colour the picture of the clean child and cross out the one who is dirty.

**Worksheet — Week 2 (exam style).**
1. We brush our teeth ____ a day. A) once B) twice C) three times D) four times
2. Which one keeps our nails clean and safe? A) a comb B) a cap C) a nail cutter D) a cup
3. Before eating we must A) run B) sleep C) sing D) wash our hands
4. Our dirty clothes should be A) put in the basin B) worn again C) kept under the bed D) given to a friend
5. Fill in: We drink ____ water so that we do not fall sick.
6. Mention two things you do every morning to keep your body clean.

**Teacher's key.** 1-B · 2-C · 3-D · 4-A · 5 clean · 6 any two of: bathe, brush teeth, wash hands,
wear clean clothes, comb hair.

### WEEK 3 — FIRSTAID: FIRSTAID BOX CONTENTS

**Reference:** NERDC NURSERY 3 · Basic Science and Technology · First Term W3 (pp 491–494)
**Objectives.** (1) Say what first aid is in one line; (2) name at least six contents of the first aid
box; (3) point to the box in the school/point out where it is kept; (4) state who may use the box.
**Previous knowledge.** Cuts and falls happen at home and at school (children's own experience).
**Materials.** The school first aid box (real), plasters, a roll of bandage, cotton wool, a clean
bottle of methylated spirit/antiseptic cream, blunt scissors, a thermometer, safety pins, gloves.

**Step 1 — What first aid means.** First aid is the **first help** we give a person who is hurt or
suddenly sick, before the doctor or the nurse comes. "If your friend falls and his knee bleeds, do we
run away? No — we go and tell a grown-up, and the first help is called first aid."
**Step 2 — The box and what is inside.** Teacher brings out the box and names each item as pupils
touch (not use) it: **plasters / band-aids, bandages, cotton wool, antiseptic liquid or cream,
scissors, thermometer, safety pins, gloves**; and the extra things: **pain relief medicine, gauze,
tape, tweezers**. Each item goes back into the box as it is named.
**Step 3 — Why we keep a box at home and at school.** So that help is ready when somebody is hurt —
the box is kept clean, in a fixed place, and it is always filled up.
**Step 4 — The safety rule.** Only grown-ups use the things in the box. We do not take the scissors,
we do not open the medicine, we do not drink the antiseptic — it is not a drink. Pupils repeat twice:
"The first aid box is for grown-ups to use."

**Board summary.** *First aid is the first help for a hurt or sick person. The box has plasters,
bandages, cotton wool, antiseptic, scissors, thermometer, safety pins, gloves. Grown-ups use it.*

**Evaluation.** (1) What is first aid? (2) Mention four things in the box. (3) Where is the box kept
in our school? (4) Who is allowed to use it?
**Assignment.** From the items on the board, draw the two things in the box that you have seen at home.

**Worksheet — Week 3 (exam style).**
1. First aid means the ____ help given to a hurt person. A) last B) first C) secret D) slow
2. Which of these is used to cover a small cut? A) a comb B) a spoon C) a plaster D) a shoe
3. Which of these is NOT kept in the first aid box? A) bandage B) cotton wool C) plaster D) chewing stick
4. Who should use the things in the first aid box? A) grown-ups B) any pupil C) a baby D) the neighbour's child
5. Fill in: We keep the first aid box clean and always ____.
6. Name three things you can find inside a first aid box.

**Teacher's key.** 1-B · 2-C · 3-D · 4-A · 5 filled/filled up · 6 any three of: plasters, bandages,
cotton wool, antiseptic, scissors, thermometer, safety pins, gloves, gauze, tape, tweezers.

### WEEK 4 — FIRSTAID: USES

**Reference:** NERDC NURSERY 3 · Basic Science and Technology · First Term W4 (pp 491–494)
**Objectives.** (1) Match each first aid item to its use; (2) say when adult help must be called;
(3) name what a thermometer is used for; (4) demonstrate staying calm when someone is hurt.
**Previous knowledge.** Contents of the box (Week 3).
**Materials.** The same box and items, a toy telephone, picture cards (small cut, big cut, burn,
sprain), a chart "Call a grown-up".

**Step 1 — What each thing does.** Plasters / band-aids → cover **small cuts** and scrapes. Bandage →
wrap a wound and **support a sprained** leg or wrist. Cotton wool → clean a wound and put medicine on
it. Antiseptic → wash the wound so that **germs do not enter** (it may sting once). Thermometer →
check whether the body is **hot with fever**.
**Step 2 — A small demonstration.** Teacher puts a plaster on his own finger, wraps a bandage loosely
on a pupil's wrist with the doll as example, shows how cotton wool is dabbed and not rubbed. Pupils
say what each step was for.
**Step 3 — When to call an adult, and who to call.** A deep cut, blood that will not stop, a big
burn, an animal bite, a nail or thorn deep inside → **call a grown-up at once**, then ring for help.
Teacher shows the emergency numbers on the chart and the class reads them together.
**Step 4 — Staying calm.** "When somebody is hurt and you shout, everybody becomes frightened. We
breathe, we speak well, we say: *'Are you okay? I am calling a teacher.'*" The class practises the
calm voice and the calm hands, twice.

**Board summary.** *Plaster → small cut. Bandage → wrap and support. Cotton wool → clean. Antiseptic
→ kills germs. Thermometer → checks fever. Deep cut, big burn, bite: call a grown-up. Stay calm.*

**Evaluation.** (1) What do we use for a small cut? (2) What is the thermometer for? (3) Mention two
injuries that need a grown-up at once. (4) Show me the calm voice you use when a friend is hurt.
**Assignment.** Match by drawing a line: plaster–cut, bandage–wrap, cotton–clean, thermometer–fever.

**Worksheet — Week 4 (exam style).**
1. A bandage is used to A) cook food B) wrap a wound and support a sprain C) brush the teeth D) write on the book
2. We use a thermometer to find out if the body has A) dirt B) a cut C) fever D) a bruise
3. Antiseptic is put on a wound to A) keep germs out B) make it bleed more C) stop it from feeling D) colour it
4. Which one should make us call a grown-up at once? A) a small scrape B) a dirty shoe C) a torn book D) a deep cut that will not stop bleeding
5. Fill in: When somebody is hurt we must keep ____ and call a grown-up.
6. Name the item used for (a) a small cut (b) checking fever.

**Teacher's key.** 1-B · 2-C · 3-A · 4-D · 5 calm · 6 (a) plaster (b) thermometer.

### WEEK 5 — BODY CARE FOR WOUNDS: MEANING, CAUSES & TYPES

**Reference:** NERDC NURSERY 3 · Basic Science and Technology · First Term W7 (pp 491–494)
**Objectives.** (1) Say what a wound is; (2) name the causes of wounds around home and school;
(3) identify the five types of wounds from pictures; (4) state two ways of preventing wounds.
**Previous knowledge.** First aid box and its uses (Weeks 3–4).
**Materials.** Picture cards for cut, graze, bruise, burn, puncture; a safe knife (blunt, for
showing only), a thorn in a bottle, a candle (lit by the teacher, for demonstration only, extinguished
immediately), bandage.

**Step 1 — What a wound is.** A wound is an injury that **breaks the skin** — where the skin is open,
or cut, or has blood on it. "If the skin is not broken, it is not a wound." Teacher shows a picture of
a cut and a picture of a dirty face; pupils say which one is a wound.
**Step 2 — Where wounds come from.** Cuts from sharp things (knife, broken glass, razor blade, tin
lid); falls and scrapes (running in the compound, falling from a chair); **burns** (kettle, pot from
the fire, lantern, candle); insect bites (mosquito, bee, ant); scratches from animals (cat, dog).
**Step 3 — The types, one by one.** **Cut** — a straight opening from something sharp. **Scrape /
graze** — the top skin rubbed off after a fall. **Bruise** — a blue-black mark from bumping; the skin
is not open. **Burn** — made by heat or fire. **Puncture** — a small deep hole from a nail or a thorn.
Pupils hold up the correct picture card as teacher names each type.
**Step 4 — Prevention, and the two kinds of wound.** Do not play with knives, blades or broken glass;
do not run where there are sharp things or water on the floor; keep away from the pot on the fire and
from the lantern. Then the rule we keep for the whole term: **some wounds are small and we clean them;
some are big and only a doctor or nurse can treat them.**

**Board summary.** *A wound breaks the skin. Causes: sharp things, falls, heat, bites, scratches.
Types: cut, scrape, bruise, burn, puncture. Some are small; some need a doctor.*

**Evaluation.** (1) What is a wound? (2) Name two things in the house that can cut. (3) Show me the
picture of a burn. (4) What kind of wound comes from a thorn or a nail? (5) Mention one way to prevent wounds.
**Assignment.** Draw one thing that can cause a cut and one thing that can cause a burn.

**Worksheet — Week 5 (exam style).**
1. A wound is an injury that A) makes the hair neat B) breaks the skin C) cleans the teeth D) warms the bed
2. A blue-black mark from bumping, with the skin not open, is a A) bruise B) burn C) cut D) puncture
3. A puncture wound can come from A) a bandage B) a plaster C) soap D) a thorn or a nail
4. Which of these causes burns? A) a toy B) cold water C) a hot pot from the fire D) a book
5. Fill in: We do not play with knives, glass or ____ blades.
6. Mention three things that can cause wounds at home.

**Teacher's key.** 1-B · 2-A · 3-D · 4-C · 5 razor · 6 any three of: sharp objects, falling, hot
liquid/objects, insect bites, animal scratches, playing with blades.

### WEEK 6 — BODY CARE FOR WOUNDS: CARE FOR WOUNDS

**Reference:** NERDC NURSERY 3 · Basic Science and Technology · First Term W8 (pp 491–494)
**Objectives.** (1) Put the steps of caring for a small wound in order; (2) state when an adult must
be called; (3) give two rules for scabs and bandages; (4) name the signs that a wound is going bad.
**Previous knowledge.** Types and causes of wounds (Week 5).
**Materials.** Clean water in a cup, cotton wool, antiseptic (teacher-handled), plaster and bandage,
picture chart "Steps for a small wound", a doll.

**Step 1 — The four steps, in order.** (i) **Wash the hands first.** (ii) **Clean the wound with
clean water.** (iii) **Put on the antiseptic** with cotton wool — dab, do not rub. (iv) **Cover with a
plaster or a bandage.** The class says the four steps with four actions: wash, rinse, dab, cover.
**Step 2 — Call an adult at once when.** The cut is deep; the blood will not stop; the burn is big;
an animal has bitten; a nail or thorn has gone deep into the skin. "We do not try to be the doctor."
**Step 3 — The rules we keep.** Never touch a wound with dirty hands. Do **not pick at the scab** —
the scab is the body's own plaster, and when it is picked the wound opens again and can mark. Change
the bandage as the grown-up says; a wet or dirty bandage is not a bandage.
**Step 4 — When it is going bad, and healing takes time.** If the wound becomes **red**, or **swells**,
or **pus** comes out, or it hurts more after two days, tell a grown-up — that is infection. A wound
heals in some days: we keep it clean, we do not spoil the scab, we let it finish. And the line we keep
for the term: **prevention is better than treatment** — it is better not to be cut at all.

**Board summary.** *Wash hands → clean with water → antiseptic → cover. Do not pick the scab. Red,
swollen or pus = tell a grown-up. Prevention is better than cure.*

**Evaluation.** (1) What is the first thing we do? (2) Show me step two and step three. (3) Why must
we not pick a scab? (4) Mention one sign that a wound is going bad. (5) What does "prevention is
better than treatment" mean?
**Assignment.** Number 1–4 the pictures of the four steps in your notebook.

**Worksheet — Week 6 (exam style).**
1. What is the FIRST thing we do before caring for a wound? A) wash our hands B) put on a plaster C) run to the doctor D) blow on it
2. We clean a small wound with A) cold dirty water B) clean water C) pepper D) mud
3. A scab should be A) picked every day B) covered with mud C) left to fall off itself D) cut with a blade
4. Which one tells us the wound is going bad? A) it is dry B) it itches a little C) the colour is the same D) it is red and swollen
5. Fill in: After a bandage gets wet or dirty, we ____ it.
6. Write the four steps for caring for a small wound in order.

**Teacher's key.** 1-A · 2-B · 3-C · 4-D · 5 change · 6 wash hands → clean with water → apply
antiseptic → cover with plaster/bandage.

### WEEK 8 — SICKNESS: DEFINITION, TYPES AND SYMPTOMS, CAUSES AND CARE

**Reference:** NERDC NURSERY 3 · Basic Science and Technology · First Term W9 **and** W10 (pp 491–494)
— the school's Week 8 row merges NERDC's two weeks, so both lists are covered here.
**Objectives.** (1) Define sickness in one line; (2) name common childhood sicknesses; (3) match
symptoms to the sickness they show; (4) state causes and preventions of sickness; (5) describe the
care of a sick person.
**Previous knowledge.** Body care (Week 2), wounds and the thermometer (Weeks 4–6).
**Materials.** Thermometer (teacher-handled), a cup, a mosquito net picture, soap, a chart of the
"signs of sickness", a doll, medicine bottle (empty, closed).

**Step 1 — What sickness is.** Sickness (illness) is when the body is **not working properly** and we
feel unwell — the body is weak, the head pains, the stomach pains. A well body plays and eats; a sick
body rests and is taken to the nurse or the doctor.
**Step 2 — The sicknesses children know and the signs they show.** **Cold and cough, fever, stomach
ache, headache, malaria, diarraria (running stomach), measles, chicken pox.** The signs — **fever /
high temperature, pain, coughing, sneezing, vomiting, weakness, loss of appetite, rashes** — are how
the body tells us something is wrong. Pupils name the sickness from the sign teacher reads out.
**Step 3 — Causes.** Germs (bacteria and viruses); dirty water and dirty food; the bite of an
infected mosquito (malaria); not eating well; not sleeping enough; very cold weather. Teacher shows
the net: "The net keeps the mosquito off you at night."
**Step 4 — Prevention, and care of a sick person.** Wash the hands, eat good food, drink clean water,
**cover the mouth when coughing** (into the elbow), sleep under the net. When someone is sick: **rest
and sleep; take the medicine the doctor gave, at the time given; drink plenty of water; eat good food;
keep warm; go to the doctor or the clinic.** And the kindest rule of all: we are **nice** to a sick
person — we do not laugh at them, we speak softly, we take them a cup. A sick child also gets better:
sickness is not for ever.

**Board summary.** *Sickness = the body not working well. Signs: fever, pain, cough, vomiting,
weakness, rashes. Causes: germs, dirty food/water, mosquito, poor food, no rest. Care: rest,
medicine as the doctor said, water, good food, keep warm, be kind.*

**Evaluation.** (1) What is sickness? (2) Name three signs of sickness. (3) What does the thermometer
check? (4) Mention two things that bring sickness. (5) What do we do for a sick brother or sister?
**Assignment.** Write four words that tell us a person is sick (e.g. fever, ____).

**Worksheet — Week 8 (exam style).**
1. Sickness means the body A) is working properly B) is not working properly C) is growing D) is sleeping well
2. Which one is a sign of sickness? A) running fast B) sharp pencil C) high temperature D) clean cloth
3. Malaria is brought to us by A) a goat B) rain C) a book D) a mosquito
4. A sick person should drink plenty of A) water B) cold soft drink C) kerosene D) palm oil
5. Fill in: We cover our mouth when we ____.
6. Mention three ways of preventing sickness.
7. What do we do with medicine that a doctor has given?

**Teacher's key.** 1-B · 2-C · 3-D · 4-A · 5 cough/sneeze · 6 any three of: wash hands, eat healthy
food, drink clean water, cover mouth when coughing, sleep under mosquito net · 7 take it as the
doctor directed, at the right time (we do not take medicine alone).

### WEEK 9 — ROCKS AND SOIL

**Reference:** NERDC NURSERY 3 · Basic Science and Technology · **Second Term W1** (pp 494–497)
**Objectives.** (1) Define a rock and soil in one line each; (2) sort rock samples by size; (3) name
three uses of rocks; (4) identify sandy, clayey and loamy soil by touch; (5) say why soil is important.
**Previous knowledge.** Our environment and the things on the ground (Weeks 1–8 of Basic Science; the
school's P1 "Exploring our environment").
**Materials.** Real samples in tins: a big rock, small stones, pebbles, sand; three shallow dishes of
dry soil — sandy, clay, loamy; water in a cup; two transparent bottles; a picture of a road being made.

**Step 1 — Rocks.** A rock is a **hard, solid thing found in nature on the earth** — nobody makes it.
Teacher passes the samples: "Where do rocks come from?" Long, long ago some rocks were made when hot
liquid from inside the earth (**lava**) cooled and became hard; others were made when little bits of
stone were pressed together for a very long time; others again were changed by great heat and
pressure. We do not need those names today — we need our eyes: **big rocks, small stones, pebbles,
sand**. Pupils sort their tins into those four piles.
**Step 2 — What we use rocks for.** Building houses, making roads, decoration, and making some tools.
Teacher shows the picture of the road and the blocks: "The road in our street is made of small stones
like these."
**Step 3 — Soil.** Soil is the **top layer of the earth where plants grow**. It comes from rocks that
broke into tiny bits over many years, mixed with dead plants and dead animals. Pupils touch the three
dishes: **sandy soil** is grainy and the water runs through it quickly; **clay soil** is sticky when
wet and smooth when dry (teacher moulds a ball); **loamy soil** is a mixture and is the **best for
plants** — that is the soil in our school garden.
**Step 4 — Why soil matters.** Plants grow in it; animals live in it (worms, ants); it gives us food.
"Take the soil away and there is no yam, no rice, no tree." Pupils put a handful of loamy soil into
each of the two bottles, add water, shake, and watch which one settles with the clearest water on top.

**Board summary.** *A rock is hard and comes from nature: big rocks, stones, pebbles, sand. We use
rocks for houses, roads, decoration, tools. Soil is the top layer of the earth where plants grow:
sandy, clay, loamy. Loam is best. Plants, animals and our food come from soil.*

**Evaluation.** (1) What is a rock? (2) Show me the pebbles. (3) Name two uses of rocks. (4) Which
soil is sticky when wet? (5) Which soil is best for plants and why?
**Assignment.** Collect two small stones and one leaf from outside; bring them in a paper bag tomorrow.

**Worksheet — Week 9 (exam style).**
1. A rock is A) soft and made by people B) hard and found in nature C) a kind of food D) a kind of animal
2. Which of these is the smallest rock size? A) big rock B) boulder C) pebble D) stone
3. Sandy soil lets water A) run through quickly B) stay on top C) freeze D) rise up
4. Which soil is the best for growing plants? A) clay B) sand C) gravel D) loam
5. Fill in: Soil is the ____ layer of the earth where plants grow.
6. Mention three uses of rocks.
7. Say two things that make up soil.

**Teacher's key.** 1-B · 2-C · 3-A · 4-D · 5 top · 6 any three of: building houses, making roads,
decoration, making tools · 7 broken-down rocks and dead plants and animals.
