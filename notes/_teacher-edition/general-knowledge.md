# General Knowledge — Nursery 2 · First Term

**Weeks:** 1–6, 8–9 (Week 7 Mid-Term, 10 Revision, 11 Examination — no notes).
**Depth floor:** school-generated subject — there is no NERDC General Knowledge scheme for this band.
The extent of each week is the school's own `Breakdown (Subtopics)` column in the master corpus,
reproduced line for line below; nothing outside it is taught or examined.
**Term theme:** Myself, My Family & My School.

---

### WEEK 1 — MYSELF: MY NAME, MY AGE AND MY BODY

**Reference:** school-generated (no NERDC equivalent); depth = the school's own breakdown for N2 F1 W1
**Objectives.** (1) Say their full name and their nickname clearly; (2) state their age and whether
they are a boy or a girl; (3) point to and name seven body parts; (4) name the sense used for each of
seeing, hearing and smelling.
**Previous knowledge.** At home each child answers to a name; they have heard body rhymes.
**Materials.** Name tags, a mirror, a hand bell, a bottle of perfume, a drum or tin, picture cards of
the eyes/ears/nose, a chart of the class roster.

**Step 1 — My name, my age.** Teacher calls the register by first name and surname; each child stands,
says "My name is Adaeze **Okafor**," then the nickname at home ("I call me Ada"). Teacher asks the age:
"I am four years old." Boys stand, girls sit — "I am a boy / I am a girl." The class says together:
**"I am special, I am me."**
**Step 2 — My body.** In front of the mirror pupils name: **head, eyes, ears, nose, mouth, hands,
legs**. Teacher points fast, pupils touch faster. Then the same list on the chart, in writing, with the
first letter traced by the pupil.
**Step 3 — How I know the world.** "I see with my **eyes**" (bell hidden behind a book); "I hear with
my **ears**" (eyes shut, bell rings); "I smell with my **nose**" (perfume bottle). Pupils say the sense
for each action; the tongue and the skin are mentioned only as "the rest of the body tells us taste and
touch".
**Step 4 — Rhyme and repeat.** "Head, Shoulders, Knees and Toes" twice, first slowly, then at speed;
pupils do the actions. End with each child saying their own name and age to the class.

**Board summary.** *My name is ______. I am ____ years old. I am special, I am me. I see with my eyes,
hear with my ears, smell with my nose.*

**Evaluation.** (1) Stand and say your full name. (2) How old are you? (3) Point to three parts of
your body. (4) What do we hear with? (5) Say the rhyme with the actions.
**Assignment.** Write your first name in your notebook five times and draw your face under it.

**Worksheet — Week 1 (exam style).**
1. When somebody asks who you are, you say your A) age only B) class C) shoe size D) name
2. We see with our A) eyes B) ears C) nose D) hands
3. Which one is NOT a part of the body? A) nose B) spoon C) chin D) knee
4. "I am special, I am ___." A) you B) we C) me D) they
5. Fill in: I am ____ years old.
6. Say your full name and your age to the class.

---

**Teacher's key.** 1-D · 2-A · 3-B · 4-C · 5 four · 6 the pupil says their own full name and age.
### WEEK 2 — MY FAMILY: MEMBERS AND THEIR ROLES

**Reference:** school-generated; depth = the school's breakdown for N2 F1 W2
**Objectives.** (1) Name the members of the family at home; (2) name members of the extended family;
(3) say one thing each member does; (4) use "please", "thank you" and "sorry" in a family scene.
**Previous knowledge.** Each child lives with people who care for them (Week 1, "myself").
**Materials.** Family pictures brought from home or printed cards, a doll, a small broom, a cooking
pot, chart "My family", crayons.

**Step 1 — Who is in my family.** Teacher sticks the pictures on the board as the class names them:
**father, mother, brothers, sisters**. Pupils say how many brothers and sisters they have (never
forced to speak; a child may point instead).
**Step 2 — The bigger family.** Beyond the house: **grandpa, grandma, uncles, aunts, cousins** —
"the family that comes for Christmas and for weddings."
**Step 3 — What each member does.** Father works and brings things home; mother cooks and buys food;
brothers and sisters play and share duties; everybody sweeps, fetches water, sets the table. Teacher
stresses: **helping at home belongs to everybody** — a boy can set the table, a girl can carry a small
broom. Love and respect: we greet the elders first, we do not shout at grandma.
**Step 4 — The three magic words at home.** Role-play with the doll: asking for water — "**please**";
being given it — "**thank you**"; spilling it — "**sorry**". Each pair acts one scene. Class sings
"This is my family" and draws their family: father, mother, brothers, sisters, themselves.

**Board summary.** *My family: father, mother, brothers, sisters. Big family: grandpa, grandma, uncles,
aunts, cousins. We help each other and say please, thank you, sorry.*

**Evaluation.** (1) Name the people in your family. (2) Who are your grandpa and grandma to you?
(3) Say one thing your mother or father does at home. (4) Act the scene where you break a cup.
**Assignment.** Draw your family and write one word under each person: father, mother, me.

**Worksheet — Week 2 (exam style).**
1. Your father's father is your A) grandpa B) uncle C) brother D) cousin
2. Which word do you say when someone gives you something? A) sorry B) thank you C) please D) goodbye
3. A girl in your family who is your parent's daughter is your A) aunt B) cousin C) grandma D) sister
4. Who cooks food and sweeps at home? A) only the father B) only the children C) everybody helps D) nobody
5. Fill in: My mother's brother is my ____.
6. Mention three people who live with you at home.

---

**Teacher's key.** 1-A · 2-B · 3-D · 4-C · 5 uncle · 6 any three of: father, mother, brothers, sisters, grandma, grandpa.
### WEEK 3 — MY SCHOOL: PLACES, PEOPLE AND THINGS

**Reference:** school-generated; depth = the school's breakdown for N2 F1 W3
**Objectives.** (1) Say the school's name and their class name; (2) name five places in the school;
(3) name four people who work in the school; (4) state what goes in the school bag and how it is cared for.
**Previous knowledge.** Family (Week 2) — "at home somebody takes care of you; at school other people do."
**Materials.** The school itself (a tour), a school bag and its contents, name cards for staff,
a chart of the school compound, crayons.

**Step 1 — Our names.** "What is the name of this school?" — Mercy Model School. "What is your class?"
— **Nursery 2**. Pupils say both aloud, then point to the sign on the gate.
**Step 2 — Places in the school.** On the tour: **classroom, playground, library, toilet, office**.
At each place the class stops and says its name; back in class the pictures are matched to the words.
"We learn in the classroom, we play in the playground, we read in the library, we freshen up in the
toilet, the head teacher works in the office."
**Step 3 — People in the school.** **The head teacher** (head of the school), **teachers** (they teach
us), **the caretaker** (he sweeps and keeps the compound clean), **the school nurse** (she looks after
anyone who is unwell or hurt). Pupils greet each one as teacher shows the card.
**Step 4 — My bag and my things.** Inside the bag: books, exercise books, pencils, crayons, a sharpener,
a handkerchief, water bottle. Rules: we do not tear the pages, we do not write on the desk, the bag is
kept in its place. Children pack their own bag as a race against the clock — unaided.

**Board summary.** *My school is ______. My class is Nursery 2. Places: classroom, playground, library,
toilet, office. People: head teacher, teachers, caretaker, nurse.*

**Evaluation.** (1) What is the name of this school? (2) Name three places in the school. (3) Who
sweeps the compound? (4) What is in your school bag? (5) How do we treat our books?
**Assignment.** Colour the picture of the school bag and ring the five things that belong inside it.

**Worksheet — Week 3 (exam style).**
1. Books are read and borrowed in the A) toilet B) library C) playground D) kitchen
2. The person who is the head of the school is the A) caretaker B) pupil C) driver D) head teacher
3. Where do we play during break? A) the playground B) the office C) the library D) the store
4. Which one should NOT be in the school bag? A) pencil B) book C) pillow D) crayon
5. Fill in: My class is Nursery ____.
6. Name four people who work in your school.

---

**Teacher's key.** 1-B · 2-D · 3-A · 4-C · 5 Two · 6 head teacher, teachers, caretaker, school nurse (any four).
### WEEK 4 — MY CLASSROOM: RULES AND MATERIALS

**Reference:** school-generated; depth = the school's breakdown for N2 F1 W4
**Objectives.** (1) Name objects in the classroom; (2) state four classroom rules; (3) say how the room
is kept clean; (4) identify objects by colour.
**Previous knowledge.** Places of the school (Week 3).
**Materials.** Real objects: table, chair, board, duster, books, pencils, crayons; a dustbin; coloured
cards (red, blue, yellow, green, black, white); a small broom.

**Step 1 — What is in our room.** Pupils touch and name: **table, chair, board, books, pencils,
crayons**. Teacher asks "What do we write on?" — the board; "What do we sit on?" — the chair.
**Step 2 — The four rules.** **Raise your hand** before you speak; **listen** when the teacher or a
friend is talking; **share** the crayons and the books; **keep quiet** when somebody else is answering.
Each rule is acted: first wrongly, then rightly, and the class says which one is correct.
**Step 3 — A clean room.** Dustbin for paper, not the floor; desks wiped and lined up; nothing left on
the chair. Two pupils dust a desk, one empties the dustbin — everybody takes a turn each week.
**Step 4 — Colours in the room and a game.** **Red, blue, yellow, green, black, white** — each colour
found on a real thing (red book, blue bag, yellow crayon, green board, black chalk, white paper).
Game: "**Find something that is red… round… soft**" — three runs, one pupil per run.

**Board summary.** *In the classroom we raise our hand, listen, share and keep quiet. We keep the room
clean and use the dustbin. Colours: red, blue, yellow, green, black, white.*

**Evaluation.** (1) Name four things in the classroom. (2) What do we do before we speak? (3) Where
does paper go? (4) Find something yellow. (5) Say the four rules.
**Assignment.** Colour the classroom picture: the book red, the bag blue, the board green.

**Worksheet — Week 4 (exam style).**
1. Before you speak in class you must A) shout B) raise your hand C) stand on the chair D) run out
2. The colour of the chalkboard in our room is A) green B) pink C) brown D) orange
3. Waste paper should go into the A) bag B) cupboard C) dustbin D) shoe
4. We write on the board with A) a spoon B) a stone C) a leaf D) chalk
5. Fill in: When a friend is talking, we ____.
6. Mention four rules of the classroom.

---

**Teacher's key.** 1-B · 2-A · 3-C · 4-D · 5 listen/quiet · 6 raise the hand before speaking, listen to the teacher, share materials, keep quiet when another answers.
### WEEK 5 — MY HOME AND MY NEIGHBOURHOOD

**Reference:** school-generated; depth = the school's breakdown for N2 F1 W5
**Objectives.** (1) Name rooms in the house and what each is for; (2) say who lives with them, where
they sleep and eat; (3) describe their street/village and two things seen on the way to school;
(4) use near/far and up/down on the walk to school.
**Previous knowledge.** Family at home (Week 2); the school tour (Week 3).
**Materials.** A drawing of a house with rooms, picture cards (bed, pot, chair, bath), a wall chart of
a street, blocks to build a house.

**Step 1 — The rooms.** **Sitting room** (visitors sit), **kitchen** (food is cooked), **bedroom**
(we sleep), **bathroom** (we bathe). Teacher says a room, pupils run their finger to the right part of
the big drawing.
**Step 2 — Who lives with me.** "I sleep in the bedroom; I eat in the sitting room/dining area." Each
pupil says who is in the house with them (or points to the picture if they prefer).
**Step 3 — My street, my way to school.** "My house is in ____ street / ____ village / ____
town." On the way to school we see: the shop, the bus stop, the church, the mosque, the market, the
gutter, the mango tree, the neighbour's dog. Pupils tell one thing they always see.
**Step 4 — Near and far, up and down.** "The gate is **near**; the shop is **far**. The hill is **up**;
the gutter is **down**." Then the idea that closes the week: **my home is my first school** — at home I
learned to walk, to speak, to greet; at school I learn more.

**Board summary.** *Rooms: sitting room, kitchen, bedroom, bathroom. My street is ______. Home is my
first school, and school is my second home.*

**Evaluation.** (1) Where do we cook? (2) Where do you sleep? (3) Name two things you see on the way to
school. (4) Point to something near, then something far. (5) Why is home called our first school?
**Assignment.** Draw your house with four rooms and write the name of each room.

**Worksheet — Week 5 (exam style).**
1. We cook food in the A) kitchen B) bedroom C) toilet D) store
2. The room where we sleep is the A) sitting room B) bedroom C) bathroom D) porch
3. The shop at the end of the long street is ___ from my house. A) near B) small C) far D) clean
4. Water for bathing is in the A) kitchen B) bedroom C) sitting room D) bathroom
5. Fill in: My home is my ____ school.
6. Name the four rooms of your house and what each one is used for.

---

**Teacher's key.** 1-A · 2-B · 3-C · 4-D · 5 first · 6 sitting room — visitors; kitchen — cooking; bedroom — sleeping; bathroom — bathing.
### WEEK 6 — COLOURS, SHAPES AND SIZES AROUND ME

**Reference:** school-generated; depth = the school's breakdown for N2 F1 W6
**Objectives.** (1) Name the primary colours and black, white, green; (2) identify circle, square,
triangle, star in the room; (3) compare two things by size; (4) name the colours of the Nigerian flag.
**Previous knowledge.** Colours of classroom objects (Week 4).
**Materials.** Colour cards, shape cut-outs, two balls of different size, a long and a short pencil, a
picture of the Nigerian flag, beads for sorting.

**Step 1 — Colours.** **Red, blue, yellow** are the primary colours (the colours that other colours
are made from); then **black, white, green**. Teacher holds a card, pupils find the same colour on
somebody's dress or on the shelf.
**Step 2 — Shapes.** **Circle** (like a plate, a ball, a clock), **square** (a tile, a handkerchief),
**triangle** (a roof, a sandwich cut in two), **star** (on the flag, in the sky). Shape hunt: each child
brings one round thing, one square thing.
**Step 3 — Sizes.** **Big and small** (big bag, small bag); **long and short** (long chalk, short
chalk); **tall and short** (two pupils stand back to back). Language: "This is bigger than…", "Mine is
smaller than…"
**Step 4 — The flag and a game.** The Nigerian flag is **green and white**: two green strips and a
white strip in the middle — green for the green lands, white for peace. Game: colour sorting (beads
into six cups), then size stacking (biggest at the bottom).

**Board summary.** *Colours: red, blue, yellow, green, black, white. Shapes: circle, square, triangle,
star. Big/small, long/short, tall/short. Our flag is green and white.*

**Evaluation.** (1) Say the three primary colours. (2) Show me something round. (3) Which is longer?
(4) What are the colours of the Nigerian flag? (5) Name one thing that is a square.
**Assignment.** Colour three circles red, two squares blue and one triangle yellow.

**Worksheet — Week 6 (exam style).**
1. Which of these is a primary colour? A) pink B) yellow C) purple D) brown
2. The Nigerian flag is A) green and white B) red and blue C) black and yellow D) purple and pink
3. A plate and a ball are shaped like a A) square B) triangle C) circle D) star
4. A roof is shaped like a A) circle B) square C) line D) triangle
5. Fill in: A handkerchief is the shape of a ____.
6. Say the colours of the Nigerian flag and what the white colour stands for.

---

**Teacher's key.** 1-B · 2-A · 3-C · 4-D · 5 square · 6 green and white; white stands for peace.
### WEEK 8 — ANIMALS AND BIRDS AROUND ME

**Reference:** school-generated; depth = the school's breakdown for N2 F1 W8
**Objectives.** (1) Name common domestic animals and the sound each makes; (2) name wild animals seen
in pictures; (3) name four birds and say where animals live; (4) classify an animal as domestic or wild.
**Previous knowledge.** Home and street (Week 5); shapes and colours used to describe pictures (Week 6).
**Materials.** Animal picture cards, a farm picture, a bush picture, sound clips or teacher's voice,
toy animals, feathers.

**Step 1 — Animals that live with us.** **Dog (woof), cat (miaow), goat (meh), chicken (koko), cow
(moo)** — domestic animals: they live near people and give us help, milk, eggs or protection. Each pupil
takes a card, names the animal and the whole class makes its sound.
**Step 2 — Animals of the bush.** In pictures and stories: **lion, elephant, monkey, snake** — wild
animals; they live where there is no house, and we do not go near them.
**Step 3 — Birds.** **Parrot, pigeon, hen, eagle.** The eagle is on our national emblem — the first look
at "our national bird". Birds have feathers, two legs and a beak; most of them fly.
**Step 4 — Where they live, and the song.** Home (dog in the compound), farm (cow, goat, hen), bush
(lion, monkey), water (fish, duck), sky (birds). Game: teacher says a place, pupils put the right animal
card under it. Then "Old MacDonald" with actions, twice.

**Board summary.** *Domestic: dog, cat, goat, chicken, cow. Wild: lion, elephant, monkey, snake. Birds:
parrot, pigeon, hen, eagle. Animals live at home, on the farm, in the bush, in water and in the sky.*

**Evaluation.** (1) Which animal says "meh"? (2) Name two wild animals. (3) Where does a fish live?
(4) Say two things that show an animal is a bird. (5) Name the bird on our emblem.
**Assignment.** Cut or draw four animals and write D for the ones that live with people, W for the wild ones.

**Worksheet — Week 8 (exam style).**
1. Which animal gives us milk? A) goat B) cow C) hen D) dog
2. The sound of the goat is A) meh B) moo C) miaow D) woof
3. Which one is a wild animal? A) cat B) chicken C) lion D) dog
4. A bird has feathers, a beak and A) four legs B) scales C) a tail like a fish D) two legs
5. Fill in: A hen and a cock live on the ____.
6. Name two animals that live with people at home and two that live in the bush.

---

**Teacher's key.** 1-B · 2-A · 3-C · 4-D · 5 farm · 6 any two domestic (dog, cat, goat, chicken, cow) with care said; any two wild (lion, elephant, monkey, snake).
### WEEK 9 — GOOD HABITS: GREETINGS, POLITENESS AND CLEANLINESS

**Reference:** school-generated; depth = the school's breakdown for N2 F1 W9
**Objectives.** (1) Greet correctly in the morning, afternoon and evening, and greet an elder first;
(2) use please, thank you, sorry, excuse me; (3) show hand-washing at the right times; (4) state the
daily body-care habits (teeth, bath, uniform).
**Previous knowledge.** Family manners (Week 2); body care habits from Basic Science Week 2 of the
same term; the classroom rules (Week 4).
**Materials.** A visitor (another teacher) for the role-play, mirror, uniform, soap and water, chart of
the Good-Habit Songs, a "magic words" card set.

**Step 1 — Greetings of the day.** Morning: "**Good morning**"; afternoon: "**Good afternoon**";
evening: "**Good evening**". To an elder we greet first and we greet respectfully — in Yoruba,
"Ẹ̀káàró / Ẹ̀káàṣánṣá / Ẹ̀káàlẹ́" with a prostration for a man and kneeling for a woman (teacher
explains both, boys and girls); in Hausa, "Ina wuni? / Ina rana? / Ina fada?" — the class says each one
twice. Greeting an elder first is a duty, not a favour.
**Step 2 — The four magic words.** **Please** (asking), **thank you** (receiving), **sorry** (when you
hurt or spoil), **excuse me** (passing, or after sneezing). Circle game: teacher passes a card, the
child uses the word on it in a sentence; the visitor enters and the class greets her — that is the
real test.
**Step 3 — Cleanliness habits.** Wash hands **before eating and after the toilet**; brush teeth
**morning and night**; bathe daily; keep the uniform neat and the shoes brushed; nails short. Pupils
inspect their own nails in pairs and say "My nails are clean" or "I must cut my nails".
**Step 4 — Closing the term with one habit.** The class picks the Good Habit of the term and writes it
on the chart; every day for the rest of the term one child is the "habit monitor". Song: the Good-Habit
song, and the chart goes on the wall.

**Board summary.** *Good morning / afternoon / evening. We greet elders first. Please, thank you,
sorry, excuse me. Wash hands, brush teeth, bathe daily, keep the uniform neat.*

**Evaluation.** (1) What do you say at 7 a.m.? (2) Greet me the way you greet an elder. (3) When do we
say "excuse me"? (4) How many times a day do we brush our teeth? (5) Name one habit you will keep.
**Assignment.** Write the four magic words and use each in one sentence.

**Worksheet — Week 9 (exam style).**
1. At 7 o'clock in the morning we say A) good night B) good morning C) good evening D) goodbye
2. When somebody gives you a pencil you say A) thank you B) sorry C) please D) excuse me
3. We wash our hands A) after playing only B) once a week C) never D) before eating and after the toilet
4. The word we use when we want to pass somebody is A) sorry B) welcome C) excuse me D) hello
5. Fill in: We brush our teeth ____ and night.
6. Say three good habits you showed in school today.

**Teacher's key.** 1-B · 2-A · 3-D · 4-C · 5 morning · 6 any three of: greeting an elder, saying please/thank you/sorry, washing hands, brushing teeth, bathing, neat uniform.
