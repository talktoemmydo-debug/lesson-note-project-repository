### WEEK 1 — THE ROAD AND ROAD SAFETY

*Depth: NERDC Primary 1 · Basic Science · First Term W1 (surroundings: road).*

**You will learn to**

• point to a road in my community
• tell a tarred road from an untarred road and a pathway
• say what a road is used for
• cross the road the safe way

**Things to know**

• A **road** is the way that vehicles and people move from place to place.
• A **tarred road** is smooth and black because tar was put on it.
• An **untarred road** is made of soil or gravel and turns muddy in the rain.
• A **pathway** is a small road that people use for walking.
• The **traffic light** has three colours: red means stop, yellow means wait, green means go.
• We cross the road at the **zebra crossing** and we look left and right first.

**Let us talk**

1. What do you see moving on the road near your home?
2. Which light of the traffic light means stop?
3. Why must we look left and right before we cross?

**Words for my notebook**

• road — the way vehicles and people move
• tarred — smooth and black with tar
• pathway — a small road for walking
• zebra crossing — the place we cross the road

**Worksheet**

1. A road that is smooth and black is called a ____ road. A) tarred B) muddy C) water D) bush
2. Which of these is a small road for walking? A) bridge B) flyover C) pathway D) highway
3. The traffic light that means stop is A) green B) red C) yellow D) blue
4. Fill in: We cross the road at the ____ crossing.
5. Mention two things that move on the road.
6. Write one sentence on how you cross the road safely.
7. Draw a traffic light and colour the light that means go.

**My own work**

Draw the road near my house and put one car and one person walking on it.

### WEEK 2 — MY ENVIRONMENT AND COLOURS

*Depth: NERDC Primary 1 · Basic Science · First Term W2 (environment and colour).*

**You will learn to**

• say what the environment is
• name things I see in my environment
• tell the primary colours
• sort things by their colour

**Things to know**

• The **environment** is everything around us at home and at school.
• In my environment I see trees, houses, people, animals and the sky.
• The **primary colours** are red, blue and yellow.
• When we mix two primary colours we get a **secondary colour** like green, orange or purple.
• The sky looks blue, the grass looks green and the sun looks yellow.

**Let us talk**

1. Name three things you see in your classroom.
2. What colour is the grass after the rain?
3. Which two colours make green?

**Words for my notebook**

• environment — everything around us
• primary — the first colours: red, blue, yellow
• secondary — colours made by mixing two
• sort — to put things in groups

**Worksheet**

1. Everything around us at home and at school is the A) notebook B) environment C) calendar D) market
2. Which of these is a primary colour? A) purple B) green C) orange D) blue
3. Red and yellow mixed together give A) grey B) green C) black D) orange
4. Fill in: The grass looks ____.
5. Mention two things you see in the sky.
6. Write the three primary colours.
7. Colour one leaf green and one flower red.

**My own work**

Look around my room at home and draw two things I see, then colour them.

### WEEK 3 — SHAPES I

*Depth: NERDC Primary 1 · Basic Science · First Term W3 (basic shapes).*

**You will learn to**

• name the circle, square, triangle and rectangle
• say how many corners a shape has
• find shapes in my environment
• trace and draw basic shapes

**Things to know**

• A **circle** is round like a ball or a wheel and has no corners.
• A **square** has four equal sides and four corners.
• A **rectangle** has two long sides and two short sides, and four corners.
• A **triangle** has three sides and three corners.
• A window can be a rectangle, a wheel is a circle and a slice of bread can look like a triangle.

**Let us talk**

1. What shape is a wheel?
2. How many corners does a square have?
3. Name one thing at home that is a rectangle.

**Words for my notebook**

• circle — round with no corners
• square — four equal sides
• rectangle — two long and two short sides
• triangle — three sides and three corners

**Worksheet**

1. A shape that is round with no corners is a A) square B) triangle C) rectangle D) circle
2. How many sides does a triangle have? A) three B) six C) five D) four
3. A shape with four equal sides is a A) square B) star C) circle D) oval
4. Fill in: A wheel is the shape of a ____.
5. Mention two things at home that are a rectangle.
6. Write the number of corners on a triangle.
7. Draw a square and a circle side by side.

**My own work**

Find three shapes in my house and draw each one in my notebook.

### WEEK 5 — LIVING THINGS

*Depth: NERDC Primary 1 · Basic Science · First Term W5 (living things).*

**You will learn to**

• say what a living thing is
• tell the things living things do
• name living things at home and on the farm
• say what living things need to live

**Things to know**

• **Living things** are things that have life, like humans, animals and plants.
• Living things **move**, **eat**, **grow** and **breathe**.
• A dog, a cat, a goat, a hen and a tree are living things.
• Living things need **food**, **water**, **air** and a place to live.
• A plant is living because it grows and needs water and air.

**Let us talk**

1. Name two animals you keep at home.
2. What does a plant need to grow?
3. How do you know a dog is living?

**Words for my notebook**

• living — has life
• grow — to get bigger
• breathe — to take in air
• need — what a living thing must have

**Worksheet**

1. Which of these is a living thing? A) book B) goat C) chair D) stone
2. Living things do all of these except A) grow B) breathe C) eat D) melt
3. A plant needs water, air and ____ to grow well. A) shoes B) paper C) money D) sunlight
4. Fill in: A dog and a cat are ____ things.
5. Mention two living things you see on a farm.
6. Write one way you care for a plant.
7. Draw a plant and a animal that are living.

**My own work**

Water one plant at home and watch it for a week, then draw it.

### WEEK 6 — NON-LIVING THINGS

*Depth: NERDC Primary 1 · Basic Science · First Term W6 (non-living things).*

**You will learn to**

• say what a non-living thing is
• tell non-living things from living things
• name natural and man-made non-living things
• say how non-living things help us

**Things to know**

• **Non-living things** do not eat, grow, breathe or move on their own.
• A stone, water, air, a chair and a book are non-living things.
• **Natural** non-living things are made by nature: rocks, soil, water and sand.
• **Man-made** non-living things are made by people: cars, houses, chairs and toys.
• Water helps us to drink and a house gives us shelter, so non-living things help living things.

**Let us talk**

1. Name two non-living things in your classroom.
2. Is a tree living or non-living? Why?
3. Which non-living thing gives us shelter?

**Words for my notebook**

• non-living — has no life
• natural — made by nature
• man-made — made by people
• shelter — a safe place to stay

**Worksheet**

1. Which of these is non-living? A) hen B) stone C) dog D) tree
2. A chair is a ____ non-living thing. A) living B) growing C) natural D) man-made
3. Which one is a natural non-living thing? A) book B) water C) car D) toy
4. Fill in: A stone does not eat, grow or ____.
5. Mention two man-made non-living things.
6. Write one way water helps living things.
7. Sort: draw one living thing and one non-living thing.

**My own work**

Look around my home and draw two non-living things I use every day.

### WEEK 8 — AIR AND WIND

*Depth: NERDC Primary 1 · Basic Science · First Term W8 (surroundings: air).*

**You will learn to**

• say what air is
• feel air by blowing and fanning
• tell the uses of air
• say what wind is

**Things to know**

• **Air** is all around us; we cannot see it but we can feel it.
• We feel air when we blow, wave or fan.
• **Moving air** is called **wind**.
• We use air for **breathing**, drying clothes, flying kites and blowing balloons.
• **Clean air** is good for us; dirty air with smoke is bad for us.

**Let us talk**

1. How do you feel air on a hot day?
2. What do we call moving air?
3. Name one thing air helps us to do.

**Words for my notebook**

• air — what we breathe all around us
• wind — moving air
• breathe — to take air in and out
• fan — to move air with a fan or card

**Worksheet**

1. Moving air is called A) rain B) sun C) wind D) cloud
2. We cannot see air but we can A) buy it B) eat it C) feel it D) wear it
3. Which of these uses air? A) reading a book B) sitting on a chair C) flying a kite D) writing a letter
4. Fill in: We use air for ____.
5. Mention two ways you can feel air.
6. Write one thing that dirty air can carry.
7. Draw a kite flying in the wind.

**My own work**

Blow on my hand and draw what I used to feel the air, like a fan or a balloon.

### WEEK 9 — SOIL

*Depth: NERDC Primary 1 · Basic Science · First Term W9 (surroundings: soil).*

**You will learn to**

• say what soil is
• tell where we find soil
• name the types of soil by look
• say the uses of soil

**Things to know**

• **Soil** is the top part of the ground where plants grow.
• We find soil on the ground, in gardens, on farms and in flower pots.
• The types of soil are **sandy**, **clay** and **loamy** soil.
• Soil can be black, brown, red or yellow.
• We use soil for growing plants, making pots and building mud houses.
• We care for soil by not littering and by planting trees.

**Let us talk**

1. Where do you see soil near your home?
2. Which soil feels sticky when wet?
3. Name one thing made from soil.

**Words for my notebook**

• soil — the ground where plants grow
• sandy — soil that feels rough like sand
• clay — soil that is sticky when wet
• loamy — the best soil for plants

**Worksheet**

1. The top part of the ground where plants grow is A) cloud B) air C) soil D) water
2. The soil that is best for plants is A) rocky B) dry C) hard D) loamy
3. A pot for cooking can be made from A) glass B) air C) clay D) water
4. Fill in: Soil can be black, brown, red or ____.
5. Mention two places we find soil.
6. Write one way we care for the soil.
7. Draw a plant growing in the soil.

**My own work**

Touch the soil in a pot or garden at home and draw what the plant in it looks like.

• **sh** says /sh/ as in **ship**, **fish**.

• **ch** says /ch/ as in **chin**, **chip**.

• **th** says /th/ as in **this**, **that**.

• In a blend we hear each sound; in a digraph we hear one sound.

• st-op makes **stop**. sh-ip makes **ship**.

• Blends and digraphs help us read more words.

**Let us talk**

2. What is a digraph?

**Words for my notebook**

• digraph — two letters making one new sound

• sh — /sh/ as in ship
