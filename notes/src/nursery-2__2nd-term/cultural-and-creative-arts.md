### WEEK 1 — MAKING AND DESIGNING A HAND FAN

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 2 · Creativity · Second Term W4 (pp 415–417); Nursery 3 · Creativity · Second Term W4 (pp 568–571).*

**Main content**

• A **fan** is a flat thing we move to and fro so that air comes to us — it keeps us **cool**.
• Two kinds we can make: the **paddle fan** (one flat piece with a handle) and the **folding fan** (paper folded like a fan and joined at one end).
• Materials: **cardboard or thick paper, popsicle sticks, glue or tape, crayons, paint, stickers**.
• Steps: cut the shape → colour or draw on the flat side → fix the stick handle at the back → let the glue dry → wave it to test it.
• A pattern is a design that repeats: red, blue, red, blue · zigzag, dot, zigzag, dot.
• Folding for the folding fan: turn the paper back and forth in little folds like a plate, then join both ends with a thread or tape.
• The handle must be firm: hold the fan by the stick and shake it — if the paper falls, the glue needs more time.
• Cardboard is stiff and keeps its shape; tissue paper is soft and tears, so thick paper is better for the paddle.
• Art is not only for looking — a fan is a thing I made and can still use on a hot day; that is **functional art**.
• Care: scissors cut paper, not hair or fingers; the glue pot is closed again; the table is wiped clean.
• We end with a **fan wave**: everybody stands, counts four, and waves their own fan — the coolest class in the school.

**Words for my notebook**

A fan moves air and keeps us cool. Cardboard, handle, pattern, glue, dry, wave.

**Let us talk**

1. What do you use a fan for?
2. Which shape did you cut for your fan?
3. Say the colours of your pattern in order.
4. Why do we put the stick at the back and not in the middle?
5. What other thing at home moves air?


![A child holds up a folded hand fan marked with zigzags, dots and stripes, while scissors, a folded card and a stick lie on the table.](assets/img/nursery-2/2nd-term/cultural-and-creative-arts/week1-the-fan-i-made.png)
**Worksheet**

1. A fan is used to A) keep us cool B) cut paper C) carry books D) boil water
2. The stick we hold a fan by is the A) cover B) wing C) spine D) handle
3. A design that repeats is called a A) pattern B) song C) picture D) story
4. Which material is best for a paddle fan? A) cardboard B) cloth of water C) tissue paper D) a leaf only
5. Fill in: The two kinds of fan we can make are the paddle fan and the ____ fan.
6. Say three materials you used to make your fan.
7. Write two sentences on your fan: its shape, its colours and its pattern.

**My own work**

Wave-test your fan at home on a hot afternoon and write one line about whether it threw enough air; then draw its pattern again in your book.

### WEEK 2 — COLOUR SPLASH ART

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Creativity · Second Term W7 (pp 568–571).*

**Main content**

• **Colour splash (splatter) art** is made by throwing small drops of paint on paper — not by drawing lines.
• Before paint touches anything: **apron on, table covered, sleeves up**; paint that dries on cloth is very hard to remove.
• Three ways to splash: **flick** the wet brush with a finger, **drop** paint from a height, or **tap** the brush against a stick.
• From high up the drops are small and many; from low down they are big and few — that is how I control the mess.
• A toothbrush and a comb make a fine spray: pull the brush teeth with a finger over the comb.
• Two colours mixed: blue + yellow = green · red + yellow = orange · red + blue = purple.
• Water makes the colour light; little paint and much water give a pale splash.
• **Abstract art** does not look like a thing; it shows a **feeling** — fast lines for joy, dark splashes for anger.
• A famous splash painter, **Jackson Pollock**, laid his paper on the floor and moved the paint with sticks and his body.
• Name the feeling in your own splash: is it a happy day or a rainy day?
• Cleaning up: brush in the water pot, drops wiped from the table, hands washed with soap, paper left flat to dry.

**Words for my notebook**

Splash, flick, drop. Blue and yellow make green. Art that shows a feeling and not a thing is called abstract.

**Let us talk**

1. What do we wear before painting?
2. Show me the hand movement for a flick.
3. Which two colours make purple?
4. Is your splash fast or quiet? What feeling is in it?


![A child in a tied apron flicks a brush over one sheet of paper so the splashes land as blobs of many sizes, with two plain pots at the corner of the table.](assets/img/nursery-2/2nd-term/cultural-and-creative-arts/week2-the-brush-flick.png)
**Worksheet**

1. Colour splash art is made with drops of A) chalk B) water only C) paint D) clay
2. Before we splash paint we put on an A) apron B) hat only C) school shoe D) glove of straw
3. Blue and yellow mixed together give A) orange B) brown C) green D) black
4. Art that shows a feeling instead of a thing is A) abstract B) a map C) a letter D) a copy
5. Fill in: To make a fine spray we use a toothbrush and a ____.
6. Mention two ways of making drops fall on the paper.
7. Write two sentences on the feeling your splash picture shows.

**My own work**

Make one splash picture on a small sheet that says "rainy day" and one that says "party day", and write the feeling under each.

### WEEK 3 — SPIKY HEDGEHOG

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 2 · Creativity · Second Term W8 (pp 415–417); Nursery 3 · Second Term W8 (pp 568–571).*

**Main content**

• A **hedgehog** is a small animal with a **round body, a pointed nose, short legs and many spikes** on its back.
• It rolls into a ball of spikes when it is frightened; the soft front is what it keeps safe.
• Draw the body as a big half circle, then add a pointed nose, two eyes and four small feet.
• For the spikes, **dip a fork in paint and print it** across the back — the fork teeth leave a fan of lines.
• Other spike ways: glue **cut paper strips** standing up, press on **safe little twigs**, or draw spikes with a pencil.
• **Mixed media** means using more than one material in one picture: paint + paper + twigs.
• **Texture** is how a thing would feel: smooth, rough, spiky, soft — a hedgehog picture should feel rough.
• Colours of a hedgehog: brown, grey, dark tan, with a lighter face.
• The background is its **habitat**: grass, dry leaves, a garden wall, the moon at night (it walks at night).
• Glue is spread with a stick, not a finger; too much glue makes the paper wet through.
• The finished hedgehog goes on the class board under the title "Spiky and Safe".

**Words for my notebook**

A hedgehog has a round body, a pointed nose and spikes on its back. Fork print, paper strips and twigs all make texture.

**Let us talk**

1. What does a hedgehog do when it is afraid?
2. Which part of the hedgehog is soft?
3. Show me how you press the fork for spikes.
4. Name two materials you used besides paint.


![A child kneels beside a paper hedgehog and presses a fork along its back to make the spikes, with paper strips and a little pile of leaves near by.](assets/img/nursery-2/2nd-term/cultural-and-creative-arts/week3-fork-pressed-spikes.png)
**Worksheet**

1. A hedgehog's back is covered with A) spikes B) hair only C) feathers D) scales
2. The front of the hedgehog ends in a A) big ear B) pointed nose C) shell D) long tail
3. Using paint, paper and twigs together in one picture is called A) modelled art B) mixed media C) mixing D) splash art
4. How a thing would feel is its A) texture B) name C) colour D) size
5. Fill in: A hedgehog rolls into a ____ when it is frightened.
6. Name three ways of making spikes for your hedgehog.
7. Draw a hedgehog, give it spikes and one line of grass under it.

**My own work**

Make a second hedgehog in the same size from only paper (no paint), then compare the two and say which one feels spikier.

### WEEK 4 — PAPER COLLAGE AND THE FRIENDSHIP TREE

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 2 · Creativity · Second Term W9 (Paper Collage, pp 415–417) and Third Term W1 (Friendship Tree, pp 418–420) — the school keeps both in one week, so this note takes both.*

**Main content**

• A **collage** is a picture made by **sticking pieces of material** on a surface.
• Tearing gives soft, feathered edges; cutting gives hard, straight edges — good pictures use both.
• Fill the inside of the shape, not outside it: the drawing is the wall of the room.
• Small pieces go near the edge, bigger ones in the middle — the shape stays tidy.
• **Composition** means how things are put together: what is big, what is small, what goes in front.
• Glue is spread little by little with a stick; a wet finger makes the paper wrinkle.
• The **friendship tree**: draw a strong trunk and wide branches; the leaves are our hand prints or cut paper leaves.
• One leaf carries one name — my own, my friends', my class mates' — so the tree is the class.
• A tree with many leaves stands better than one with two; so everybody's name goes on.
• Collage work takes **patience**: it is slow, and slow work looks careful.
• When it is finished we stand back and look: can we see the shape of the tree? Are the names readable?

**Words for my notebook**

Collage means sticking torn or cut paper into a shape. Our friendship tree carries a name on every leaf.

**Let us talk**

1. What is a collage?
2. Which edge is nicer for the tree trunk — torn or cut?
3. How many names are on your tree?
4. Why is patience part of art?


![Torn scraps of paper are glued inside a drawn tree whose leaf shapes are left empty, with a bowl of scraps and a stick of glue on the table.](assets/img/nursery-2/2nd-term/cultural-and-creative-arts/week4-the-tree-of-torn-paper.png)
**Worksheet**

1. A collage is a picture made by A) pouring water B) sticking pieces on a surface C) tying ropes D) singing
2. Torn paper gives an edge that is A) hard B) soft and feathered C) hot D) straight
3. A tree whose leaves carry names in our class is the A) palm tree B) mango tree only C) friendship tree D) family tree
4. How the parts of a picture are put together is its A) glue B) paper C) composition D) size
5. Fill in: Glue is spread little by little with a ____.
6. Mention two things you cut or tore for your collage.
7. Write two sentences on why everybody's name was needed on the friendship tree.

**My own work**

Cut five small pieces of paper and make a house on a fresh sheet with them: roof, wall, door and a garden.

### WEEK 5 — MAKING JEWELLERY

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 2 · Creativity · Third Term W2 (pp 418–420); Nursery 3 · Second Term W11 (pp 568–571).*

**Main content**

• **Jewellery** is the ornament people wear: necklace, bracelet, ring, anklet and bead for the wrist.
• Safe materials: **big beads, painted pasta shells, buttons, paper beads, pieces of straw, thread or yarn**.
• **Paper beads**: cut a long triangle of paper, roll it from the wide end on a stick, glue the tip, and let it dry — it becomes hard like wood.
• A **pattern** or **sequence** is an order that repeats: red, red, blue · red, red, blue.
• Count as you thread: two small, one big, two small, one big.
• Colour matching: yellow looks bright beside blue; white goes with everything; too many colours make the pattern hard to see.
• The thread must be **knot-ty at the end**; a knot is tied before threading so the beads do not fall off the back.
• To finish: tie the two ends twice and pull — a bracelet is a ring with a knot.
• Safety: beads are **not for the mouth, the nose or the ear**; a small bead swallowed is dangerous and must be reported at once.
• Scissors cut the thread only; the needle (a blunt plastic one) is carried point-down.
• We wear our own work for one song, then the jewellery goes home in a pouch, not in a pocket.

**Words for my notebook**

Necklace, bracelet, ring. Red, red, blue — the pattern repeats. Beads never go into the mouth.

**Let us talk**

1. Say the pattern of your necklace in words.
2. How do we stop the beads falling off the thread?
3. Which colours did you choose, and why?
4. Name one thing that is never safe to put in your mouth.


![A half-made necklace lies in a loop on the mat with a short bracelet, one ring and a little bowl of spare beads.](assets/img/nursery-2/2nd-term/cultural-and-creative-arts/week5-the-beads-strung.png)
**Worksheet**

1. Which of these is jewellery worn on the wrist? A) collar B) bracelet C) anklet D) necklace
2. A roll of paper glued and dried can be used as a A) plate B) bead C) cup D) shoe
3. Two small, one big, two small, one big is a A) colour B) song C) story D) pattern
4. Before we tie the ends we must make a A) drawing B) hole C) mark of chalk D) knot
5. Fill in: A bead must never be put into the ____.
6. Name three materials we threaded with.
7. Write two sentences on the necklace you made and the pattern in it.

**My own work**

Make one bracelet for somebody at home from the last of your beads, and say what colours you chose for them.

### WEEK 6 — RUBBER AND FOAM COLLAGE

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Creativity · Third Term W2 (pp 572–575).*

**Main content**

• A **foam collage** is a picture made by sticking **cut pieces of foam or soft rubber** on paper.
• Foam comes in **craft foam sheets**, in old **slippers**, in **sponge** and in the packing foam things are sold in.
• Foam is soft, so the scissors must be **sharp and small**, and the cut is made on a board, not on the hand.
• **Flat collage** lies on the paper; **3D (dimensional) collage** rises up from it because pieces are stacked.
• **Layering** means sticking one piece on top of another: a big green leaf, then a small dark green leaf on it.
• Layering makes the picture look near and far: the biggest piece is nearest to me.
• Foam holds its shape, so a foam animal has a body that stands out; paint on it looks soft, not shiny.
• Glue for foam must be thick (paste or fabric glue); watery glue lets the piece lift up again after a while.
• Weight matters: too many layers make the paper bend — the base sheet should be card, not foolscap.
• **Spatial awareness** is what I am learning here: which piece goes above, below, beside or behind another.
• Clean hands before the work is done; foam scraps go in the box, not on the floor for the class to slip on.

**Words for my notebook**

Cut, layer, press. A flat collage lies down; a 3D collage stands up.

**Let us talk**

1. Where does the biggest piece of foam go?
2. What kind of glue holds foam best?
3. Name one thing you would not cut foam on.
4. Is your picture flat or raised? Show me.


![A child cuts a fish shape out of foam while two foam pieces already lie glued in layers on a card, one standing proud of the other.](assets/img/nursery-2/2nd-term/cultural-and-creative-arts/week6-foam-cut-in-layers.png)
**Worksheet**

1. A foam collage is a picture made of A) song B) water C) sand only D) cut foam pieces
2. A picture that rises up from the paper is called A) 3D B) old C) quiet D) flat
3. Sticking one piece on top of another is A) waving B) counting C) tearing D) layering
4. Which base is best for a heavy foam picture? A) cardboard B) a leaf C) cloth of water D) foolscap paper
5. Fill in: Foam is soft, so we cut it on a ____, not on the hand.
6. Mention two things that can give us foam for collage.
7. Write two sentences on your foam picture: what you cut and what you layered.

**My own work**

Cut three foam shapes and build an animal with them on a card — two shapes must be layered under the others.

### WEEK 8 — SAND COLLAGE

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 2 · Creativity · Third Term W4 (pp 418–420); Nursery 3 · Third Term W3 (pp 572–575).*

**Main content**

• A **sand collage** is a picture with parts of it covered in sand, so that it feels **rough and grainy**.
• Steps: draw the picture → paint glue on one small area at a time → **sprinkle** sand over it → **shake off** what did not stick → let it dry.
• Glue the parts first with clean paper beside them, or the sand will stick everywhere.
• **Natural sand** is pale yellow; sand can be coloured by mixing it with a little dry cement or paint powder and drying it on paper.
• Different sands give different feelings: fine sand is smooth, coarse sand is rough, gravel is bumpy.
• Sand is a **natural material** — like clay, stick, leaf and shell, it came from the earth and not from a factory.
• Sections: sky (no sand), beach (fine sand), sea (blue paper), rocks (grit) — the eye sees the difference.
• Sprinkle low and gently; from a height the sand flies out of the shape.
• Sand goes on the paper, not on the floor, and never near the eyes; hands are washed after the work.
• Extra sand is poured back into the tin — it is still good for the next class.
• A sand picture is finished when the glue is dry and the surface is firm: touch it and it should not move.

**Words for my notebook**

Glue a small area, sprinkle, shake off the extra, let it dry. Sand is a natural material; it gives texture.

**Let us talk**

1. What does a sand picture feel like?
2. Why do we put glue only on part of the drawing?
3. Name one other thing that comes from the earth and is used in art.
4. What do we do with the sand that does not stick?


![A fish is drawn on the paper with its glue patch inside, a cup lies tipped and the spare sand waits on a plate.](assets/img/nursery-2/2nd-term/cultural-and-creative-arts/week8-the-sand-and-the-fish.png)
**Worksheet**

1. In a sand collage, sand sticks because of A) glue B) sun C) water D) chalk
2. After sprinkling sand we ____ A) burn it B) press with the foot C) shake off the extra D) wet it again
3. Sand, clay and shells are A) painted things B) natural materials C) factory things D) soft foods
4. Which part of a beach picture is best left without sand? A) the rocks B) the path C) the sand bank D) the sky
5. Fill in: Coarse sand feels ____ while fine sand feels smooth.
6. Say the four steps of putting sand on a picture, in order.
7. Write two sentences on the texture of your sand picture and where you got the sand.

**My own work**

Collect a little sand at home, dry it on paper in the sun, and use it to fill one shape you have drawn.

### WEEK 9 — DESIGNING A CONE HAT

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 2 · Creativity · Third Term W8 (pp 418–420); Nursery 3 · Third Term W8 (pp 572–575).*

**Main content**

• A **cone hat** is a hat that comes to a point at the top, like a party hat or the cap of a fishing net.
• Roll a **fan-shaped piece** of cardboard: the wide arc becomes the bottom, the point becomes the top.
• A **sector** is that fan shape — a circle with a wedge cut out of it.
• Hold the roll with a clothes pin while the glue dries; tape or staples can do the same work.
• Do not roll it too tight — the head must go inside; try it on a friend's head before the glue sets.
• Decorate with paint, crayons, **stickers, ribbons, pom-poms, paper stars and streamers**; a hat is the place for the brightest colours.
• A band of paper round the base hides the rough edge and makes the hat stronger.
• To keep it on the head, thread an **elastic or string** through two holes at the base and tie it.
• The **hat parade**: one by one, walk to the drum, turn at the end of the line, and come back; nobody laughs at another's hat.
• Wearable art is 3D art — it moves with the body, so it must be light and must not cover the eyes.
• When the parade is over, the hat is kept on a shelf, not folded — a cone that is crushed cannot be opened again.

**Words for my notebook**

Roll, hold, glue, dry, decorate, wear. A cone hat has a point at the top and a band at the base.

**Let us talk**

1. What shape must the paper be before you roll it?
2. What holds the roll while the glue dries?
3. Which decorations did you put on your hat?
4. Why must the hat be light?


![The finished cone hat stands on the table with its ribbon, two stars and a zigzag, beside a rolled strip held by a peg.](assets/img/nursery-2/2nd-term/cultural-and-creative-arts/week9-the-cone-hat-and-the-peg.png)
**Worksheet**

1. A cone hat ends at the top in a A) point B) flat board C) hole D) wheel
2. Before rolling, the paper is cut in the shape of a A) square B) triangle of cloth C) long rope D) circle with a wedge out
3. While the glue dries, the roll can be held with a A) shoe B) stone C) clothes pin D) wet cloth

6. Mention four things used to decorate a cone hat.
7. Write two sentences on your hat and what happened in the class hat parade.

**My own work**

Make a small cone hat for a doll or a bottle from a leftover piece of paper, and give it one pattern of three repeating colours.
