### WEEK 1 — REVIEW OF THREE-LETTER WORDS (CVC)

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy (Letter Work) · Second Term W1 (pp 458–464).*

**You will learn to**

• blend three sounds into a word and read it
• read and write the word families -at, -an, -en, -in, -og, -un, -op
• match each word to its picture
• write words as they are said to me

**Things to know**

• **CVC** means **consonant – vowel – consonant**: three letters, one vowel in the middle — **c a t**, **p e n**, **b e d**.
• Blending is saying the sounds and running them together: **"c… a… t… cat"** — never say the letter names ("see-ay-tee"), say the **sounds** (/k/-/æ/-/t/).
• The trick that keeps the sound clean: **do not add "uh"** — /m/ is a hum, not "muh".
• Six families to read this week, one line each:
 **–at**: cat · bat · hat · mat · rat · sat · fat
 **–an**: fan · man · pan · can · ran · tan
 **–en**: pen · hen · ten · men · wen
 **–in**: pin · tin · win · fin · sin · kin
 **–og**: dog · log · fog · hog · jog
 **–un**: sun · bun · run · fun · gun · nun
• **–op**: mop · top · hop · pop · stop · prop — and note that **cup, pup, sup** belong to the **-up** family, not this one
• Change the **first** sound and the word changes: **cat → bat → hat → mat → rat**; the **-at** stays the same.
• Change the **middle** sound: **bat → bet → bit → bot → but** — five words, one letter apart.
• Read a whole line of a family, then read it backwards (top, pop, mop, hop) — the eye must not need the order.
• **Matching to pictures**: the picture of a **fan** goes with f-a-n; say the word slowly and count the sounds — three sounds, three letters.
• **Dictation**: I hear the word, I say it in sounds, then I write the letters under my own voice; then I read back what I wrote.
• Writing with a pencil: the letters sit on the line; **a, c, e, g, i, m, n, o, p, r, s, u, v, w, z** are small letters only in these words. And the first word of nothing needs a big letter here.
• Confidence rule: if I stumble, I go back to the **first sound** and start again slowly — nobody is laughed at for reading slowly.

**Words for my notebook**

cat, hat, mat · pen, hen, ten · dog, log, fog · sun, bun, run. Three sounds make one word.

**Let us talk**

1. Blend this for me: /k/ /æ/ /t/.
2. What word family is "pin, tin, win"?
3. Change one sound in "bat" to make a new word.
4. How many sounds are in "dog"?

### WEEK 2 — FOUR-LETTER WORDS: CVCC PATTERN (PART 1)

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy (Letter Work) · Second Term W2 (pp 458–464).*

**You will learn to**

• read four-letter words that end in two consonants
• blend the ending -nd, -nt and -st
• write CVCC words from a picture
• say what the last two letters do to the word

**Things to know**

• A **CVCC** word has **four letters**: consonant, vowel, consonant, consonant — the last two consonants stand together at the **end**: **hand**, **tent**, **nest**.
• We already know three-letter words; here we **add one more sound at the tail** — that is all that changes.
• The **-nd** family: **hand · sand · land · band · mend · wind** — the **d** is quiet in the mouth but it is heard at the end.
• The **-nt** family: **bent · sent · tent · went · rent · mint** — after the **t** the mouth just stops; we do not add "uh".
• The **-st** family: **best · rest · test · west · nest · last** — the **t** at the end is a small tap.
• Blend the **four** sounds: **h-a-n-d → hand**; say them slowly, then faster, then as one word; count on the fingers: four sounds, four letters.
• Read a line where the head changes but the tail stays: **ban · band · sand · stand** — one new letter makes a new word.
• Sort the words: which family does **"gift"** go in? — **ft**, not this week's line; **which does "land" go in? — nd**.
• Picture work: the picture of a **tent** goes with t-e-n-t; a picture of a **band** (a rubber band) with b-a-n-d; the hand with h-a-n-d.
• Write from a dictation: **b-e-d** is three sounds; **s-e-n-t** is four — the number of sounds must match the number of letters.
• Meaning: a **hand** is a body part, a **band** holds hair or paper, **sand** is what we play with, **land** is the ground under us — the last two letters stay, the first two change the meaning.
• Read the sentence: **"The hen went to the tent."** — say which words are CVCC in it.

**Words for my notebook**

hand, sand, land · bent, sent, tent, went · best, rest, nest, west. Four sounds, four letters.

**Let us talk**

1. How many sounds are in "hand"?
2. Say three words that end in -nt.
3. What is the difference between "sand" and "band"?
4. Read the sentence "The hen went to the tent" and pick out the four-letter words.

### WEEK 3 — FOUR-LETTER WORDS: CVCC PATTERN (PART 2)

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy (Letter Work) · Second Term W3 (pp 458–464).*

**You will learn to**

• read the ending families -mp, -nk and -ft
• blend four sounds in a new word without stopping
• read a sentence full of CVCC words
• write four-letter words from my own speaking

**Things to know**

• The **-mp** family: **jump · lamp · camp · bump · damp · stamp** — after the **m**, the lips close again for the **p**; that is why -mp looks like two lips sounds together.
• The **-nk** family: **bank · tank · pink · sink · wink · bunk** — the nose sound **n** slides into the **k**; say "nk" without a vowel between.
• The **-ft** family: **left · gift · lift · soft · raft** — the **f** bites the lip and the **t** taps.
• Three new endings plus last week's three make **six tails**: -nd, -nt, -st, -mp, -nk, -ft; a four-letter word is now something I can attack, not guess.
• Blend and read: **c-l-a-m-p?** (that's CLVC) — read: **jump, lamp, camp, damp** — the vowel **u, a, a, a** changes the head, the tail stays.
• Change the head, keep the tail: **pink → wink → sink → bunk**; **gift → lift → left → soft**.
• Read the sentences aloud, one breath each:
 *"The **lamp** is on the **bank**."* · *"Do not **jump** in the **damp**."* · *"She put the **gift** on the **soft** sand."*
• **Writing practice**: say the word in sounds, write the letters, then count the sounds and the letters again — four and four.
• **Dictation** list for the week: jump, bank, pink, gift, lamp, sink, soft, camp.
• Word recognition game: the flash card is shown for a count of three; I must say the word, and to do that I must read the **whole** word, not the first letter.
• Meaning of the hard ones: a **bank** is the side of a river or the place money is kept; a **tank** holds water; a **camp** is where we sleep in tents; **damp** is wet in a cold way.
• Write your own line: *"I can jump."* — read it back with the finger under every word.

**Words for my notebook**

jump, lamp, camp · bank, tank, pink, sink · left, gift, lift, soft. Six tails: nd, nt, st, mp, nk, ft.

**Let us talk**

1. Say the six tails we have learnt in two weeks.
2. What sound do the lips make at the end of "jump"?
3. Read: "The lamp is on the bank."
4. Give a word that rhymes with "wink".

### WEEK 4 — FOUR-LETTER WORDS: CCVC PATTERN

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy (Letter Work) · Second Term W4 (pp 458–464).*

**You will learn to**

• read words that start with two consonants
• say the **l** blends and the **r** blends
• blend a consonant cluster without putting a sound between
• write CCVC words

**Things to know**

• A **CCVC** word has **two consonants at the front** and then a vowel and a consonant: **blu-e → blue**, **flag**, **drum**, **tree**.
• Two consonants that run together are called a **blend**; a blend is still said **sound by sound**, fast — not "buh-lag" but /bl/ag.
• **The l-blends** (the second sound is **l**):
 **bl** — blue, black, block, blind · **cl** — clap, clip, club, clay · **fl** — flag, flip, flat, fly
 **gl** — glad, glue, glow, globe · **pl** — play, plug, plan, plum · **sl** — slap, slip, sled, slow
• **The r-blends** (the second sound is **r**):
 **br** — brush, brick, bring, brown · **cr** — crab, crop, cry, crown · **dr** — drum, drip, drop, draw
 **fr** — frog, from, free, front · **gr** — grip, grab, grass, grey · **pr** — pray, prop, press, pride · **tr** — tree, trip, tray, troop
• Say the pair first: **"fl — fla — flag"**; then add the tail: **/fl/ + /a/ + /g/ → flag**.
• Tricky: **gl** is not **kl**; **br** is not "bur"; keep the **r** growl, do not turn it into a vowel.
• Sort the words by their blend — the **l-blends**: flag, blue, play · the **r-blends**: crab, drum, grass, frog, tree
• Read the sentence: **"The frog can sit by the green tree and grab a fly."** — say every word that starts with a blend.
• **Writing**: the two first letters are close together, no space inside a blend — **f-l-a-g**, not "f lag".
• Put a blend in front of a family you already know: **-ap** → **clap, flap, slap, gap** · **-ag** → **flag, drag, brag** (drag and brag are new — learn these two only) · **-op** → **stop, flop**
• Play the blend game: I pull a blend card (br) and a family card (-ick) and say the word: **brick**; the class says it back and writes it.
• End of the week: read a page of mixed four-letter words — CVCC and CCVC together, without a finger.

**Words for my notebook**

bl, cl, fl, gl, pl, sl · br, cr, dr, fr, gr, pr, tr · flag, drum, tree, blue, frog, clap.

**Let us talk**

1. What is a blend?
2. Say three words that begin with fl.
3. Which blend starts the word "grass"?
4. Read the sentence about the frog and the tree.


![A blank flag on a short pole, a drum on its side with two sticks crossed, and a frog sitting with its webbed feet showing.](assets/img/nursery-2/2nd-term/mathematics-english-literacy/week4-the-flag-the-drum-and-the-frog.png)
**Worksheet**

1. A CCVC word begins with A) a full stop B) one letter C) two consonants D) two vowels
2. Which two sounds make the blend in "flag"? A) l and a B) f and l C) f and r D) a and g
3. crab, crop and crown all begin with A) cl B) br C) gr D) cr
4. Which word begins with the blend dr? A) tray B) grip C) draw D) play
5. Fill in: In "blue", the first two letters are the blend ____.
6. Write three words beginning with bl and three beginning with tr.
7. Read a CCVC word and a CVCC word of your own, and say what is different about them.

Make two columns in your book: l-blends and r-blends. Write four words under each, and put a small picture beside one word in each column.

### WEEK 5 — INTRODUCTION TO SIGHT WORDS (PART 1)

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy (Letter Work) · Second Term W7 (pp 458–464).*

**You will learn to**

• say what a sight word is
• read ten sight words at once without blending
• use the words in a simple sentence
• play the flash-card and memory games with them

**Things to know**

• A **sight word** is a word we see **so often** that we know it by its **shape**, the way we know a face — we do not blend it.
• Why not blend? Because many of them break the rules: **"the"** is not "t-h-e" said fast; we just **know** it.
• The ten words of this week: **I · a · the · is · am · and · it · in · at · on**.
• **I** is always a big letter, even in the middle of a sentence — because it is me.
• **a** is one small letter; **the** is three; both tell us which thing we mean: "Give me **a** cup" (any cup) vs "Give me **the** cup" (that cup).
• **is / am** say what a thing **is**: "It **is** a cat." "I **am** four."
• **and** joins: "The dog **and** the hen."
• **in / at / on** are place words: the pen is **in** the bag; I am **at** school; the cup is **on** the table.
• Read them by the **eye and no more**: the card shows for three seconds — say the word, do not sound it.
• Sentences to read and copy: **"I am a boy."** · **"It is a cat."** · **"The pen is in the bag."** · **"I am at school."**
• Flash-card drill: ten cards, shuffled; put them in the order you know them best to worst; move the worst three to the front of the pack every day.
• **Memory game**: the cards lie face down; turn two; if they are the same word, keep them and say a sentence with it.
• **Matching to pictures**: **on** goes with the cup above the table; **in** goes with the pen inside the bag — the picture and the word must agree.
• Writing: **a, it, in, on, at** are short words but they must be written **on the line**, not floating under it; **the** has the tall **h**; **I** is tall alone.
• Sight words read fast is the beginning of reading for pleasure: when these ten are free in my eye, my brain can think about the story instead of the letters.

**Words for my notebook**

I · a · the · is · am · and · it · in · at · on — read them with your eyes, not your mouth.

**Let us talk**

1. What is a sight word?
2. Why is "I" always a big letter?
3. Say a sentence with "in" and one with "on".
4. Which word joins two things — "and" or "the"?

### WEEK 6 — INTRODUCTION TO SIGHT WORDS (PART 2)

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy (Letter Work) · Second Term W8 (pp 458–464).*

**You will learn to**

• learn ten more sight words
• read them quickly in a game of bingo
• put sight words together with CVC words to make a sentence
• write sentences that begin with a big letter

**Things to know**

• The ten new words: **he · she · we · me · my · to · go · no · up · can**.
• **he** is for a boy or a man · **she** is for a girl or a woman · **we** is me and you together · **me** is me when somebody does something to me: "Give it to **me**."
• **my** says a thing belongs to me: "This is **my** bag."
• **to** shows where a thing goes: "I go **to** school." · **up** is the other side of **down**: "Look **up**."
• **go** and **no** look alike but sound different — **no** begins with the nose, **go** begins with the throat: the **g** is the difference.
• **can** says I am able: "I **can** run." — the sentence everybody likes best.
• Twenty sight words are now in my eye (this week's ten plus last week's ten); the pack of cards grows by ten.
• Sentences mixing sight words and CVC words: **"I can run."** · **"We go up."** · **"She can see a hen."** · **"He can pat the dog."** · **"My pen is in the bag."**

• Speed rule: the word must be said in **less than one second** — this is the only reading that is allowed to be fast, because it is already known.
• Writing: begin the sentence with a **big letter** and end it with a **full stop**; leave a **space** between words — "Icanrun" is three words crushed together.
• Build a sentence with cards: put the word cards on the table in an order, read it, then move one card and read the new sentence.
• Reading at home this week: find one of these words on every page of your story book and put a tick beside it — the words are everywhere.

### WEEK 8 — SIMPLE SENTENCE READING AND WRITING

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy (Letter Work) · Second Term W9 (pp 458–464).*

**You will learn to**

• say what a sentence is
• read simple sentences with the right stops
• begin with a capital letter and end with a full stop
• write my own three-line sentences

**Things to know**

• A **sentence** is a group of words that makes **one complete sense**: "The cat sat on the mat" tells me a whole thing — a **who** and a **what he did**.
• Two things a sentence must have: a **name of a thing or person** (who/what) and **what about it** (did/is/saw) — "cat the on" is a heap of words, not a sentence.
• **Three rules of writing a sentence** — the sentence uniform: 1) a **capital (big) letter** at the start, 2) a **space between every word**, 3) a **full stop** at the end.
• Why the spaces? Without them the eye cannot find where one word stops and the next begins: **thecat** is one long mistake.
• Why the full stop? It tells the reader: *here, breathe* — and it tells the writer: *that thought is finished*.
• Read these, one breath per sentence: *"The cat sat on the mat."* · *"I can see a dog."* · *"Ben has a red pen."* · *"We went to the shop."* · *"She is in the bath."*
• Reading with the finger under the line, then without: the finger helps the eye not to jump back.
• **Matching sentences to pictures**: the picture of a cat on a mat goes with "The cat sat on the mat" — not with "The dog ran".
• **Oral sentence making**: I look at a thing in the class and say one sentence about it; then a friend makes a second sentence about the same thing.
• Build a sentence from word cards: **The / dog / is / on / the / mat** → put the cards in order, read it, then move one card and read the new sentence.
• Sentence length: three words is already a sentence ("I am Ben"); six words is a good one; more than eight is where the marks are lost, so keep mine short.
• Our class book: each child writes one sentence on a strip; the strips are pinned in order and read together on Friday as "Our Book, Part One".

**Words for my notebook**

A sentence says a whole thing. Big letter first, space between, full stop last.

**Let us talk**

1. What three things does a written sentence need?
2. Say a sentence about the board.
3. Which of these is a sentence: "the red pen" or "The pen is red."?
4. Why do we put spaces between words?

### WEEK 9 — RHYMING WORDS AND WORD FAMILIES

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy (Letter Work) · Second Term W10 (pp 458–464).*

**You will learn to**

• say what rhyming is and hear it in a word
• group words into families by their ending sound
• make up my own rhyme
• tell why rhyming helps me read

**Things to know**

• Two words **rhyme** when they sound the **same at the end** — the beginning is different: **cat / hat**, **pen / ten**.
• **cat, bat, rat** are one **word family**: they share the **-at**; the first letter changes, the tail sound stays.
• Rhyming is heard, not seen: **boat** and **coat** rhyme, but **cat** and **cart** do not — so say them aloud and listen.
• Families to sort into lines: **-at** (cat, hat, mat, rat, bat) · **-en** (pen, hen, ten, men) · **-og** (dog, log, fog, jog) · **-ump** (jump, bump, lump, stump) · **-ight** (light, night, fight, might) · **-and** (hand, sand, land, band).
• Find the odd one out: **pin, tin, cup, win** → cup (it is -up); **map, nap, tap, hop** → hop.
• Rhymes are inside our songs and poems: *"Jack and Jill went up the **hill** / **pail** came **tumbling** down"* — **hill / will / still** rhyme. And **pail / tail / whale** rhyme; the ending sound, not the spelling, is what matters.
• Play the rhyme chain: I say **sun**, you say **bun**, the next says **run** — the chain goes round the class and does not break.
• **Making my own rhyme**: take a name and match it — *"Kemi, Kemi **dreamy**"*, *"Ada with the **paddle**"* — then every child makes one line, no matter how funny it sounds.
• Listening game: I say two words; if they rhyme, clap once; if not, keep the hands down — **bed/red** clap, **bed/bag** nothing.
• Why it matters: a child who hears that **cat, hat, sat** end the same way will read a new word **-at** word he has never seen — rhyming is how the eye learns to predict.
• Finish with the rhyme we chant and stamp: *"This is the way we **read** our words, / read our words, read our words; / this is the way we read our words, / blend them all together!"*

**Words for my notebook**

Rhyming words sound the same at the end: cat–hat, pen–ten, jump–bump, hand–sand.

**Let us talk**

1. What is rhyming?
2. Say three words that rhyme with "log".
3. Do "boat" and "coat" rhyme? Do "cat" and "cart"? Why?
4. Say one rhyme line you made yourself.

**Worksheet**

1. Words that rhyme A) sound the same at the end B) are sight words C) begin the same D) are long
2. Which word rhymes with "pen"? A) ten B) pin C) pan D) pit
3. cat, hat and sat belong to the word family A) -an B) -og C) -en D) -at
4. Which pair does NOT rhyme? A) hand/sand B) bed/bag C) boat/coat D) light/night
5. Fill in: jump, bump and lump all belong to the ____ family.
6. Write two lines of your own rhyme and put a line under the words that rhyme.
7. Say five words from the -og family and then five from the -and family.

**My own work**

Choose one word, find six words that rhyme with it, and write a two-line rhyme using three of them for a friend to read.
