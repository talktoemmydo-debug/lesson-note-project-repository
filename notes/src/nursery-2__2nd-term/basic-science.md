### WEEK 1 — FOOD: WHY WE EAT, AND WHERE FOOD COMES FROM

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Basic Science and Technology · Second Term W2 (pp 494–497).*

**You will learn to**

• say what food is
• give reasons why people eat
• name the three main meals of the day
• say which foods come from plants and which come from animals

**Things to know**

• **Food** is anything we eat or drink that gives the body energy and helps it to grow.
• We eat to get **energy** for work and for play.
• We eat to **grow** tall and strong, to **stay healthy** and fight sickness, and to **repair** the body when we are hurt.
• Food makes us feel **satisfied and happy**; a hungry child cannot learn.
• **Everything living needs food** — people, animals and plants too.
• The three main meals are **breakfast** (morning), **lunch** (midday) and **dinner** (evening).
• A **snack** is a small food between meals; groundnut, fruit or yogurt is a good snack, sweets are not.
• Plant foods: yam, rice, beans, maize, plantain, vegetables, fruit, palm oil.
• Animal foods: eggs, milk, meat, fish, snails.
• Different people eat different foods, and that is good — one family eats rice, another eats yam.
• We wash our hands and the food before we eat, and we do not eat food that has fallen on the floor.

**Words for my notebook**

Food is anything we eat or drink that gives us energy and helps us to grow. Breakfast, lunch and dinner.

**Let us talk**

2. Why do we eat food?
3. Name one food that comes from an animal.
4. Which of your meals is the biggest?
5. Whose food at home is different from yours?

### WEEK 2 — LIVING THINGS AND NON-LIVING THINGS

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Basic Science and Technology · Second Term W4 (pp 494–497).*

**You will learn to**

• say what a living thing is
• name the signs of life
• sort things into living and non-living
• observe living things around me

**Things to know**

• **Living things are things that have life.**
• Living things: people, animals, birds, fish, insects and plants.
• The seven signs of life — living things **move**, **grow**, **feed**, **breathe**, **have young ones**, **answer what happens around them**, and **get rid of waste**.
• Movement: an animal walks; a plant turns its leaves towards the light.
• Growth: a chick becomes a hen; a seedling becomes a big tree.
• Feeding: animals eat, plants take light and water from the soil.
• Breathing: every living thing takes in air; a fish breathes with gills in the water.
• Young ones: a hen lays eggs, a cow has a calf — that is **reproduction**.
• Answering the surroundings: a snail goes inside its shell when you touch it; you pull your hand away from something hot.
• Getting rid of waste: we pass urine and sweat; a tree drops its dry leaves — that is **excretion**.
• Non-living things — a stone, water, a chair, a bicycle — do not do any of these things by themselves.
• Living and non-living can go together: a river is non-living but fish live in it.

**Words for my notebook**

Living things have life. They move, grow, feed, breathe, have young ones, answer their surroundings and get rid of waste.

**Let us talk**

2. Name two things in this room that are not living.
3. What does a snail do when you touch it?
4. Do fish drink water? How do they breathe?
5. Say three signs you look for before you call a thing living.

### WEEK 3 — GROWTH IN ANIMALS

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Basic Science and Technology · Second Term W7 (pp 494–497).*

**You will learn to**

• say what growth means
• tell the young one of common animals
• put a life cycle in order
• say what animals need to grow

**Things to know**

• **Growth** is a change from small to big over a long time — not a thing that happens in one day.
• An animal's **life cycle** is the order of its growing: baby → young → adult.
• A human grows: **baby → toddler → child → teenager → adult**.
• A hen: **egg → chick → young fowl → hen or cockerel**.
• A dog: **puppy → young dog → adult dog**; a cat: **kitten → adult cat**.
• A frog: **egg → tadpole → froglet → frog** — the tadpole lives in water and swims like a fish.
• A butterfly: **egg → caterpillar → chrysalis (pupa) → butterfly**.
• As animals grow, their **size increases**, their **features develop** (wings, teeth, feathers) and at last they can **have young ones** of their own.
• Animals need **food, water, air and shelter** to grow; an animal without enough food stays small and weak.
• Baby animals that suck milk from their mothers are called **mammals** — dog, cat, cow, bat, and you.
• We can watch growth by keeping a snail, a fish or a chick in a box and writing what it does each day.

**Words for my notebook**

Growth is a slow change from small to big. Egg → chick → hen. A puppy grows into a dog.

**Let us talk**

2. Which animal gives birth to a puppy?
3. How is a tadpole different from a frog?
4. Name two things an animal needs to grow.
5. In what way are you bigger now than in Nursery 1?

### WEEK 4 — GROWTH IN PLANTS

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Basic Science and Technology · Second Term W8 (pp 494–497).*

**You will learn to**

• tell the stages of a plant's life
• name what a plant needs to grow
• plant a seed and care for it
• say what changes as a plant grows

**Things to know**

• A plant grows from a **seed** into a **mature plant**, and that plant makes **new seeds** again.
• The order is: **seed → germination → seedling → young plant → mature plant → flowers → fruits → seeds**.
• **Germination** means the seed begins to grow and pushes out its first root and shoot.
• The **root** grows down into the soil and holds the plant; the **shoot** grows up towards the light.
• Leaves come out, the stem becomes strong, flowers open, fruits form, and inside the fruit are seeds.
• A plant needs five things: **water, sunlight, air, soil (or nutrients) and space**.
• Keep a seed in a damp cloth and it will sprout without soil — but it needs soil later to stay strong.
• We plant beans in a tin of soil: water it a little every day, put it where the sun comes, and watch.
• Changes during growth: the plant gets taller, has more leaves, then flowers, then fruits.
• We do not pull young plants, we do not pour paraffin or salt on them, and we shade them from the hot afternoon sun.
• A plant that is not watered turns yellow and dries up; that is how we know it was alive.

**Words for my notebook**

Seed → seedling → young plant → big plant → flower → fruit → seed. A plant needs water, sunlight, air, soil and space.

**Let us talk**

2. Which part grows first, the root or the flower?

4. Name something that grows inside a fruit.

### WEEK 5 — WATER, AND HOW PEOPLE AND THINGS MOVE

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Basic Science and Technology · Second Term W9 (pp 494–497).*

**You will learn to**

• say what water is used for and where it comes from
• tell clean water from dirty water
• define transportation
• name means of transport on land, water and air
• keep safe while travelling

**Things to know**

• **Water** is used for drinking, cooking, bathing, washing, farming and for animals; living things cannot live long without it.
• Sources of water: **rain, river, stream, well, borehole and the tap**.
• Clean water is clear and has no bad smell; dirty water has mud, colour or smell in it.
• **Transportation** means moving people and things from one place to another.
• On **land**: car, bus, motorcycle, bicycle, lorry, train, wheelbarrow.
• On **water**: canoe, boat, ship, ferry.
• In **air**: aeroplane, helicopter.
• We travel to go to school, to work, to the market, to the hospital, and to move goods from farm to town.
• Safety in transport: **seat belt** in a car, **life jacket** on a boat, hold the rail on a bus, and follow the traffic lights and the zebra crossing.
• A motorcycle is dangerous without a **helmet**; we never sit on the back of a lorry.
• Water and transport meet: a river can be a road for a canoe, and a ferry carries cars across the water.

**Words for my notebook**

Water comes from rain, rivers, wells, boreholes and taps. Transport on land, on water and in the air. Buckle the seat belt, wear a life jacket.

### WEEK 6 — BONES: THE FRAME INSIDE MY BODY

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Basic Science and Technology · Second Term W10 (pp 494–497).*

**You will learn to**

• say what bones are and where they are
• name the big bones of the body
• tell what bones do for us
• say how to keep bones strong

**Things to know**

• **Bones** are the hard parts inside the body; all the bones together are the **skeleton**.
• Without bones the body would fall down like a sack of water.
• Bones give the body **shape**, they **support** it, they **protect** the soft parts inside, and with muscles they help us **move**.
• The **skull** (the bone of the head) protects the **brain**.
• The **ribs** are the bones of the chest; they protect the **heart** and the **lungs**.
• The **spine** is the bone of the back; it lets me bend and keeps me upright.
• The arm bones and the leg bones are long bones; the fingers have many small bones.
• Feel your arm: the hard part under the skin is the bone. Now bend it — that is the **joint**.
• Bones are still growing at my age; that is why a child must not lift heavy things.
• To keep bones strong: **milk, eggs, fish and beans** (calcium), playing and running, and sunlight in the morning.
• A broken bone is put in a plaster cast by a doctor and it joins again; we do not pull a hurt arm.

**Words for my notebook**

Bones are hard parts inside the body. The skeleton gives shape, supports, protects and helps me move. The skull guards the brain.

**Let us talk**

### WEEK 8 — TECHNOLOGY AND THE MOBILE PHONE

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Basic Science and Technology · Second Term W11 (pp 494–497).*

**You will learn to**

• say what technology means
• name the things people use every day because of technology
• tell what a mobile phone is used for
• keep myself safe with a phone

**Things to know**

• **Technology** means the tools and machines people make to do work more easily.
• Old way, new way: we once carried water in a clay pot; now we have a tap and a pump.
• Things made by technology: torch, fan, radio, refrigerator, sewing machine, bicycle, car, generator, phone.
• A **mobile phone** is a device for talking to people far away without a wire.
• A phone can **call**, send a **message**, **take pictures**, play music, and **teach** (letters, numbers, stories).
• A phone is very useful in an **emergency**: calling for help when somebody is hurt or sick.
• A phone is **not a toy**; it is small, easily dropped, and its screen can break.
• Ask before you use a grown-up's phone; never take a phone that is not yours.
• Do not play with a phone that is charging, and do not touch a charging cable with wet hands.
• Too much time on a phone makes the eyes tired and takes away playtime; a phone is a servant, not a master.

**Words for my notebook**

Technology is the tools and machines people make to do work easily. A phone calls, messages, photographs and teaches. It is not a toy.

### WEEK 9 — MAKING WATER SAFE TO DRINK

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Basic Science and Technology · Third Term W1 (pp 497–502) — the school places this week in the second term.*

**You will learn to**

• say why dirty water must be made clean
• name the ways of purifying water
• put the steps of cleaning water in order
• say why we drink only clean water

**Things to know**

• **Purification of water** means making dirty water clean and safe to drink.
• We purify water to **remove the dirt**, to **kill the germs**, and to **keep away the diseases** that come from bad water.
• **Boiling** means heating water until it bubbles for some minutes; this kills germs and it is the common way in the home.
• Boiled water must be **left to cool** in a clean covered cup before drinking.
• **Filtration** means passing water through a filter (cloth, sand and charcoal) so the dirt is left behind.
• **Adding chemicals** means putting in chlorine or a water purification tablet to kill germs.
• **Settling and decanting** means letting the dirt fall to the bottom, then pouring the clear water off the top.
• In the factory, water goes through all four of these before it enters the pipe — so **tap water at a treatment plant is already treated**.
• Water from a stream, a pond or a hand-dug well must be boiled or filtered before drinking.
• A covered cup keeps dust and flies out; an open bucket collects them.
• Dirty water brings sickness to the stomach; clean water keeps the body well.

**Words for my notebook**

Boiling, filtering, adding chlorine, letting it settle — these are the ways we make water safe to drink.
