# General Knowledge — Primary 2 · First Term

These are the notes for the first term of Primary 2. They follow the NERDC
Basic Education Curriculum (2025 revision) and the school's scheme of work.
The eight weeks below are the weeks that carry a written note; the remaining
weeks of the term are for revision and the end-of-term paper, so no note is
written for them.

*Depth: NERDC Primary 2 · General Knowledge · First Term W1-6, 8-9 (states and capitals by zone; how we are governed; our leaders today; national symbols and their meanings; the colonial era and independence; Nigerian heroes).*

---

### WEEK 1 — STATES AND CAPITALS I: NORTH-CENTRAL AND NORTH-WEST

**Main content**

• Nigeria has **36 states** and the **FCT** (Federal Capital Territory).

• States are grouped into **zones**.

• **North-Central** states include **Benue**, **Kogi**, **Niger** and **Plateau**.

• The capital of Benue is **Makurdi**; of Kogi, **Lokoja**.

• The capital of Niger is **Minna**; of Plateau, **Jos**.

• **North-West** states include **Kano**, **Kaduna**, **Katsina** and **Sokoto**.

• The capital of Kano is **Kano**; of Kaduna, **Kaduna**.

• The capital of Katsina is **Katsina**; of Sokoto, **Sokoto**.

• Each state has a **capital** city.

• Knowing our states helps us know our country.

**Let us talk**

1. How many states does Nigeria have?

2. Name one North-Central state.

3. Name one North-West state.

**Words for my notebook**

• state — a part of a country with its own government

• capital — the chief city of a state

• zone — a group of states in one area

**Worksheet**

1. Nigeria has ___ states and the FCT.
   A) 36 B) 10 C) 50 D) 12

2. The capital of Benue State is ___.
   A) Makurdi B) Kano C) Jos D) Lokoja

3. The capital of Plateau State is ___.
   A) Jos B) Minna C) Katsina D) Sokoto

4. Kano is a state in the ___ zone.
   A) North-West B) South-East C) South-West D) North-Central

5. Fill in: The chief city of a state is its _____.

6. Mention two states in the North-West.

7. Write the capital of Kogi State.

**My own work**

• Draw the map of Nigeria and colour one state.

### WEEK 4 — HOW WE ARE GOVERNED: LOCAL, STATE, FEDERAL

**Main content**

• Nigeria has **three** levels of government.

• The **local government** is the closest to the people.

• The local government is led by a **chairman**.

• The **state government** looks after a state.

• The state government is led by a **governor**.

• The **federal government** looks after the whole country.

• The federal government is led by the **president**.

• Each level has its own work.

• The government makes **laws** and provides **services**.

• Good government helps the people.

**Let us talk**

### WEEK 6 — NATIONAL SYMBOLS II: MEANINGS AND PROPER USE

**Main content**

• **National symbols** are signs of our country.

• The Nigerian **flag** is green, white, green.

• The **green** stands for our farms and land; the **white** stands for peace.

• The **coat of arms** shows Nigeria's strength.

• The **National Anthem** and **Pledge** honour our country.

• The **Naira** is our money.

• The **eagle** on the coat of arms stands for strength.

• We **respect** our national symbols.

• We do not spoil or misuse our symbols.

• We stand for the National Anthem.

**Let us talk**

### WEEK 8 — NIGERIAN HISTORY II: COLONIAL ERA AND INDEPENDENCE

**Main content**

• Long ago, **Britain** ruled Nigeria.

• This time is called the **colonial era**.

• The British came to **trade** and later to **rule**.

• Nigeria was a **colony** of Britain.

• Nigerians worked hard for **independence**.

• Nigeria became **independent** on **1 October 1960**.

• **Independence** means ruling ourselves.

• The first Prime Minister was **Abubakar Tafawa Balewa**.

• The first President was **Nnamdi Azikiwe**.

• We celebrate Independence Day every **1 October**.

**Let us talk**

1. Who ruled Nigeria in the colonial era?

2. When did Nigeria become independent?

3. What does independence mean?

**Words for my notebook**

• colonial era — the time when Britain ruled Nigeria

• independence — ruling ourselves

• colony — a country ruled by another
