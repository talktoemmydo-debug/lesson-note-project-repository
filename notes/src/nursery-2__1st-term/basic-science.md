### WEEK 1 — OUR BODY

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Basic Science and Technology · First Term W1 (pp 491–494).*

**Main content**

• My body is everything about me — from the top of my head to the tips of my toes.
• The five big parts of the body are the **head, neck, trunk, arms and legs**.
• The head is on top and it is round. Inside the head is the brain.
• The neck joins the head to the body. The trunk is the middle part.
• Parts of the head: **eyes, nose, ears, mouth, hair**.
• Parts of the trunk: **chest, stomach, back, waist**.
• Parts of the limbs: **shoulders, elbows, wrists, hands, fingers, hips, knees, ankles, feet, toes**.
• I have ten fingers and ten toes.
• Eyes see, ears hear, nose smells, mouth eats and talks, legs walk, hands hold.
• Nobody else has a face exactly like mine. My body is special.
• I use my body to see, hear, run, jump, eat, hold and play.

**Words for my notebook**

I have a body. My body has a head, neck, trunk, arms and legs. My body is special.

### WEEK 2 — CARE FOR OUR BODY

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Basic Science and Technology · First Term W2 (pp 491–494).*

**Main content**

• A body that is looked after stays strong, does not itch, does not smell and does not fall sick often.
• I bathe every day with soap and water.
• I brush my teeth **twice a day** — in the morning and at night.
• I wash my hands before eating and after using the toilet.
• Hair: I wash it, I comb it and I keep it neat.
• Nails: I keep them short and clean, because long nails hide dirt and scratch the skin.
• I wear clean clothes every day; the dirty ones go to the basin, not back on my body.
• I eat good food — rice, beans, yam, vegetables and fruit — and I drink clean water.
• I sleep early and rest well, so that my body can grow.
• Running and playing make my body strong.
• Once in a while a health worker checks my teeth, my eyes and how tall I have grown.

I bathe daily. I brush twice a day. I wash my hands. I keep my nails short and my clothes clean.

### WEEK 3 — FIRSTAID: FIRSTAID BOX CONTENTS

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Basic Science and Technology · First Term W3 (pp 491–494).*

**Main content**

• First aid is the **first help** we give to a person who is hurt or suddenly sick, before the nurse or the doctor comes.
• The things in the box are kept in a box so that help is ready when it is needed.
• Contents of the first aid box: **plasters (band-aids), bandages, cotton wool, antiseptic liquid or cream, scissors, thermometer, safety pins, gloves**.
• Other things that may be inside: pain relief medicine, gauze, tape and tweezers.
• A plaster covers a small cut. A bandage wraps a wound. Cotton wool cleans a wound.
• The box is kept clean, in one fixed place, and it is always filled up.
• Only grown-ups use the things in the box. We do not take the scissors, we do not open the medicine and we never drink the antiseptic.

**Words for my notebook**

### WEEK 4 — FIRSTAID: USES

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Basic Science and Technology · First Term W4 (pp 491–494).*

**Main content**

• Plasters and band-aids cover **small cuts** and scrapes.
• A bandage wraps a wound and **supports a sprained** wrist or leg.
• Cotton wool cleans a wound and carries the medicine to it.
• Antiseptic washes the wound so that **germs do not enter**; it may sting once.
• A thermometer checks whether the body is hot with fever.
• Call a grown-up at once for a deep cut, blood that will not stop, a big burn, an animal bite, or a nail or thorn gone deep into the skin.
• We never try to be the doctor for a big wound; we go and call for help.
• When somebody is hurt and we shout, everybody becomes frightened. We breathe, we speak well, and we say: *Are you okay? I am calling a teacher.*

### WEEK 5 — BODY CARE FOR WOUNDS: MEANING, CAUSES AND TYPES

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Basic Science and Technology · First Term W7 (pp 491–494).*

**Main content**

• A wound is an injury that **breaks the skin** — where the skin is open, or cut, or has blood on it.
• If the skin is not broken, it is not a wound.
• Cuts come from sharp things: a knife, broken glass, a razor blade, a tin lid.
• Falls and scrapes come from running in the compound or falling from a chair.
• Burns come from a hot pot, a kettle, a lantern or a candle.
• Insect bites (mosquito, bee, ant) and animal scratches (cat, dog) also make wounds.
• A **cut** is a straight opening made by something sharp.
• A **scrape (graze)** is the top skin rubbed off after a fall.
• A **bruise** is a blue-black mark from bumping; the skin is not open.
• A **burn** is made by heat or fire. A **puncture** is a small deep hole from a nail or a thorn.
• We do not play with knives, blades or broken glass, and we keep away from the pot on the fire.
• Some wounds are small and we clean them; some are big and only a doctor or nurse can treat them.

**Words for my notebook**

A wound breaks the skin. Types: cut, scrape, bruise, burn, puncture. Small wounds are cleaned; big wounds need a doctor.

### WEEK 6 — BODY CARE FOR WOUNDS: CARE FOR WOUNDS

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Basic Science and Technology · First Term W8 (pp 491–494).*

**Main content**

• Step one: **wash the hands first**.
• Step two: **clean the wound with clean water**.
• Step three: **put on the antiseptic** with cotton wool — we dab it, we do not rub it.
• Step four: **cover the wound with a plaster or a bandage**.
• Never touch a wound with dirty hands.
• Do not pick at the scab. The scab is the body's own plaster; when it is picked, the wound opens again and can leave a mark.
• A wet or dirty bandage is not a bandage — it is changed.
• If the wound becomes red, or swells, or pus comes out, or it pains more after two days, that is infection: tell a grown-up.
• A wound heals in some days if we keep it clean and let the scab finish its work.
• Prevention is better than treatment: it is better not to be cut at all.

**Words for my notebook**

Wash hands, clean with water, put on antiseptic, cover. Do not pick the scab. Red or swollen = tell a grown-up.

### WEEK 8 — SICKNESS: DEFINITION, TYPES AND SYMPTOMS, CAUSES AND CARE

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Basic Science and Technology · First Term W9 and W10 (pp 491–494) — this
week takes both NERDC weeks together.*

**Main content**

• Sickness (illness) is when the body is **not working properly** and we feel unwell.
• A well body eats and plays; a sick body is weak and needs rest.
• Common sicknesses of children: **cold and cough, fever, stomach ache, headache, malaria, diarrhoea, measles, chicken pox**.
• The signs of sickness are **fever or high temperature, pain, coughing, sneezing, vomiting, weakness, loss of appetite and rashes**.
• Signs are how the body tells us that something is wrong.
• A thermometer is used to check whether the body has fever.
• Sickness is brought by germs (bacteria and viruses), dirty water and dirty food, the bite of an infected mosquito, not eating well, not sleeping enough and very cold weather.
• We prevent sickness by washing the hands, eating healthy food, drinking clean water, covering the mouth when we cough, and sleeping under a mosquito net.
• A sick person needs rest and sleep, the medicine the doctor gave at the time given, plenty of water, good food and warmth.
• When somebody is sick we go to the doctor or the clinic; we do not take medicine alone.
• We are kind to a sick person — we do not laugh at them, we speak softly, we take them a cup of water.
• Sickness is not for ever: with care we get better.

**Words for my notebook**

Sickness is the body not working well. Rest, water, good food and the doctor's medicine make us well again.

### WEEK 9 — ROCKS AND SOIL

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Basic Science and Technology · Second Term W1 (pp 494–497).*

**Main content**

• A rock is a **hard, solid thing found in nature** on the earth; nobody made it.
• Some rocks were made when hot liquid from inside the earth (lava) cooled and became hard.
• Some were made when little bits of stone were pressed together for a very long time; others were changed by great heat and pressure.
• Sizes we can see: **big rocks, small stones, pebbles and sand**.
• Rocks are used for building houses, making roads, decoration and making some tools.
• Soil is the **top layer of the earth where plants grow**.
• Soil comes from rocks that broke into tiny bits over many years, mixed with dead plants and dead animals.
• **Sandy soil** is grainy and the water runs through it quickly.
• **Clay soil** is sticky when wet and smooth when dry; it can be moulded into a ball.
• **Loamy soil** is a mixture of both and is the best soil for plants.
• Plants grow in soil, animals live in soil, and the food we eat comes from soil.
• Take the soil away and there is no yam, no rice and no tree.

**Words for my notebook**

A rock is hard and comes from nature. Soil is the top layer of the earth. Loamy soil is best for plants.
