### WEEK 1 — LEADERSHIP

*Depth: NERDC Primary 1 · SCS · First Term W1 (pp 655–656).*

**Main content**

• A **leader** is somebody who guides others and shows them what to do.
• My leaders are: **father and mother** at home, **the teacher** in class, **the head teacher** in the school, and **the class captain** among the children.
• Outside: the **street leader (baale)**, the **chairman** of the local government, the **governor** of the state, and the **President** of the whole country.
• A good leader is **kind**, **fair**, **honest** and **helpful**.
• A good leader goes first in what is right, and does not do what he forbids others to do.
• We need leaders so that people will be **guided**, order will be **kept**, and work will be **finished**.
• A class without a monitor becomes noisy; a house without a head becomes untidy; a country without leaders has no direction.
• Leaders are given titles: chief, emir, oba, igwe, chairman, president, captain.
• We respect our leaders: we listen when they speak, we obey the rules they set, and we greet them.

**Words for my notebook**

A leader guides others. A good leader is kind, fair, honest and helpful. We listen to our leaders.

**Let us talk**

1. Who is a leader?
2. Name your four leaders at home and in school.
3. Mention three qualities of a good leader.
4. What happens in a class with no leader?

**Worksheet**

1. A leader is somebody who A) sleeps B) buys things C) runs away D) guides others
2. Which of these is a quality of a good leader? A) stealing B) rudeness C) fairness D) laziness
3. The leader of our class is the A) class captain B) driver C) head teacher D) trader
4. The leader of the whole country is the A) monitor B) baale C) president D) chairman
5. Fill in: A good leader is kind, fair, honest and ____.
6. Name four leaders you know.
7. Write two sentences on what a good leader does for people.

**My own work**

Write the names of the four leaders of your home and one thing each of them does.

Draw the person who leads in your class and colour the thing they hold.

### WEEK 2 — LEADERSHIP AT HOME

*Depth: NERDC Primary 1 · SCS · First Term W2 (pp 655–656).*

**Main content**

• **Father and mother** are the leaders of the home; one of them carries the final decision.
• A parent's duties are to give **food, a place to live, clothes, education, love and protection**.
• **Grandparents** are leaders too: their advice is heavy, and we do not answer them back rudely.
• An **elder brother or sister** leads the smaller children when father and mother are not at home.
• Children help by **tidying their own things, sweeping, fetching light items, carrying messages, and looking after the babies**.
• We obey our family leaders: their rule is our safety — the way we greet, the time we return, the food we eat.
• A home where children help is a home that finishes its work early and rests in peace.
• Respect at home begins with small things: answering "yes ma/sir", carrying a bag, not slamming doors.

**Words for my notebook**

Father and mother lead the home. They feed, clothe, teach and protect us. I help and I obey.

**Let us talk**

1. Who are the leaders of your home?
2. Mention two things parents do for the family.
3. What can you carry or tidy at home?
4. Why should we obey the rules of our home?

**Worksheet**

1. The leaders of the home are the A) father and mother B) traders C) neighbours D) friends
2. Which of these is a duty of parents? A) buying toys only B) playing all day C) sleeping D) feeding the children
3. When parents are away, the smaller children are led by A) the dog B) an elder sibling C) a baby D) the visitor
4. Grandparents' advice is A) forbidden B) to be respected C) useless D) not needed
5. Fill in: Children can tidy their own ____ and fetch light items.
6. Mention three duties of a parent.
7. Write two sentences on one way you help in your house.

**My own work**

Tidy your own sleeping place and your box of books today, and write what you did.

Draw your own house and colour the door of the room where the family talks.

### WEEK 3 — COMMUNITY MOBILIZATION (PART 1)

*Depth: NERDC Primary 1 · SCS · First Term W3 (pp 655–656).*

**Main content**

• A **community** is people living together in one area — a street, a village, a town.
• My community has **neighbours, shopkeepers, drivers, teachers, nurses, farmers and worshippers**.
• Community names differ: **village** is small, **town** is bigger, **city** is very big; a **estate** or **layout** is a planned community.
• Our community has a **name**; I can say it: "I live in ______."
• The leaders of the community are the **chief (oba, emir, igwe)**, the **baale** of the street, the **imam** of the mosque, the **pastor** of the church, the **mayor or chairman**.
• These leaders meet to plan for the people: a new borehole, a blocked gutter, a school, a market.
• **Mobilization** means calling the people together to do a work they must do — clean-up, protecting children, keeping the peace.
• Everybody belongs to a community, and nobody lives in a community alone.

**Words for my notebook**

A community is people living in one area. Its leaders plan for everybody; mobilizing means calling the people together.

**Let us talk**

1. What is a community?
2. What is the name of your community?
3. Name three people who live in a community.
4. Who are the leaders of our community?

**Worksheet**

2. A bigger town is called a A) city B) hut C) farm D) village
3. The head of a village or town in Yorubaland may be called the A) monitor B) oba C) pilot D) trader
4. Mobilizing the people means A) sending them away B) closing the market C) sleeping D) calling them together for a work
5. Fill in: Every community has a ____, and I can say mine.

**My own work**

### WEEK 4 — COMMUNITY MOBILIZATION (PART 2)

*Depth: NERDC Primary 1 · SCS · First Term W4 (pp 655–656).*

**Main content**

• **Cooperation** means people working together to finish a job that one person cannot finish alone.
• Community activities: **clean-up exercise** on the last Saturday, **festivals**, **town hall meetings**, **building a bridge or a classroom**, **watering the school garden**.
• When somebody's house falls, the neighbours bring sand, blocks and hands — that is community support.
• At a naming ceremony, a wedding or a funeral, the whole street comes; joy and sorrow are shared.
• A strong community gives **safety** (watchmen, street light, neighbours who know one another), **unity** and **progress**.
• Our part as children: **greet elders, do not litter, keep the gutters free, help the old cross the road, do not fight on the street**.
• Work done together finishes quickly: "Many hands make the work light."
• A community that does not work together leaves its gutters blocked and its park in weeds.

**Words for my notebook**

Cooperation = working together. Clean-up, festivals and meetings are community work. Many hands make light work.

**Let us talk**

1. What is cooperation?
2. Mention three community activities.
3. How do neighbours help in times of need?
4. Name two things you can do for your community.

**Worksheet**

1. People working together is called A) fighting B) sleeping C) cooperation D) trading
2. A common clean-up day in a community is usually A) every morning B) on a fixed day C) at midnight D) never
3. A strong community gives its people A) dirt B) fear C) safety and unity D) quarrels
4. Which one is NOT a community activity? A) clean-up B) festival C) town meeting D) solitary quarrelling
5. Fill in: Many ____ make the work light.
6. Mention three ways communities help one another.
7. Write two sentences on one community work you have seen.

**My own work**

Pick one piece of litter in your street or compound today and put it in the bin; write what you saw afterwards.

Draw the place in your community where people meet and colour the roof.

### WEEK 5 — CIVIC RESPONSIBILITIES (PARTS 1 AND 2)

*Depth: NERDC Primary 1 · SCS · First Term W7 and W8 (pp 655–656) — both weeks in one.*

**Main content**

• A **responsibility** is a thing we **must** do, not a thing we may choose to do.
• At home: cleaning my own room, washing my plate, putting away my clothes, helping with small chores.
• At school: coming on time, doing my homework, keeping the classroom clean, bringing my books, respecting the bell.
• Being responsible with my belongings: my bag, my books, my uniform and my shoes are kept in order.
• If I am not responsible, there is a consequence: no homework means a poor class work; a lost book must be replaced.
• Respecting **elders**: greet them, give them a seat, do not answer back.
• Respecting **people of my age**: no fighting, no bullying, no giving nicknames that pain.
• Respecting **younger ones**: do not push them, help them when they fall.
• Use the polite words: **"please", "thank you", "sorry", "excuse me"**.
• Take turns, share, and help those in need — a responsible child does not watch another suffer.

**Words for my notebook**

Responsibility is what I must do. I keep my things, do my work, respect elders and friends, and use polite words.

**Let us talk**

1. What is a responsibility?
2. Mention two duties of a pupil at school.
3. What is the consequence of not doing homework?
4. Say four polite words.

**Worksheet**

1. A responsibility is a thing we A) must do B) buy C) may ignore D) forget
2. Which of these is a duty at school? A) fighting B) doing homework C) coming late D) tearing books
3. Bullying other pupils is A) polite B) responsible C) wrong D) brave
4. What should we do with our books and bag? A) lose them B) keep them in order C) throw them D) hide them
5. Fill in: The four polite words are please, thank you, excuse me and ____.

### WEEK 6 — NATIONAL IDENTITY AND SYMBOLS (PART 1)

*Depth: NERDC Primary 1 · SCS · First Term W9 (pp 655–656).*

**Main content**

• **Nigeria is my country**; I am a **Nigerian** and Nigeria is my identity.
• Nigeria is in the west of the continent of **Africa**; our neighbours are **Benin Republic, Niger, Chad and Cameroon**.
• The **capital** of Nigeria is **Abuja**; the biggest city is **Lagos**.
• Our national flag has **three vertical parts — green, white, green**.
• **Green** stands for agriculture and our forests; **white** stands for **peace and unity**.
• The flag was made by **Michael Akinkunmi** in 1959, and it was first raised on the 1st of October 1960.
• The flag is saluted at school in the morning; it is flown at half a mast when a great person dies.
• We treat the flag with respect: it is not used as a towel, a napkin, a bag or a table cloth, and it is not allowed to touch the ground.
• Other documents of my identity (seen later at school): birth certificate, national identity card, passport.

**Words for my notebook**

Nigeria is my country in Africa. Our flag is green, white, green: land, peace and unity. We respect the flag.

**Let us talk**

1. What is your country?

### WEEK 8 — NATIONAL IDENTITY AND SYMBOLS (PART 2)

*Depth: NERDC Primary 1 · SCS · First Term W10 (pp 655–656).*

**Main content**

• The **national anthem** is sung standing at school and at national events. First stanza: *"Arise, O compatriots, Nigeria calls you all; To serve her throne, her country, with heart and might…"* (learned as the class is taught it).
• The **national pledge** is also said at school; we pledge to be faithful and to honour Nigeria.
• The **coat of arms** has: two **horses** on the sides, a **shield** in the middle with a **black band** in a **Y-shape**, a **red eagle** on top, and **grass and a flower** below.
• The **Y** stands for the rivers **Niger and Benue**; the **black band** is the fertile, rich soil of Nigeria.
• Our money is the **Naira (₦)**, and its small part is the **kobo**: **100 kobo make one Naira**; we use **notes and coins**.
• Our **independence** is on the **1st of October**; it is kept as a public holiday with parades and the President's broadcast.
• Other things that identify us: the **eagle**, our membership of the United Nations and the African Union, and the languages of our peoples.
• Being proud to be Nigerian means: I do not say my country is the worst; I work to make it better.

**Words for my notebook**

Anthem, pledge, coat of arms, Naira and kobo, October 1st — these are the symbols of Nigeria.

**Let us talk**

### WEEK 9 — PEOPLE, PLACES AND OBJECTS TO RESPECT (PARTS 1 AND 2)

*Depth: NERDC Primary 1 · SCS · Second Term W1 and W2 (pp 657–658) — both weeks in one.*

**Main content**

• **Elders are respected**: father and mother, grandparents, teachers, community leaders, and every grown person older than me.
• Ways of showing respect: **greeting them well**, **listening**, **obeying**, and **using their respectful title** (ma, sir, papa, chief, alhaji, pastor, reverend).
• We also respect **people of all ages and backgrounds** — the small child, the person with a disability, a person of another tribe or religion.
• Kindness is respect: we speak softly, we help, we do not mock.
• Respect matters in society because without it people quarrel, and no work goes on.
• **Sacred places**: the church, the mosque, the shrine and the traditional site. In them we are **quiet**, we **dress properly**, and we **do not litter or run about**.
• **Public places**: school, hospital, market, bus park, library — we keep them clean, queue, and do not damage them.
• **Objects to respect**: the **flag**, **religious books** (Bible, Qur'an), the **elders' belongings**, and **national monuments**.
• Respecting national monuments means not writing on the wall, not breaking, and telling others to stop.
• A child who respects is respected; a child who honours is honoured.

**Words for my notebook**

I respect elders, all people, sacred places, public places, the flag, religious books and monuments.

**Let us talk**
