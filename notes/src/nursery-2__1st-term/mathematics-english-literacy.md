### WEEK 1 — REVIEW OF ALPHABET: Aa – Ff

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy (Letter Work) · First Term W1 (pp 454–457).*

**You will learn to**

• know the letters A to F in big and small form
• match a capital letter to its small letter
• say the sound of each letter
• trace and write Aa to Ff

**Things to know**

• The alphabet has **26 letters**; I am reviewing the first six: **A a, B b, C c, D d, E e, F f**.
• A letter has two shapes: **capital** (big) and **small** (lower case). Capitals begin names and sentences.
• **A a** says /a/ — **apple**, **ant**; **B b** says /b/ — **ball**, **bat**; **C c** says /k/ — **cat**, **cup**.
• **D d** says /d/ — **dog**, **doll**; **E e** says /e/ — **egg**, **hen**; **F f** says /f/ — **fan**, **fish**.
• The sound a letter makes is not the same as its **name**: the letter is "bee", the sound is /b/.
• Matching game: capital card to small card — A with a, B with b, and so on.
• **Initial sound**: the first sound I hear in a word. "Fan" starts with /f/, so it belongs to F.
• Writing: **a** sits on the line, **b** has a tall stem up, no tail; **f** goes up and has a cross-bar.
• Alphabet song slowly: A B C D E F, then stop and clap the six sounds.

**Words for my notebook**

Aa Bb Cc Dd Ee Ff — apple, ball, cat, dog, egg, fan.

**Let us talk**

1. Sing the first six letters.
2. What sound does C make?
3. Name a word that starts with /d/.
4. Which small letter goes with E?
5. Trace b and d: which one has its tall stem up?

**Worksheet**

1. The small letter of "B" is A) p B) b C) d D) q
2. Which word begins with /f/? A) fan B) cat C) man D) van
3. The sound of the letter A in "apple" is A) /a/ B) /e/ C) /o/ D) /i/
4. Which pair matches? A) Ee B) Cc C) Dd D) Ff — find the odd one out in the pairs Aa, Bb, Cc, De
5. Fill in: The letter that begins "egg" is ____.
6. Write Aa to Ff, one line of capitals and one line of small letters.
7. Draw one thing for B and one thing for F, and write the letter beside each.

**My own work**

Find six things at home that begin with A, B, C, D, E, F and write their letters.

### WEEK 2 — REVIEW OF ALPHABET: Gg – Ll

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy (Letter Work) · First Term W2 (pp 454–457).*

**You will learn to**

• know the letters G to L
• say the sound of each of them
• match capitals to small letters
• write Gg to Ll on the line

**Things to know**

• **G g, H h, I i, J j, K k, L l** — the next six letters.
• Sounds: **G** /g/ — goat, gun; **H** /h/ — hat, hen; **I** /i/ — ink, insect; **J** /dʒ/ — jug, jam; **K** /k/ — kite, key; **L** /l/ — lion, lamp.
• **G** and **K** make the same sound in most small words (goat, kite) — that is not a mistake, English is like that.
• The letter **I** is also the word for me: **"I"** is always a capital, even in the middle of a sentence.
• Matching: capital to small, letter to picture, letter to the first sound in my name.
• Writing: **g** goes down below the line; **h** is tall; **i** has a dot; **j** has a dot and a tail; **k** is tall with two arms; **l** is a tall straight line.
• Sound hunt: point to things in the room that begin with /h/, /l/, /g/.
• My letters so far: **Aa Bb Cc Dd Ee Ff Gg Hh Ii Jj Kk Ll** — twelve.

**Words for my notebook**

Gg Hh Ii Jj Kk Ll — goat, hat, ink, jug, kite, lion.

**Let us talk**

1. What sound does H make?
2. Which letter always looks like a capital, even in the middle of a sentence?
3. Name a word that starts with /k/.
4. Say your letters from A to L.

**Worksheet**

1. The letter that begins "lion" is A) i B) n C) r D) l
2. Which pair does NOT match? A) Jj B) Kk, kite — choose: G–goat, H–hat, J–kite C) Gg D) Hh
3. /g/ is the sound of A) l B) g C) j D) k
4. Which letters both say /k/? A) G and L B) H and J C) C and K D) I and K
5. Fill in: The small letter of "H" is ____.
6. Circle the words that start with I: ink — insect — igloo — apple.
7. Write Gg to Ll, capitals and small letters.

**My own work**

Write the first letter of your name and your friend's name in capital and small form.

### WEEK 3 — REVIEW OF ALPHABET: Mm – Rr

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy (Letter Work) · First Term W3 (pp 454–457).*

**You will learn to**

• know the letters M to R
• say their sounds
• hear the first sound in a spoken word
• write Mm to Rr neatly

**Things to know**

• **M m, N n, O o, P p, Q q, R r**.
• Sounds: **M** /m/ — mat, man, milk; **N** /n/ — net, nut; **O** /ɒ/ — orange, ox; **P** /p/ — pen, pan; **Q** /kw/ — queen; **R** /r/ — rat, run.
• **Q** is nearly always followed by **u**: queen, quick, quiet — that is why its sound is /kw/.
• **M** and **N** are nasal sounds: I feel them in my nose, and my mouth closes for /m/ but not for /n/.
• Initial sound practice: hear "pan" → /p/; "net" → /n/; "run" → /r/.
• Writing: **m** has three strokes, **n** two, **p** goes down below the line, **q** below too; **o** is a full circle.
• Sorting game: which letters sit on the line and which have tails below it (g, j, p, q).
• My alphabet so far: eighteen letters, from **Aa** to **Rr**.

**Words for my notebook**

Mm Nn Oo Pp Qq Rr — mat, net, orange, pen, queen, rat.

**Let us talk**

1. What sound does N make?
2. Which letter always wants a "u" after it?
3. Say the first sound in "orange".
4. Which of your letters has a tail below the line?

**Worksheet**

1. "Queen" begins with the sound A) /k/ B) /n/ C) /q/ D) /kw/
2. The letter that begins "net" is A) r B) n C) m D) o
3. Which letter goes below the line when we write it? A) p B) m C) n D) o
4. The sound /m/ is heard first in A) net B) rat C) mat D) pen
5. Fill in: Q is nearly always followed by the letter ____.
6. Write Mm to Rr, capitals and small letters.
7. Say three words that start with /p/.

**My own work**

Look round the class and write down the first letter of six things you can see.

Write the letters M to R in large shapes on one page and colour each letter a different colour.

### WEEK 4 — REVIEW OF ALPHABET: Ss – Zz

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy (Letter Work) · First Term W4 (pp 454–457).*

**You will learn to**

• know the last letters S to Z
• say the sound of each
• write the whole alphabet in order
• finish the alphabet review

**Things to know**

• **S s, T t, U u, V v, W w, X x, Y y, Z z** — the last eight letters.
• Sounds: **S** /s/ — sun, sock; **T** /t/ — tap, tin; **U** /ʌ/ — umbrella, up; **V** /v/ — van, vat. **W** /w/ — water, wet; **X** /ks/ — box, fox; **Y** /j/ — yam, yes; **Z** /z/ — zebra, zone.
• **X** usually comes at the **end** of small English words: fox, box, six. Its sound is /ks/.
• **V** and **W** are not the same: for /v/ my teeth touch my lip; for /w/ my lips make a round hole.
• "X-mas" is not a word to start with x — say the sound at the end of "fox".
• Now the whole alphabet: **Aa Bb Cc Dd Ee Ff Gg Hh Ii Jj Kk Ll Mm Nn Oo Pp Qq Rr Ss Tt Uu Vv Ww Xx Yy Zz** — 26 letters.
• Singing it all: the alphabet song from A to Z with the sounds after each letter.

**Words for my notebook**

Ss Tt Uu Vv Ww Xx Yy Zz — sun, tap, umbrella, van, water, box, yam, zebra. The alphabet has 26 letters.

**Let us talk**

1. Say the alphabet from A to Z without looking.
2. What sound does Z make?
3. Where does the letter X usually appear in a small word?
4. Show me the difference between /v/ and /w/.

**Worksheet**

1. The number of letters in the English alphabet is A) 24 B) 27 C) 26 D) 25
2. "Zebra" begins with the sound A) /s/ B) /dʒ/ C) /v/ D) /z/
3. Which letter is NOT in the last eight? A) Q B) V C) W D) Y
4. The sound of X in "fox" is A) /s/ B) /ex/ C) /ks/ D) /z/
5. Fill in: The letter between T and V is ____.
6. Write the whole alphabet in small letters in your book.
7. Circle the letters that begin your name, your mother's name and your father's name.

**My own work**

Write your full name and underline each letter you have learnt this term.

Write the letters S to Z in large shapes and colour only the ones made with straight lines.

### WEEK 5 — THREE-LETTER WORDS: CVC PATTERN (Part 1)

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy (Letter Work) · First Term W7 (pp 454–457).*

**You will learn to**

• say what a CVC word is
• blend three sounds into one word
• read the -at and -an word families
• write three-letter words

**Things to know**

• A **three-letter word** has three sounds: a **consonant**, a **vowel**, a **consonant** — we call it **CVC**.
• To **blend** means to run the sounds together: **c – a – t** … *cat*. Say it slowly, then fast.
• The middle letter of these words is the short vowel **a**, so the family rhymes.
• The **-at** family: **cat, bat, rat, mat, hat, sat, fat**.
• The **-an** family: **can, man, pan, ran, fan, van, tan**.
• Changing the first sound makes a new word: *cat → hat → bat → mat* — only the first sound moves.
• Reading a CVC sentence: *"The cat sat on the mat."* — six words, all easy once I blend.
• Writing: say each sound, write its letter, then read my word back from left to right.
• Never write the sounds in the wrong order — read what you wrote to be sure.

**Words for my notebook**

c-a-t = cat. The -at family: cat, bat, rat, mat, hat, sat. The -an family: can, man, pan, ran, fan, van.

**Let us talk**

1. Blend this for me: m – a – t.
2. What is the middle sound in "pan"?
3. Change one sound in "cat" to make a new word.
4. Read: "The man ran."

**Worksheet**

1. A CVC word has the pattern A) three consonants B) three vowels C) vowel-consonant-vowel D) consonant-vowel-consonant
2. The word made by b-a-t is A) bat B) but C) tab D) bet
3. Which word belongs to the -an family? A) pen B) pin C) mat D) pan
4. In "cat", the vowel is A) none B) t C) a D) c
5. Fill in: m – a – ____ = man.
6. Write four words from the -at family.
7. Read this sentence twice: "The rat sat on the mat."

**My own work**

Write five CVC words and draw a small picture for two of them.

### WEEK 6 — THREE-LETTER WORDS: CVC PATTERN (Part 2)

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy (Letter Work) · First Term W8 (pp 454–457).*

**You will learn to**

• read the -en, -in and -og families
• blend words with the short e and short i sounds
• hear the difference between e and i
• take dictation of three-letter words

**Things to know**

• The **-en** family: **pen, hen, ten, men, den, wren**.
• The **-in** family: **pin, tin, bin, win, fin, sin**.
• The **-og** family: **dog, log, fog, hog, jog**.
• Short **e** — my mouth is open a little and flat: *pen, ten, hen*. Short **i** — my mouth is smaller and higher: *pin, tin, hit*.
• The pair that tricks people: **pen / pin**, **ten / tin**, **beg / big**. Listen for the vowel.
• Blending practice: **p – e – n** = pen; **d – o – g** = dog.
• Dictation: hear the word, say its sounds one by one with a finger for each, then write the letters.
• Word-picture matching: the picture of ten fingers goes with "ten", not "tin".
• Make a new word from an old one by changing the middle sound: *pen → pin → pan*.

**Words for my notebook**

pen, hen, ten · pin, tin, win · dog, log, fog. Short e and short i are different sounds.

**Let us talk**

1. Blend: h – e – n.
2. Say "ten" and "tin" — how are they different?
3. What word is d-o-g?
4. Change the middle sound in "pin" to make "pan".

**Worksheet**

1. The word made by t-e-n is A) ten B) tin C) ton D) tan
2. Which word has the short i sound? A) hen B) pen C) ten D) bin
3. "dog" belongs to the family A) -in B) -en C) -at D) -og
4. The first sound in "hen" is A) /hen/ B) /h/ C) /n/ D) /e/
5. Fill in: p – i – n spells ____.
6. Write two words from -en and two from -in.
7. Take dictation of four words and read your answers back.

**My own work**

Write the -en, -in and -og words on three cards each and shuffle them into their families at home.

Draw a cat, a pen and a pot; write the word under each picture and colour the pot.

### WEEK 8 — THREE-LETTER WORDS: CVC PATTERN (Part 3)

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy (Letter Work) · First Term W9 (pp 454–457).*

**You will learn to**

• read the -un, -op and -ug families
• build words from letter cards
• read all six word families together
• write CVC words without help

**Things to know**

• The **-un** family: **sun, bun, run, fun, gun, spun**.
• The **-op** family: **top, hop, mop, pop, cop, drop**.
• The **-ug** family: **bug, hug, mug, rug, jug, tug**.
• Word building with cards: give me s, u, n — I put them in order and read "sun".
• Sentence practice: *"The big bug sat on the rug."* — five words, and I can read all of them.
• The families so far: **-at, -an, -en, -in, -og, -un, -op, -ug** — that is the whole of my CVC collection.
• Reading games: sound a word, write it, and match it to a picture; read the fastest list in the class.
• Independent writing: no looking, say the sounds slowly, and write one letter per sound.
• Words that look similar: *bun / fun / run*, *top / hop / mop* — the first sound changes the word.

**Words for my notebook**

sun, bun, run · top, hop, mop · bug, hug, mug. I can read eight word families now.

**Let us talk**

1. Read: "The pup ran home."
2. What is the family of "mop"?
3. Change one sound in "sun" to make "fun".
4. Name the middle sound in "rug".

**Worksheet**

1. The word s-u-n is A) sun B) son C) sin D) san
2. "mug" belongs to the family A) -en B) -op C) -un D) -ug
3. Which word is made from the sounds b-u-g? A) bog B) bag C) bug D) big
4. The first sound in "top" is A) /p/ B) /t/ C) /to/ D) /o/
5. Fill in: The word that rhymes with "hop" and means the highest place is ____.
6. Build and write four words from the -un family.
7. Read this sentence: "The fun bug ran on the rug."

**My own work**

Write one sentence that uses three words from the families you have learnt.

### WEEK 9 — STORY TELLING AND LETTER WORK INTEGRATION

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy (Letter Work) · First Term W10 (pp 454–457).*

**You will learn to**

• listen to a simple story and answer questions on it
• pick out letters and sounds from story words
• retell a story in my own words
• find CVC words inside a story

**Things to know**

• Listening rules: eyes on the teller, mouth quiet, hands still, and think about what is happening.
• Our story for the week: **"The Rat and the Sun"** — a rat, a mat, a pot and a hot sun.
• Comprehension questions are asked in this order: **who**, **what**, **where**, **when**, **why**.
• The words in the story that I can already read: *rat, sat, mat, sun, bun, pin* — all CVC words.
• Sound hunt: in the story, how many words begin with /s/? with /r/?
• Retelling: say it in three parts — the **beginning**, the **middle**, the **end**.
• The **moral** is the lesson the story teaches: for our story, small and clever can beat big and hot.
• Drawing story elements: I draw the rat, the mat, the sun, and label each with its first letter.
• Vocabulary from the story: **scorching**, **clever**, **shade** — new words go in my notebook.
• Telling my own story: every child says one sentence in turn, and we build a story together.

**Words for my notebook**

Who? What? Where? When? Why? — the five questions for any story.

**Let us talk**

1. Who was in our story?
2. What did the rat sit on?
3. Where was the shade?
4. Tell the beginning of the story in one sentence.
5. Draw one part of the story and write the letter it begins with.

**Worksheet**

1. The question that asks about a person is A) what B) where C) why D) who
2. Which of these is a CVC word from our story? A) rat B) shade C) scorching D) clever
3. The lesson a story teaches is called the A) ending B) rhyme C) title D) moral
4. The first part of a story is the A) beginning B) cover C) middle D) end
5. Fill in: In the story, the rat sat on the ____.
6. Write the letters of the three characters you liked in our story.
7. Retell our story in three sentences: beginning, middle, end.

**My own work**

Tell a story of your own to your classmate and ask them two questions about it.
