### WEEK 1 — EXERCISES AND GAMES: IMPORTANCE

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · PHE · First Term W1 (pp 547–551).*

**You will learn to**

• say what exercise means and what a game is
• name the things exercise does for my body
• tell why games have rules
• show that exercise is fun, not a punishment

**Things to know**

• **Exercise** means the physical activities that make the body move and work.
• A **game** is an organized activity with rules, usually played for fun and sometimes to win.
• Exercise and games keep the body strong and healthy and help me grow properly.
• They develop my muscles and bones, and they make my body bend and balance better.
• They give me energy, and they help my heart and my lungs work well.
• Playing keeps me from becoming too heavy (obesity).
• A child who plays sleeps better at night and wakes up fresh.
• Games teach discipline and focus: I wait for my turn, I listen for the whistle, I remember the rule.
• Team games teach me to play with others, and my eyes and hands learn to work together.
• Every game has rules, and the rules make the game fair for everybody.

**Words for my notebook**

Exercise makes the body move. Games have rules. Both keep me strong, quick and happy.

**Let us talk**

1. What is exercise?
2. Mention three things exercise does for the body.
3. Why does a game need rules?
4. Say one game you love to play.

### WEEK 2 — GAMES: BASIC GAMES

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · PHE · First Term W2 (pp 547–551).*

**You will learn to**

• play the basic games of the field
• keep the balance in the egg-and-spoon race
• follow the rules of each game
• play fairly and encourage the others

**Things to know**

• **Egg and spoon race:** a small ball or bean bag is balanced on a spoon and carried to the line; if it falls, you pick it up and go on from where it fell.
• The egg-and-spoon game teaches balance and control of the hand.
• **Sack race:** you stand inside a sack and jump to the finish line; it trains the legs and the sense of balance.
• **Blindfold and fill a basket:** a player is blindfolded and, guided by the voices of teammates, picks objects up and puts them in a basket.
• The blindfold game teaches trust, listening and knowing where things are around you.
• **Three-legged race:** the inside legs of two players are tied together, and the pair must walk as one.
• Other basic games: **musical chairs, freeze dance and the obstacle course** (run, crawl under, step over, jump across).
• Fair play means: no pushing, no cheating, no taking another's place.
• We take turns, and when a friend loses we say "well played" instead of laughing.

**Words for my notebook**

Balance on the spoon, jump in the sack, listen with the eyes shut. Play fairly and clap for the others.

**Let us talk**

1. Which game uses a spoon and a ball?
2. What does the sack race train?
3. Who guides the blindfolded player?
4. Say two things fair players do.

### WEEK 3 — BODY EXERCISE: STRETCHING, BENDING, RAISING ARMS AND LEGS, SKIPPING, LEAPING AND HOPPING

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · PHE · First Term W3 and W4 (pp 547–551) — the school takes both weeks together.*

**You will learn to**

• stretch the neck, arms, back and legs
• bend and raise the arms and legs in the right form
• skip with and without a rope
• leap and hop without falling

**Things to know**

• **Stretching**: reach for the sky, touch your toes, bend sideways, roll the neck slowly, turn the arms in circles, turn the ankles.
• Stretching warms the muscles up, keeps us from being hurt, and makes the body bend easily.
• **Bending**: bend forward, bend backward carefully, bend sideways, and bend the knees (squat) like a frog.
• **Raising arms and legs**: arm raise to the front, to the side, up over the head; leg raise marching, to the side, and behind.
• **Skipping**: jump with a rope forwards, jump backwards, or skip without a rope and count the skips — 1, 2, 3… to 20.
• Skipping makes the legs strong, the heart quick, and the whole body well-coordinated.
• **Leaping** is a big jump from one foot to the other foot; arms swing to help; we leap over a line or a cone.
• **Hopping** is on one foot only — five hops on the right leg, five on the left.
• Form and safety come first: land with soft knees, keep space between you and your neighbour, and never push.

**Words for my notebook**

Stretch, bend, raise, skip, leap and hop. Warm up first, land with bent knees, and keep your space.

**Let us talk**

1. Why do we stretch before exercise?
2. Show me a squat and a side bend.
3. What is the difference between leaping and hopping?
4. Count twenty skips out loud.

### WEEK 4 — BODY CONDITIONING AND BALANCING

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · PHE · First Term W7 (pp 547–551).*

**You will learn to**

• say what body conditioning means
• say what balance is
• stand on one leg for ten counts
• play the dance-and-freeze game well

**Things to know**

• **Body conditioning** means exercises that make the whole body fit and in good control.
• **Balance** means staying steady without falling.
• Stand on the right leg and count to ten; then the left leg; then, when you are strong, try with your eyes shut.
• Arms are stretched out sideways while balancing — they act like the beam of a scale.
• Balancing strengthens the small muscles that keep us upright, and it teaches the mind to focus.
• **Dancing and freezing game**: dance while the music (or a drum) plays; when it stops, freeze like a statue and hold that position.
• Holding the freeze tests balance, teaches rhythm and trains the ears to listen.
• Other balancing activities: walk heel to toe on a line drawn on the floor, stand on tiptoe, walk along a low, safe beam.
• Balance falls when the eyes are shut, when the head moves about, or when somebody pushes — so we try again quietly.

**Words for my notebook**

Conditioning keeps the body fit; balance keeps us steady. Arms out, eyes fixed, count to ten.

**Let us talk**

1. What is balance?
2. What do the arms do when we stand on one leg?
3. When do we freeze in the dancing game?
4. Name one other balancing activity.

### WEEK 5 — SIMPLE MOVEMENTS OF PARTS OF THE BODY

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · PHE · First Term W8 (pp 547–551).*

**You will learn to**

• move to the beat of a tambourine
• walk, jog, run and crawl correctly
• bend, turn, jump and swing the body parts
• move safely in a space shared with others

**Things to know**

• **Tambourine movement**: move when it plays; move **fast** for fast beats, **slow** for slow beats; **freeze** when it stops.
• Different body parts answer the beat: the hands clap, the feet stamp, the head nods, the trunk twists.
• **Walking** has many forms: normal walk, fast walk, slow walk, on tiptoe, on the heels, and walking backwards.
• **Jogging** is light running — in one place or moving along.
• **Running** is faster: knees lift, arms pump at the elbow, eyes ahead.
• **Bending** is at the waist or at the knees, forwards, backwards and sideways.
• **Turning, spinning, jumping and crawling** are also basic movements; crawling is done on hands and knees under a line.
• Fundamental movement patterns are the foundation of every sport: without them a child cannot kick, throw or catch well.
• Moving safely in a shared space: keep your own spot, do not run into a neighbour, stop when the whistle blows.

**Words for my notebook**

Fast, slow, freeze — that is the tambourine. Walk, jog, run, bend, turn, jump, crawl. Keep your space.

**Let us talk**

1. What do you do when the tambourine stops?
2. Show me walking on the heels and on tiptoe.
3. What makes running faster than walking?
4. Name three movements you do with the trunk.

### WEEK 6 — MANIPULATIVE MOVEMENTS: THROWING AND CATCHING

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · PHE · First Term W9 (pp 547–551).*

**You will learn to**

• say what manipulative movement means
• throw underhand and overhand
• catch with two hands at different heights
• keep the safety rules when throwing

**Things to know**

• **Manipulative movements** are movements that control and move an object using a body part.
• They are the skills every sport is built on: throwing, catching, kicking, hitting, bouncing, rolling.
• **Underhand throw**: the arm swings from behind and low, the object leaves the hand in front of the body.
• **Overhand throw**: the arm goes up behind the head, the body turns sideways, and we **step and throw**.
• We can throw at a **target** (a hoop on the wall), for **distance** (as far as we can), or into a partner's hands.
• **Catching** is done with **two hands**, arms out in the ready position, eyes on the object all the way to the hands.
• Catch a bean bag first, then a soft ball; catch high throws and low throws separately.
• With a partner we throw and catch, and slowly move a step further apart after each ten good catches.
• Safety: only soft objects are used for throwing in class; we never throw at a person's face, and we look before we throw.

**Words for my notebook**

Throw underhand or overhand; step and throw. Catch with two hands, eyes on the ball. Soft things only.

**Let us talk**

1. What is a manipulative movement?
2. Show me the ready position for catching.
3. What do we do with our feet when we throw far?
4. Name one throwing safety rule.

### WEEK 8 — NEURO-MUSCULAR SKILLS: DANCING AND SOMERSAULTING

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · PHE · First Term W10 (dancing) and Nursery 2 · PHE · Second Term W10 (somersaulting), pp 547–551.*

**You will learn to**

• say what a neuro-muscular skill is
• do simple dance steps with the arms and legs together
• roll forward on a mat with the chin tucked
• say why the roll is done only with supervision

**Things to know**

• **Neuro-muscular** means the working together of the nerves (the brain and its wires) and the muscles to produce movement.
• A skill of this kind needs timing: the brain says go, the muscles obey, and the body obeys at the right moment.
• **Dancing** is moving the body to music or rhythm: step left, step right, clap, jump, turn.
• Simple dance steps are done in a routine — the same order again and again so the body remembers it.
• Cultural dances (simple ones) teach us where our people come from, and the whole class joins in.
• Dancing trains rhythm, timing and the coordination of the arms and the legs, and it is fun exercise.
• **Somersaulting (forward roll)**: from a squat, hands on the mat, **tuck the chin to the chest**, push with the legs and roll on the rounded back.
• The roll is done **only on a mat or soft ground, with a grown-up watching** — never on a hard floor, never on a bed, never on a friend.
• A child who is not ready does not roll: that is alright. Confidence is built one small step at a time.

**Words for my notebook**

Neuro-muscular = brain and muscles working together. Dance to the beat. Roll only on a mat, chin tucked, with a grown-up.

**Let us talk**

1. What does neuro-muscular mean?
2. Show me two dance steps in order.
3. Where should the chin be during a forward roll?
4. Why must an adult watch the roll?

### WEEK 9 — DEVELOPMENT THROUGH SPORT ACTIVITIES: PHYSICAL DEVELOPMENT

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · PHE · Second Term W1 (pp 552–557).*

**You will learn to**

• name the parts of the body that sport develops
• say what flexibility, balance and speed mean
• tell which activity builds which quality
• say what a child of my age can do

**Things to know**

• Sports and physical activity help the **whole body develop**, not only the legs.
• **Flexibility** is the ability to bend and stretch easily; it is improved by stretching exercises.
• **Agility** is the ability to move quickly and change direction easily; games and drills build it.
• **Balance** is staying steady without falling; balancing exercises (one leg, the line, the beam) improve it.
• **Coordination** is using different parts of the body together smoothly — eyes and hands, arms and legs.
• **Reaction** is answering a signal quickly: ear hears the clap, leg starts to run.
• **Speed** is how fast we move; running activities develop it.
• **Strength** is how strong the muscles are; exercise and play build it.
• Regular sport develops all of these together; one game can train three or four of them.
• At my age I am learning these things, not competing as a grown-up athlete; my bones and muscles are still growing, so rest is part of the plan.

**Words for my notebook**

Sport builds flexibility, agility, balance, coordination, reaction, speed and strength — at my own age, with rest.

**Let us talk**

1. What is flexibility?
2. Which quality tells you how fast you move?
3. Name one activity that builds balance.
