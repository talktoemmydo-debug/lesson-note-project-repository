### WEEK 1 — MEANING OF AGRICULTURE; IMPORTANCE OF AGRICULTURE TO HUMANS

*Depth: NERDC Primary 4 · Prevocational Studies · First Term W1–W2 (p 833).*

**You will learn to**

• say what agriculture means in one line
• name the things agriculture gives us
• tell who a farmer is
• say why every person needs agriculture

**Things to know**

• **Agriculture** is the work of growing crops and rearing animals for man to use.
• A person who does this work is a **farmer**.
• Agriculture gives us the food we eat: yam, rice, beans, maize, plantain, vegetables and fruit.
• It gives us animals for food: eggs and meat from the hen, milk from the cow, meat from the goat and the sheep.
• It gives us cotton for cloth, wood for furniture, and leaves and roots some people use as medicine.
• Without agriculture there would be no food on the table and no raw material for the factory.
• Farmers sell their surplus at the market and use the money for school fees, clothes and houses.
• Many people in Nigeria earn their living from agriculture.
• Agriculture is important to every one of us, whether we live in the town or in the village.

**Words for my notebook**

Agriculture is growing crops and rearing animals. It gives us food, clothes, work and money.

**Let us talk**

1. What is agriculture?
2. Who is a farmer?
3. Mention three things agriculture gives us.
4. Why does a town child also need agriculture?

### WEEK 2 — AGRICULTURE IN OUR DAILY LIVES

*Depth: NERDC Primary 4 · Prevocational Studies · First Term W1–W2 (p 833).*

**You will learn to**

• trace a meal from the farm to the table
• name farm foods taken at breakfast, lunch and dinner
• say which farm things are used at home besides food
• tell why we must not waste farm produce

**Things to know**

• The swallow we eat — rice, yam flour, beans, garri — all came from a farm.
• The oil in our soup came from the palm fruit; the groundnut in our snack came from the ground.
• Fresh fish and catfish come from the river and the fish pond; milk and eggs come from animals kept at home.
• Fruit that I eat between meals — mango, orange, watermelon, banana — grew on a tree or a creeper.
• Farm things are not only for eating: broom from the midrib, mats from raffia, wood for the chair, and the cow's skin for shoes.
• A market day is where farm produce comes together: the seller's basket holds what the farmer's hoe made.
• Food that is wasted was grown with rain, sweat and time; we finish what we take and we keep the rest for later.
• If there is no farm work, the shop has nothing to sell and the table has nothing to carry.

**Words for my notebook**

From the farm to my plate: rice, oil, fish, milk, fruit. What the farm gives, we do not throw away.

**Let us talk**

1. Name three foods you ate today that came from a farm.
2. Where does palm oil come from?
3. Mention one farm thing used at home that is not food.
4. Why must we not waste food?

### WEEK 3 — BASIC FARMING ACTIVITIES

*Depth: NERDC Primary 4 · Prevocational Studies · First Term W1–W2 (p 833).*

**You will learn to**

• put the farming activities in order
• name the tools used at each stage
• say how crops are cared for
• tell what happens at harvest time

**Things to know**

• Farming has an order, and every stage waits for the one before it.
• **Clearing**: the bush and old sticks are removed from the land with a cutlass.
• **Tilling**: the soil is turned and broken with a hoe so that it becomes soft for planting.
• **Planting**: seeds, yam setts, cassava sticks or cocoyam are put into the ground at the right time of the year.
• **Weeding and watering**: grass that competes with the crop is removed; where there is no rain, the crop is watered.
• **Applying manure**: rotten leaves and animal droppings are added to make the soil rich.
• **Protecting the crop**: birds, grasshoppers, goats and cattle are kept away from the growing farm.
• **Harvesting**: yam, maize, cassava and rice are gathered when they are ripe.
• **Storage and selling**: the harvest is dried, kept safe from weevils, eaten, or taken to the market.
• Simple tools of the small farm: cutlass, hoe, rake, watering can, knapsack sprayer, basket.

**Words for my notebook**

Clear, till, plant, weed, water, harvest, store, sell — the farmer's work goes in that order.

**Let us talk**

1. What is done before planting?
2. Which tool turns the soil?
3. Why do we weed the farm?
4. Name two things done after harvest.

### WEEK 4 — SCOPE OF AGRICULTURE: CROPS

*Depth: NERDC Primary 4 · Prevocational Studies · First Term W1–W2 (p 833).*

**You will learn to**

• say what crop production means
• group crops as food, cash and fruit crops
• name crops grown in our state
• say which part of a crop we eat

**Things to know**

• **Crop production** (crop farming) is the branch of agriculture that deals with growing plants for man and animals.
• **Food crops** are grown to feed the family: rice, yam, maize, cassava, beans, plantain, cocoyam.
• **Cash crops** are grown to be sold and to bring money: cocoa, groundnut, cotton, palm produce, rubber, ginger.
• **Fruit and vegetable crops**: mango, orange, watermelon, banana; tomatoes, pepper, spinach, ewédú, ugu.
• **Cereals** are grain crops — rice, maize, guinea corn, wheat; they are the staff of life.
• Some crops give us oil (palm, groundnut, sunflower) and some give us drink (cocoa, coffee, sugarcane).
• We eat different parts: the **root or tuber** (yam, cassava), the **grain** (maize, rice), the **leaf** (spinach), the **fruit** (mango).
• **Leguminous crops** such as beans and groundnut make the soil rich as they grow.
• Trees like iroko and mahogany are forestry; crops in the field are crop production — they are not the same branch.

**Words for my notebook**

Crops divide into food crops, cash crops, fruit and vegetable crops. Cereals are grains; beans make soil rich.

**Let us talk**

1. What is crop production?
2. Name three food crops and two cash crops.
3. Which part of the cassava plant do we eat?
4. Give one example of a cereal crop.

### WEEK 5 — SCOPE OF AGRICULTURE: ANIMALS

*Depth: NERDC Primary 4 · Prevocational Studies · First Term W1–W2 (p 833).*

**You will learn to**

• say what animal rearing means
• name farm animals and their young ones
• state what each animal gives us
• say how farm animals are cared for

**Things to know**

• **Animal rearing** (animal husbandry) is the branch of agriculture that deals with keeping farm animals.
• Farm animals: **cow, goat, sheep, pig, rabbit, dog, cat**, and the birds we keep — **hen, duck, turkey, guinea fowl**.
• The young of the cow is the **calf**; of the goat, the **kid**; of the sheep, the **lamb**; of the hen, the **chick**.
• The cow gives milk, meat and hide; the goat gives meat and skin; the hen gives eggs and meat.
• Animals also give **manure** for the farm and **strength** for ploughing and carrying loads.
• An animal needs four things every day: **food, clean water, shade, and a clean, safe place** to sleep.
• The animal is dosed and vaccinated when the vet or the health worker says so; sick animals are kept apart.
• Animals are not beaten; they are driven gently, and water is given to them in the hot season.
• Fish are kept in ponds and in the river — that part of agriculture is called fishery.

**Words for my notebook**

Animal rearing gives milk, eggs, meat and manure. Every animal needs food, water, shade and a safe place.

**Let us talk**

1. What is animal rearing?
2. What do we call the young one of a hen?
3. Mention two things the cow gives us.
4. Say three things an animal needs daily.

### WEEK 6 — SCOPE OF AGRICULTURE: FORESTRY

*Depth: NERDC Primary 4 · Prevocational Studies · First Term W1–W2 (p 833).*

**You will learn to**

• say what forestry means
• name trees that give timber and trees that give fruit
• state the uses of trees
• say why we plant trees and do not cut them all

**Things to know**

• **Forestry** is the branch of agriculture that deals with the growing, management and protection of trees and the forest.
• **Timber trees** give us wood for planks, furniture and building: iroko, mahogany, teak, pine, obeche.
• **Fruit trees** give us food: mango, orange, guava, coconut, oil palm, cashew.
• Trees also give us medicine (neem leaves, bitter leaf roots), firewood, charcoal, and shelter from the sun.
• The forest is home to wild animals and birds: monkey, antelope, parrot, snail.
• Trees hold the soil with their roots, so the ground does not wash away when rain falls.
• Trees clean the air we breathe and bring the rain; where the trees are all cut, the land becomes dry.
• We cut only the trees that are marked, we cut a tree and **plant two**, and we do not burn bushes in harmattan.
• In Nigeria there are forest reserves, and there is a ministry that looks after them.

**Words for my notebook**

Forestry deals with trees: timber, fruit, medicine, shade. Cut one, plant two.

**Let us talk**

1. What is forestry?
2. Name two timber trees and two fruit trees.
3. What do the roots of trees do for the soil?
4. Why should we not burn bushes in the dry season?

### WEEK 8 — SCOPE OF AGRICULTURE: FISHERY

*Depth: NERDC Primary 4 · Prevocational Studies · First Term W1–W2 (p 833).*

**You will learn to**

• say what fishery means
• name fish we eat and where they come from
• state the tools used in fishing
• say how fish are kept and prepared for market

**Things to know**

• **Fishery** is the branch of agriculture that deals with catching or rearing fish and other water animals.
• **Catch fishing** takes place in the river, the lake and the sea; **fish farming** (aquaculture) keeps fish in a pond or a cage.
• Fish we eat in Nigeria: **catfish, tilapia, titus/mackerel, croaker, bonga, sardine**; also prawn, crayfish and periwinkle.
• The tools of the fisherman: **net, hook and line, fishing rod, basket, trap, canoe and boat**.
• Fish are kept fresh with **salt and smoke or ice**; dried fish and smoked fish travel far to the market.
• Fish give us body-building food (protein); a child who eats fish grows strong.
• Fishing is done with rules: small fish are thrown back, so that there will be fish next year.
• Fish must not be taken from dirty water, and water for the pond must be kept clean.
• The river, the lake and the sea give work to fishermen, fish sellers and the women who smoke the fish.

**Words for my notebook**

Fishery is catching or rearing fish: net, hook, canoe, pond. Small fish thrown back keeps the water full.

**Let us talk**

1. What is fishery?
2. Name three fish we eat.
3. What tool does a fisherman use?
4. Why is a small fish thrown back into the water?

### WEEK 9 — AGRICULTURE IN NIGERIA

*Depth: NERDC Primary 4 · Prevocational Studies · First Term W1–W2 (p 833) and Primary 6 ·
Prevocational Studies · Third Term W9 (p 952) for the sustainable-use lines.*

**You will learn to**

• name the crops Nigeria is known for
• say where farming is done in the country
• explain sustainable and organic farming in a child's words
• say what a pupil can do to protect the environment

**Things to know**

• Nigeria farms in every part of the country: **groundnut and cotton** in the north, **rice and maize** on the middle belt, **palm produce, cocoa and cassava** in the south, **fish** in the rivers of the south-south.
• Crops that bring money into Nigeria include **cocoa, groundnut, palm oil, rubber and cashew**.
• Most food in Nigeria comes from small family farms, worked with the hoe and the cutlass.
• **Sustainable agriculture** means farming in a way that the land will still be good for our children — not taking everything and leaving nothing.
• **Organic farming** uses compost (rotten leaves and dung) instead of only chemical fertiliser.
• **Reducing waste at home**: finish the food on the plate; use the peels for the compost; do not pour oil down the gutter.
• **Conserving resources**: water is used once well; trees are left standing; the bush is not burnt.
• **Protecting the environment** keeps the soil, the river and the air clean for farming and for us.
• A pupil's part: grow one plant, water a tree, keep the compound clean, and finish the food on the plate.

**Words for my notebook**

Nigeria farms groundnut, cocoa, palm produce, rice and fish. Sustainable farming leaves good land for the next child.

**Let us talk**

1. Name two crops of the northern part of Nigeria.
2. What does sustainable agriculture mean in your own words?
3. Mention two ways of reducing waste at home.
4. Say one thing you can do to protect the environment.
