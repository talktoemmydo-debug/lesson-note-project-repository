### WEEK 1 — TECHNOLOGY AND DIGITAL DEVICES

*Depth: NERDC Primary 4 · Basic Digital Literacy · First Term W1 (pp 827–828).*

**You will learn to**

• say what technology means in one line
• name the digital devices I know
• point to a computer, a tablet, a phone, a watch and a camera
• tell which things at home are digital

**Things to know**

• **Technology** means the smart things people make and use to do work more easily.
• A **digital device** is a machine that thinks with electricity and shows us things on a screen.
• Devices I know: **computer, tablet, smartphone, smart watch, camera**.
• A **computer** is big and has a screen, keys and a mouse; a **tablet** is flat and I touch the screen with my finger.
• A **smartphone** fits in the hand; it calls, photographs, and plays music.
• A **smart watch** is worn on the wrist; it tells the time and counts my steps.
• A **camera** takes pictures; some cameras are inside a phone.
• Digital things at home: the television remote, the decoder, the radio with a screen, the microwave, the fan regulator, the ATM.
• Not everything electrical is a computer — the torch and the fridge use electricity but do not think.

**Words for my notebook**

Technology is what people make to work easily. Computer, tablet, phone, watch and camera are digital devices.

**Let us talk**

1. What is a digital device?
2. Point to the device your father or mother uses for calling.
3. What can a camera do?
4. Name one thing at home that is not a digital device.

### WEEK 2 — TYPES OF COMPUTER: DESKTOP, LAPTOP, TABLET

*Depth: NERDC Primary 4 · Basic Digital Literacy · First Term W1 (pp 827–828).*

**You will learn to**

• tell a desktop from a laptop and a tablet
• say what each type is good for
• say which parts come with a desktop
• choose the right computer for the right work

**Things to know**

• A **desktop** sits on a table and does not move about; it has a separate **monitor, keyboard, mouse** and a **CPU box**.
• A **laptop** opens and shuts like a book; the screen, the keys and the touchpad are all in it, and it has a battery.
• A **tablet** is one flat board with a screen you touch with your finger; it is the smallest and the lightest to carry.
• A desktop is used at the office or at home where it can stay; a laptop is carried to school, to work, to a journey.
• A tablet is good for watching, reading and drawing; a desktop or laptop is better for typing a long letter.
• All three can do the same kind of work; they differ in size, in how they are held, and in how they are moved.
• The **CPU** is the part that does the thinking, so it is called the brain of the computer.
• A laptop is closed when it is not in use, so that its screen is safe.

**Words for my notebook**

Desktop stays on the table, laptop folds and goes, tablet is held in the hand. The CPU is the brain.

**Let us talk**

1. Which computer do you carry in a bag?
2. What is the brain of the computer called?
3. Point to a device in this room that has a touchpad.
4. Why do we close a laptop?

### WEEK 3 — BASIC PARTS: MONITOR, KEYBOARD, MOUSE, CPU

*Depth: NERDC Primary 4 · Basic Digital Literacy · First Term W1 (pp 827–828).*

**You will learn to**

• name the four basic parts of a computer
• say what each part does
• point to each part on a real computer
• put the parts in the right order of work

**Things to know**

• The four basic parts: **monitor, keyboard, mouse and CPU**.
• The **monitor** is the screen; it shows what the computer is doing — pictures, letters and numbers.
• The **keyboard** has the letters and numbers; we press the keys to put information into the computer.
• The **mouse** is held in the hand; it moves the arrow on the screen and clicks to choose things.
• The **CPU** is the box that does the work and remembers things — the brain of the computer.
• Order of work: I **give** with the keyboard or the mouse → the **CPU thinks** → the **monitor shows** the answer.
• Other parts I may see: speakers (they give out sound), a printer (it puts the picture on paper), and a USB drive (it carries files about).
• The mouse is not an animal — it is a part of the computer, and it is held gently, not thrown.

**Words for my notebook**

Keyboard and mouse put work in; CPU does the work; monitor shows the work.

**Let us talk**

1. Which part shows you the pictures?
2. What do we press to type a letter?
3. Show me how you hold the mouse.
4. Which part is called the brain?

### WEEK 4 — USES OF THE COMPUTER IN DAILY LIFE

*Depth: NERDC Primary 4 · Basic Digital Literacy · First Term W1 (pp 827–828), with the Primary 5
Third Term "AI in daily life" line kept only as examples of uses.*

**You will learn to**

• say what people use a computer for at work
• say what I use a tablet for at home
• name machines in the shop, the bank and the hospital
• tell the difference between work play and learning on a device

**Things to know**

• People write letters, draw pictures and keep records on a computer.
• In the **bank**, a computer counts money and prints the receipt; in the **shop**, the machine that reads the price is a computer.
• In the **hospital**, the computer keeps the name of the patient and shows the result of the test.
• In the **school**, we use it to learn counting, letters and reading; a tablet can show me a story with pictures.
• Computers send messages and calls to people far away, and they print photographs.
• **Voice assistants** answer a question when we speak to them, and the device that shows me cartoons also **learns which cartoons I like** — that is the computer learning from what I do.
• Time on a device is of two kinds: **learning** (letters, numbers, a story) and **play** (games, cartoons). Play is only for a little while, and my eyes rest when I look away and go out to run.
• Nobody should watch a screen for the whole day; a device is a servant, not a master.

**Words for my notebook**

The computer counts money, prints, teaches and carries messages. We use it for learning, not only for play.

**Let us talk**

1. Mention two places where a computer is used.
2. What does the machine at the shop do?
3. Say one thing you can learn on a tablet.
4. Why should we not look at a screen all day?

### WEEK 5 — CARE AND HANDLING OF DEVICES

*Depth: NERDC Primary 4 · Basic Digital Literacy · First Term W1 (pp 827–828).*

**You will learn to**

• keep a device clean and dry
• hold and carry a device with two hands
• charge a device safely with a grown-up near
• shut a device the right way

**Things to know**

• Devices are **expensive and easily spoiled**, so we handle them gently.
• Keep food and drink away from the keyboard; water inside a device spoils it for good.
• Wipe a screen with a **soft, dry cloth** — not with a rough rag, not with wet water.
• Carry a laptop or tablet with **two hands**, closed, and put it in a padded bag before it travels.
• Do not pull the cable out of the socket by the wire — hold the plug itself.
• Charging: the charger is put in and taken out by a grown-up; a charging device is not played with in the bath.
• Switch off properly: save my work, shut the programme, then turn the power off; do not just remove the light.
• Do not drop, throw, hit or sit on a device; keep it out of the sun and away from a fan blowing dust.
• A phone with a cracked screen can cut a finger — tell a grown-up at once if a device breaks.

**Words for my notebook**

Clean hands, dry cloth, two hands to carry, grown-up for the plug. Care for the device and it serves long.

**Let us talk**

1. What two things spoil a keyboard quickly?
2. Show me how you carry a tablet.
3. Who puts the charger into the socket?
4. What do you do before switching a computer off?

**Worksheet**

1. A screen is wiped with A) a soft dry cloth B) a duster with water C) a rough stone D) a wet mop
2. Liquid near a keyboard is A) cheap B) dangerous to the device C) needed D) good
3. A laptop is carried with A) one finger B) a rope C) the teeth D) two hands
4. The charger is put into the socket by A) a grown-up B) the class dog C) any pupil D) a baby
5. Fill in: Do not pull the cable by the ____; hold the plug.
6. Mention three rules for taking care of devices.
7. Write two sentences on what you do before switching a computer off.

**My own work**

Wipe the dust off one device in your house with a soft dry cloth, and tell your class what you cleaned.

### WEEK 6 — COMPUTER LAB SAFETY RULES

*Depth: NERDC Primary 4 · Basic Digital Literacy · First Term W1 (pp 827–828).*

**You will learn to**

• say what a computer lab is
• state the rules we keep in the lab
• sit and move correctly in the lab
• tell the teacher at once when something is wrong

**Things to know**

• A **computer lab** is the room in the school where many computers are kept for lessons.
• We **walk** in the lab; we do not run, because a cable or a chair can throw us down.
• We go in **quietly**, when we are called, and we sit at the machine we are given.
• **No food, no drink and no water bottle** on the desk with a computer.
• We do not touch the cables, the sockets or the back of the machine; electric wire can bite.
• Only the teacher switches on the computers and the lights.
• We do not change the settings, delete somebody's work, or move the mouse and keyboard violently.
• If a machine gives smoke, a bad smell, a shock or a strange sound: **leave it and tell the teacher at once**.
• We push our chair in and leave the lab as we found it.

**Words for my notebook**

In the computer lab we walk, we sit quietly, we keep food out, and we tell the teacher at once if something is wrong.

**Let us talk**

1. What is a computer lab?
2. Why must we not run in the lab?
3. Who switches on the computers?
4. What do you do if a machine begins to smoke?

**Worksheet**

1. In the computer lab we must A) run B) walk C) shout D) jump
2. Food and drink in the lab are A) not allowed B) allowed on the desk C) needed D) given to machines
3. The first thing to do when a cable is loose is to A) pull it B) tell the teacher C) hide it D) cut it
4. The computers are switched on by A) the monitor B) the teacher C) the pupils D) any visitor
5. Fill in: We leave the lab as we ____ it.
6. Mention four safety rules of the computer lab.
7. Write two sentences on what you saw in the lab today and the rule that kept it safe.

**My own work**

### WEEK 8 — FROM THE ABACUS TO TODAY'S COMPUTER

*Depth: NERDC Primary 4 · Basic Digital Literacy · First Term W2 (pp 827–828).*

**You will learn to**

• name the first tool people used to count
• tell how computers changed over time
• name the five generations in order
• say what became smaller, faster and cheaper

**Things to know**

• Long ago people counted with **stones and fingers**, then with the **abacus** — a frame with beads that slide on wires.
• The abacus is still used in some markets and some schools: beads up means add, beads down means take away.
• The first real computing machines were **very big** — one filled a whole room, needed a bed of vacuum tubes, and could be spoiled by a fly inside.
• **Five generations of computers**, in order: **vacuum tubes → transistors → integrated circuits → microprocessors → AI**.
• Vacuum tubes glowed and grew hot; the **transistor** replaced them and was tiny; an **integrated circuit** put many parts on one little chip; a **microprocessor** put the whole brain on one chip.
• The fifth generation is the generation of **AI** — machines that learn and decide.
• With every generation the computer became **smaller, faster and cheaper**: what once filled a room now sits on a wrist.
• Old machines are found in museums; some grandparents still keep an abacus in a drawer.

**Words for my notebook**

Before computers: fingers, stones, the abacus. Then five generations — each smaller, faster and cheaper.

**Let us talk**

1. What did people use to count before machines?
2. What does an abacus look like?
3. Name the two biggest changes from the first computer to your father's phone.
4. Which generation are we living in now?

### WEEK 9 — KEY INVENTORS: CHARLES BABBAGE AND ADA LOVELACE

*Depth: NERDC Primary 4 · Basic Digital Literacy · First Term W2 (pp 827–828).*

**You will learn to**

• name the father of the computer
• say what Ada Lovelace did
• put the two inventors in their time order
• say why an idea can be as valuable as a machine

**Things to know**

• **Charles Babbage** was an Englishman who, about two hundred years ago, designed a machine called the **Analytical Engine**; because of that plan he is called the **father of the computer**.
• His first machine was called the **Difference Engine**; it was built to work out tables of numbers without mistakes.
• Babbage drew the plan and made parts, but the workshop of his day could not build the whole thing from his drawing.
• **Ada Lovelace** wrote the first set of instructions for his machine — the first **computer programme**; she is called the first programmer in the world.
• Ada saw further than her machine: she wrote that one day such a machine could compose music and do more than merely count.
• The **Ada** language of programming was named after her.
• One built the body, the other taught the body to think: an idea can be as valuable as a machine.
• Their names are remembered today in museums, in books and in the names of computer languages.

**Words for my notebook**

Babbage planned the first computer machine; Ada Lovelace wrote the first programme for it.

**Let us talk**

1. Who is called the father of the computer?
2. What did Ada Lovelace write?
3. Which of them came first with the plan — the machine or the programme?
4. Why do we still say their names today?
