### WEEK 1 — MEANING AND IMPORTANCE OF HISTORY (PART 1)

*Depth: NERDC Primary 1 · Nigerian History · First Term W1 (pp 649–650).*

**You will learn to**

• say what history is in one line
• give an example of the past from my own life
• name the people and things that tell us about the past
• say one reason we learn history

**Things to know**

• **History is the story of the past** — of what happened before now.
• Yesterday is past. Last week is past. Last year is past. This very minute, when it has gone, is past too.
• What I did yesterday is a little history: "I went to my uncle's house." "We ate rice."
• Last week: a birthday, a naming ceremony, a funeral, the day the rain broke the footpath.
• Last year: "I was in Nursery 1, and I was smaller."
• Who tells us the past? **Grandparents, fathers and mothers, the pastor and the imam, the old men of the street.**
• What shows us the past? **A photograph, an old object (the kerosene lamp, the iron that was heated in the fire), a book, a ruin, a monument.**
• Why we learn history: **it tells us about our families** — who our grandparents were, and where our people come from.
• History is not a fairy tale: a story that is *true* and *happened* is history.

**Words for my notebook**

History is the story of the past. Yesterday, last week and last year are all past. Grandparents and photographs tell us the past.

**Let us talk**

1. What is history?
2. Say one thing you did yesterday.
3. Mention two people who can tell you about the past.
4. What can an old photograph tell us?
5. Say one reason we learn history.

**Worksheet**

1. History is the story of A) today's lunch B) the past C) the future D) the sky
2. Which one belongs to the past? A) right now B) yesterday C) this minute D) today at noon
3. Who can tell us about the past? A) a grandparent B) a plate C) a stone D) a chair
4. A photograph of when you were small tells us about A) next term B) the weather C) the past D) the price of yam
5. Fill in: Last year I was in Nursery ____.
6. Mention two things that help us know about the past.
7. Write two sentences on something you did last week.

**My own work**

Ask at home for one true story about when you were a baby, and come to tell the class.

Draw three things that were here before you were born and colour the oldest one.

### WEEK 2 — MEANING AND IMPORTANCE OF HISTORY (PART 2)

*Depth: NERDC Primary 1 · Nigerian History · First Term W2 (pp 649–650).*

**You will learn to**

• say which family I belong to
• say where my people come from
• name a lesson the past teaches
• draw my family tree

**Things to know**

• History is about **identity**: to which family do I belong? Every family has a name, and my name is part of it.
• "I am a child of the ______ family." That family name came from my grandfather, and his father before him.
• History tells us **where we come from**: my father's people come from ______; my mother's people come from ______. It may be a village, a town, or a street in this city.
• Learning from the past is how we grow wise: grandma says, *"The farmer who worked his land ate well; the one who slept was hungry."*
• History helps us **make good decisions**: before I do a thing, I can ask "What happened last time somebody did this?"
• A **family tree** is a drawing that shows the order of the family: **grandparents at the top, then father and mother, then the children**.
• Drawing activity of the week: our trees went on the wall, and we looked at one another's.
• The past also tells us what has changed: lamp then, bulb now; mortar and pestle then, milling machine now.

**Words for my notebook**

I belong to the ______ family. My people come from ______. The past teaches us what is right.

**Let us talk**

1. What is your family name?
2. Where do your grandparents come from?
3. Say one lesson the past teaches us.
4. Whom do we put at the top of a family tree?
5. Mention one thing that has changed since your grandfather was small.

**Worksheet**

1. At the top of the family tree we write A) the babies B) the friends C) the grandparents D) the children
2. Your father's mother is your A) aunt B) sister C) cousin D) grandma
3. Knowing your family's name gives you A) a pen B) a shoe C) food D) identity
4. The past can teach us to A) work hard B) quarrel C) steal D) lie
5. Fill in: My father's people come from ____.
6. Draw or name the three levels of a family tree.
7. Write two sentences on something your family is known for.

### WEEK 3 — PEOPLES AND PLACES IN THE COMMUNITY (PART 1)

*Depth: NERDC Primary 1 · Nigerian History · First Term W3 (pp 649–650).*

**You will learn to**

• say what a community is
• name the people who live around me
• name the people who help us in the community
• greet a neighbour well

**Things to know**

• A **community** is the people and the places around us — the street, the houses, the shops, the church and the mosque, the field where children play.
• In our community there are **different people**: children, youths, mothers, fathers, grandmothers, teachers, drivers, traders, the watchman.
• **Neighbours** are the people who live beside us; **friends** are the people we play with. A neighbour is not always a relation, but we still greet him.
• People who help us: **the nurse** (she treats the sick), **the policeman** (he keeps us from harm), **the driver** (he carries us), **the trader** (she sells food), **the teacher** (she teaches us), **the cleaner** (he sweeps the street).
• Some people help with their hands, some with a machine, some with words — all of them are needed.
• Nobody can do everything alone: the man who plants the rice is not the man who drives it to town.
• Living near people means: greet in the morning, do not throw dirt into another man's gutter, share what you have, and keep noise down when a baby is sleeping.
• Role-play of the week: carry a basket to the neighbour's door, ask for a cup, say thank you.

**Words for my notebook**

A community is the people and places around us. Neighbours, friends and helpers all belong to it.

**Let us talk**

1. What is a community?
2. Name two people who live near you.
3. Who treats people when they are sick?
4. Who keeps the street safe?
5. Say two things we do to live well with neighbours.

**Worksheet**

2. The person who sells us food is a A) nurse B) trader C) teacher D) pilot
3. Who keeps the community safe? A) the goat B) the baker C) the policeman D) the baby
4. Our neighbours are the people who A) live beside us B) sleep C) teach in school D) live far away
5. Fill in: The person who sweeps our street is the ____.
6. Mention three people who help us in the community.
7. Write two sentences on what you do when you meet your neighbour in the morning.

**My own work**

Draw three people who help people in your community and write one thing each of them does.

### WEEK 4 — PEOPLES AND PLACES IN THE COMMUNITY (PART 2)

*Depth: NERDC Primary 1 · Nigerian History · First Term W4 (pp 649–650).*

**You will learn to**

• name the three major ethnic groups of Nigeria
• say one thing each group is known for
• name other groups living in our community
• say how we treat people of other groups

**Things to know**

• Nigeria has many peoples. The three **major ethnic groups** are the **Hausa**, the **Igbo** and the **Yoruba**.
• The **Hausa** are mainly in the **north**; they greet with **"Sannu"**; they eat **tuwo** with soup; the big embroidered robe is the **babban riga**.
• The **Igbo** are in the **south-east**; they greet with **"Ndewo"**; they eat **fufu** with soup; some men wear a red cap.
• The **Yoruba** are in the **south-west**; they greet with **"Ẹ̀ káàárọ̀"**; they eat **amala** with **ewédú** soup; their cloth is **àdìrè**, and a chief wears **agbádá**.
• Other groups in Nigeria: **Nupe, Tiv, Kanuri, Ijaw, Efik, Fulani, Igala, Ibibio, Edo, Urhobo** — some of them live in our own community.
• What we learn from this: **different is not wrong**. Another people's food is not a laughing matter; another people's greeting is not a foolish thing.
• We **respect** people of every group: we greet in their own language when we can, and we do not call anybody's tribe names.
• The line of the week, said three times: **"We are all Nigerians."**

**Words for my notebook**

Hausa, Igbo and Yoruba are the three big groups. There are many others. Different tongues, one country — we are all Nigerians.

**Let us talk**

1. Name the three major ethnic groups of Nigeria.
2. Which greeting belongs to the Hausa people?
3. Mention one food and one cloth of the Yoruba.
4. Name one other ethnic group you know.
5. What should we never do to another group's language?

**Worksheet**

**My own work**

### WEEK 5 — PLACES IN THE COMMUNITY (PART 1)

*Depth: NERDC Primary 1 · Nigerian History · First Term W7 (pp 649–650).*

**You will learn to**

• name the important places in our community
• say what each place is used for
• say how we behave in each place
• tell where help is found in an emergency

**Things to know**

• Important places: **school, hospital, market, church, mosque, police station, fire station, park and playground**, the motor park, the bank, the post office.
• The **school** is where we learn; we come on time and we carry our books.
• The **market** is where food and things are bought; we hold our money well and we say "thank you" to the seller.
• The **hospital** is where the sick are treated; we speak softly and we sit down to wait for our turn.
• **Church** and **mosque** are places of prayer: we go in quietly, we do not run about, and we respect the people worshipping whatever our own family's place is.
• The **police station** is for a thief, for a lost child, for any danger; the **fire station** is for fire. Both work at night as well as in the day.
• The **park and playground** are for games and rest: we take turns and we do not push the smaller children.
• If a grown-up is not around and something is wrong, a child can go to the police station — help is kept there for everybody.

**Words for my notebook**

School for learning, market for buying, hospital for sickness, church and mosque for prayer, police and fire station for help.

4. How should we behave in a place of prayer?
5. What do we do on the playground when two children want one swing?

### WEEK 6 — PLACES IN THE COMMUNITY (PART 2)

*Depth: NERDC Primary 1 · Nigerian History · First Term W8 (pp 649–650).*

**You will learn to**

• name the traditional places in our community
• say who rules in the palace and what he does
• say what the community hall is used for
• describe one old thing in our area

**Things to know**

• Besides the hospital and the market, every town has **old, traditional places**: the **palace**, the **community hall**, and sometimes a **monument**.
• The **palace** is the home of the **traditional ruler**. In Yorubaland he is the **Oba** (in some towns the Aláàfin, the Deji, the Olú); in the north he is the **Emir**; in Igboland he may be the **Igwe** or the eldest man of the kindred.
• The ruler wears **beads and a crown**, sits on a **stool**, gives titles, and settles quarrels in his palace. He is honoured, and he listens to the chiefs with him.
• The **community hall** is where the whole town meets: to plan the water, to fix the broken road, to arrange the festival, to congratulate a child who got a scholarship.
• A **monument** is something old that people keep because it tells the story of the place: an old bridge, a wall, a tree where the first settlers met, the site of the first school.
• Picture study of the week: the class looked at the pictures and said — What is this? Where is it? Is it old or new? Who uses it?
• If our town has an old place near us, a short walk to see it is a field trip — the safety rules of Basic Science apply.
• These places belong to **everybody**; that is why we do not write on their walls or break anything there.

**Words for my notebook**

The palace of the ruler, the community hall of the town, and the monuments of the past are our traditional places.

**Let us talk**

1. Where does a traditional ruler live?
2. Name the ruler's title in the north of Nigeria and in Yorubaland.
3. What happens in the community hall?
4. What is a monument?
5. Who do the old places of a town belong to?

**Worksheet**

### WEEK 8 — LIVING TOGETHER IN PEACE

*Depth: NERDC Primary 1 · Nigerian History · First Term W9 (pp 649–650).*

**You will learn to**

• say what tolerance means
• name ways of respecting people of other groups
• say why we play and learn together
• give examples of sharing and caring

**Things to know**

• **Tolerance** means: other people are different from me, and that is all right.
• Different things about people: their name, their language, their food, their cloth, their place of worship, the way they greet.
• A child with tolerance does four things: he **does not mock another child's name**, he **does not say another person's god is wrong**, he **is ready to sit with anybody**, and he **learns to greet in another language**.
• Playing together teaches more than any lesson: on the ball field the big boy must pass to the small one, or there is no game.
• Learning together: when one child knows the answer and another does not, the one who knows helps, and both are better for it.
• **Sharing and caring**: share the biscuit, share the crayons; help the child that fell; speak to the child who is sitting alone.
• We are **stronger together**: one stick breaks in the hand, a bundle of sticks does not; one thread is not a rope, many threads are.
• If a quarrel starts, the peaceful child walks away and tells a grown-up; he does not add to the noise.
• The sentence of the week: **"What I want for myself, I also want for you."**

**Words for my notebook**

Tolerance is respecting people who are different. We play together, learn together, share and care. We are stronger together.

**Let us talk**

1. What is tolerance?
2. Mention two things a tolerant child does not do.
3. What happened when the class drew on one sheet of paper?
4. Name two things you can share at school.
5. What do you do when two friends begin to quarrel?

**Worksheet**

3. With one ball in class we should A) take it home B) fight for it C) take turns D) hide it

### WEEK 9 — NATIONAL UNITY AND DIVERSITY

*Depth: NERDC Primary 1 · Nigerian History · First Term W10 (pp 649–650).*

**You will learn to**

• say that Nigeria is one country with many peoples
• name the differences among Nigerians
• describe how our diversity is celebrated
• identify the Nigerian flag and say what it represents

**Things to know**

• **Nigeria is one country with many peoples** — more than **two hundred and fifty** groups live inside it.
• Our differences: **languages** (Hausa, Igbo, Yoruba, Tiv, Nupe, Ijaw and the rest), **foods** (garri, tuwo, fufu, eba, rice), **cloths** (agbádá, babban riga, George wrapper, àdìrè), and **festivals**.
• Festivals we know: the **Durbar** in the north (horses and colour), **Eyo** in Lagos (white cloth and beads), the **New Yam** festival in the south-east (the first yam of the season), the **Argo/masquerade** days in many towns.
• At a festival, **everybody is invited**; food is given to the stranger as well as to the family. That is how diversity is celebrated, not hidden.
• The language we all use to speak to one another in school is **English**, while each child keeps his own language at home — a Nigerian can hold two or three languages.
• The **Nigerian flag** is **two green strips with one white strip in the middle**: **green for the land and its green things**, **white for peace and unity**.
• We stand and sing the **national anthem**; the flag is saluted in the morning assembly.
• The sentence of the week, said by the whole class: **"Different people, one Nigeria."**

**Words for my notebook**

Nigeria has more than 250 peoples. Different tongues, foods and festivals; one flag, one country. Different people, one Nigeria.

**Let us talk**

4. Mention one festival and where it is held.
5. Say the sentence of the week.
