### WEEK 1 — VOWELS AND CONSONANTS

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy (Language Domain) · First Term W1 (pp 465–469).*

**You will learn to**

• name the five vowels: a, e, i, o, u
• say that all the other letters are consonants
• sort letters into vowels and consonants
• find the vowels inside a word

**Things to know**

• Of the **26** letters, **five are vowels: a, e, i, o, u**. Sometimes **y** works as a vowel too (my, fly, happy).
• The other **twenty-one** letters are **consonants**: b, c, d, f, g, h, j, k, l, m, n, p, q, r, s, t, v, w, x, y, z.
• Why they are special: when I say a vowel my mouth stays open and the air goes out freely; a consonant is made by closing or narrowing the mouth with lips, teeth or tongue.
• **Every word in English has at least one vowel.** "I" and "a" are words made of one vowel only.
• A word without a vowel is not readable — that is why we write "skul" as **school**.
• Two vowels together often make one long sound: **see, boat, rain, read**.
• Sorting game: two hoops on the floor, vowel letters in one hoop, consonant letters in the other.
• Counting vowels in a word: **banana** has 3 a's and 2 a's — five vowels in all; **book** has 2.
• Clap the vowels: I clap once for each vowel sound I hear in a spoken word.
• This work is the foundation for reading and writing; every word I read will need a vowel.

**Words for my notebook**

The vowels are a, e, i, o, u. The other 21 letters are consonants. Every word has at least one vowel.

**Let us talk**

1. Say the five vowels.
2. How many consonants are there?
3. Circle the vowels in your name.
4. Is "m" a vowel or a consonant, and how do you know?

**Worksheet**

1. The five vowels are A) a, e, i, o, u B) a, b, c, d, e C) x, y, z, w, q D) b, c, d, f, g
2. Letters that are not vowels are called A) names B) sounds C) consonants D) words
3. The vowel(s) in "cat" is/are A) t B) c C) a D) none

5. Fill in: The letter that is sometimes a vowel is ____.

### WEEK 2 — SINGLE SOUNDS: d, a, i, m

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy (Language Domain) · First Term W2 (pp 465–469).*

**You will learn to**

• say the sounds /d/, /a/, /i/, /m/
• write the letters d, a, i, m
• blend these four sounds into real words
• hear where a sound sits in a word

**Things to know**

• Four sounds this week: **/d/** as in dog, doll, dad, dig; **/a/** as in apple, ant, cat, mat; **/i/** as in ink, insect, sit, pin; **/m/** as in mum, man, mat, milk.
• With only four sounds I can already make real words: **dad, mad, dim, aim, amid, man** (with /n/ from before).
• **Blending** is saying the sounds fast together: **d – a – d** = *dad*; **m – a – d** = *mad*; **d – i – m** = *dim*.
• **Segmenting** is the opposite: I hear "dim" and say /d/ /i/ /m/, three sounds, three letters.
• Sound positions: in "dog", /d/ is at the **beginning**; in "mad", /a/ is in the **middle**; in "bid", /d/ is at the **end**.
• Writing: **d** is a circle with a tall stem up; **a** is a circle with a short line; **i** is a short line with a dot; **m** is three little humps.
• Say the sound, not the letter name: /m/, not "em", when we are building words.
• Finger spelling: one finger per sound, then write the letters in order.
• Tricky pair: /d/ and /b/ feel the same in the mouth — the difference is that /d/ uses the tongue tip and stays short.
• Practice game: I raise a card when I hear /m/ in a word you say.

**Words for my notebook**

d a i m — /d/ dog, /a/ apple, /i/ ink, /m/ mum. d-a-d = dad. m-a-d = mad. d-i-m = dim.

**Let us talk**

1. Make the sound /m/ for me.
2. Blend: m – a – n.
3. What is the first sound in "dog"?
4. Which of our four letters needs a dot?
5. Say a word that ends with /d/.


![Four blank tiles in a row with a pencil.](assets/img/nursery-2/1st-term/mathematics-english-language/week2-four-blank-tiles.png)
**Worksheet**

1. The first sound in "mum" is A) /m/ B) /u/ C) /d/ D) /n/
2. d – i – m spells A) dam B) dim C) mad D) mid
3. The sound /a/ is heard at the beginning of A) dog B) mum C) apple D) ink
4. Which letter is written with a dot on top? A) a B) m C) d D) i
5. Fill in: The word "dad" is made of ____ sounds.
6. Write each of d, a, i, m four times in small letters.

Write the words dad, mad, dim, man and use each in a spoken sentence in class.

### WEEK 3 — SIGHT WORDS: You, Me, Her, We

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy (Language Domain) · First Term W3 (pp 465–469).*

**You will learn to**

• read four sight words at once
• write them correctly
• say what each one means
• use them in sentences

**Things to know**

• A **sight word** is a word I know on sight — I do not sound it out, I read it in one look.
• This week's four: **you, me, her, we**.
• **me** points at myself; **you** points at the person I am talking to; **her** points at a girl or woman; **we** means me and other people together.
• "You" and "we" start with the same letter **w**? No — **y**ou and **w**e: look carefully, these two are often swapped.
• These words break the sound rules a little, so they must be memorised by the eye: "you" has a **y** but no /y/ sound in it.
• Flash-card drill: the card shows for two seconds; I say the word before it is hidden.
• Sentences I can now read: *"You and me." "We see her." "Her bag is red."*
• Writing: these words go on the line with no capitals unless they begin a sentence; "I" is the only small word that is always a capital.
• Memory games: word-to-picture matching, and "what is missing?" when one card is hidden under a book.
• My sight-word bank is growing: this week four, next week three more.

**Words for my notebook**

you · me · her · we — I read them without spelling. "We see her."

**Let us talk**

1. Which word points at yourself?
2. Read this card: "you".
3. Make a sentence with "we".
4. Which of these four words is about a girl?

### WEEK 4 — SIGHT WORDS: I, Go, Car

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy (Language Domain) · First Term W4 (pp 465–469).*

**You will learn to**

• read and write three sight words you have not used yet
• always write "I" as a capital
• join new words with the old ones
• read short sentences

**Things to know**

• Three new words: **I**, **go**, **car**.
• **I** is a single letter but it is always a **capital**, wherever it stands: *"I go." "She and I run."*
• **go** is a verb — it is a doing word; **car** is a noun — it is a thing.
• My sight words are now seven: **you, me, her, we, I, go, car**.
• Short sentences I can read and write: *"I go." "We go." "I see a car." "You and I go." "I go in her car."*
• Word order matters in English: *"I go car in"* is wrong; *"I go in the car"* is right.
• Read the whole sentence, then look back at the words you did not know.
• Building sentences: add one word at a time to a short sentence and read it again each time.
• Copying practice: read the word, cover it, write it, then check — never copy letter by letter.
• Dictation: I hear the sentence twice, then write it, then read my own writing back.

**Words for my notebook**

I · go · car. "I" is always big. Sentences: I go. We go. I see a car.

**Let us talk**

1. Why is "I" written big?
2. Read this sentence: "I go in her car."
3. Which of this week's words is a doing word?
4. Make a sentence that has "car" and "we".


![Three blank cards with a small toy car.](assets/img/nursery-2/1st-term/mathematics-english-language/week4-three-cards-and-a-car.png)
**Worksheet**

1. The word "I" is written A) always a capital B) always small C) only at the start D) with a dot
2. "car" is a A) thing B) doing word C) letter D) sound
3. Which sentence is correct? A) I go in the car. B) I go car. C) Car I go. D) Go I car.
4. Which sight words do I now know? A) six B) seven C) four D) ten
5. Fill in: "We ____ in her car." (go / car / I)

7. Read aloud: "You and I go. I see her car."

**My own work**

Write five sentences using any of the seven sight words, and underline every "I".

### WEEK 5 — DIGRAPH: th, ck, wh

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy (Language Domain) · First Term W7 (pp 465–469).*

**You will learn to**

• say what a digraph is
• read and write words with th, ck and wh
• hear /th/ at the beginning and at the end of words
• sort words by their digraph

**Things to know**

• A **digraph** is **two letters that make one sound**: *sh, ch, th, ph, wh, ck*.
• **th** makes two different sounds: a soft/buzzy one at the start of **the, this, that, with, they**, and a hissy one at the end of **bath, math, three, think, thumb**.
• For the buzzy /th/ my tongue tip touches the teeth and my voice works; for the hissy /th/ the voice is off. Feel your throat to tell them apart.
• **ck** says /k/ and is nearly always at the **end** of a small word: **back, clock, duck, pick, stick, black, kick**.
• **wh** says /w/ at the start of questions: **what, when, where, which, white, wheel**.
• Words where two letters do one job are not spelled the way they sound — that is why they need practice.
• Sorting game: three columns on the board — th, ck, wh — and I put each word card in its column.
• Reading practice: *"Who took the clock from the bench?"* — three digraphs in one sentence.
• Writing: underline the two letters that work together, so I do not forget them.
• Sound hunt: how many words with **th** can our class say in one minute?

**Words for my notebook**

A digraph is two letters, one sound. th: this, bath. ck: duck, clock. wh: what, when, where.

**Let us talk**

1. What is a digraph?
2. Say "this" and "bath" — where is your tongue?
3. Name two words ending in ck.
4. Which two letters are in "what"?
5. Is "sh" a digraph? Why?


![Three blank cards beside a hand bell for hearing sounds.](assets/img/nursery-2/1st-term/mathematics-english-language/week5-three-cards-and-a-bell.png)
**Worksheet**

1. A digraph is A) a capital letter B) a word with six letters C) two letters making one sound D) two sounds in one letter
2. The digraph at the end of "duck" is A) ck B) wh C) sh D) th
3. "think" begins with the sound A) /t/ B) /th/ (voice off) C) /f/ D) /s/
4. Which word has "wh"? A) rich B) chin C) back D) which
5. Fill in: The two letters that make the /k/ sound at the end of "clock" are ____.
6. Write two th-words, two ck-words and two wh-words.
7. Underline every digraph in this sentence: "They took the black clock home."

**My own work**

### WEEK 6 — SIGHT WORDS: The, This, They

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy (Language Domain) · First Term W8 (pp 465–469).*

**You will learn to**

• read and write three new sight words
• notice the "th" digraph inside them
• use them in sentences
• read longer sentences

**Things to know**

• Three more sight words: **the**, **this**, **they**.
• All three begin with the **th** digraph I learnt last week — so my eye already knows two letters of each word.
• **"the"** is the most common word in English; it points at the one thing you mean: *the car, the fan*.
• **this** is for one thing near me; **these** would be for many (that comes later).
• **they** means more than one person or thing already spoken about: *"Where are the boys? They are outside."*
• My sight words are now ten: **you, me, her, we, I, go, car, the, this, they**.
• Sentences I can read: *"This is the car." "They see me." "The cat sat on the mat." "I go with them."*
• Do not write "teh" — the letters in "the" are t-h-e and the h is not a mistake.
• Flash-card speed: ten cards, ten seconds each, no sounding out.
• Writing practice: one sentence with "this", one with "they", then read them back to check.

**Words for my notebook**

the · this · they — all start with the th digraph. "This is the car." "They see me."

**Let us talk**

1. Which of our new words is the commonest word in English?
2. Read: "They see this."
3. Point at one thing and say a sentence with "this".
4. What do "the", "this" and "they" have in common at the start?

### WEEK 8 — DIGRAPH: ph, sh, ch

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy (Language Domain) · First Term W9 (pp 465–469).*

**You will learn to**

• read and write words with ph, sh and ch
• know that ph sounds like f
• tell the three sounds apart by ear
• sort words into digraph families

**Things to know**

• **ph** says **/f/**: **phone, photo, elephant, graph, dolphin**. Strange, but true in most English words.
• **sh** says **/ʃ/** — the quiet sound a mother makes: **ship, shop, fish, wash, she, shell, brush, shoe**.
• **ch** says **/tʃ/** — a quick soft hitting sound: **chair, church, chat, chip, much, bench, lunch, chin**.
• "sh" and "ch" are easy to confuse by ear: *ship / chip*, *wash / watch*. Say them slowly, then check.
• **sh** can sit at the start or the end of a word (shop, fish); **ch** is common at both ends too (chair, much); **ph** is usually at the start or end (phone, graph).
• Digraph families I now have: **th, ck, wh, ph, sh, ch**.
• Sorting game: put word cards under the right digraph on the wall chart.
• Sentence reading: *"The children wish for a new phone."* — ch, sh and ph all in one line.
• Writing: write the two letters together, never one alone; "fone" is a spelling mistake even though it sounds right.

**Words for my notebook**

ph = /f/ (phone, photo). sh (ship, fish). ch (chair, much). Six digraphs now: th, ck, wh, ph, sh, ch.

**Let us talk**

1. What sound does ph make?
2. Name three things with "sh" in their names.
3. Say "ship" then "chip" — how are they different?
4. In "elephant", which letters say /f/?
5. Which digraph is in "lunch"?

**Worksheet**

1. "ph" is pronounced like A) f B) v C) p D) ph
2. Which word has the "sh" digraph? A) step B) soap C) shop D) ship and shop — choose the odd one
3. The digraph in "much" is A) th B) ck C) sh D) ch
4. Which spelling is correct? A) phoe B) phone C) phon D) fone

### WEEK 9 — SIGHT WORDS: Photo, She, Chart · CONSONANT BLENDS: Play, Fly, Greet

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy (Language Domain) · First Term W10 (pp 465–469).*

**You will learn to**

• read and write three sight words that carry digraphs
• say what a consonant blend is
• read words with pl, fl and gr
• tell a blend from a digraph
• read and write sentences using everything I know

**Things to know**

• Three sight words: **photo** (has **ph**), **she** (has **sh**), **chart** (has **ch**) — my digraph words are now sight words.
• A **consonant blend** is **two consonants together where I still hear both sounds**: **pl**ay, **fl**y, **gr**eet, **bl**ue, **cl**ap, **fr**og.
• That is the difference: in a **digraph** two letters make **one** sound (sh, ch, ph); in a **blend** two letters keep **both** sounds (pl = /p/ + /l/).
• **pl**: play, please, plant, plus, plum.
• **fl**: fly, flag, flip, flat, flower.
• **gr**: greet, green, grab, grass, grape.
• My sight words are now thirteen: you, me, her, we, I, go, car, the, this, they, photo, she, chart.
• Reading: *"She can play with the children." "I greet the girl in the green dress." "We go to the chart."*
• Blending practice with three sounds: **s-p-l-a-t** has five letters but the blend /spl/ plus at — that is how longer words begin to open for me.
• Last week of teaching: I read my thirteen words and my six digraphs from the wall chart without help.

**Words for my notebook**

photo · she · chart. Blends: pl (play), fl (fly), gr (greet). A blend keeps both sounds; a digraph makes one.

**Let us talk**

1. Which digraph is in "photo"?
2. What sounds are in the blend "fl"?
3. Say a word beginning with "gr".
4. Is "sh" a blend or a digraph, and how do you know?
5. Read this sentence: "They play in the green grass."

1. "photo", "she" and "chart" each contain A) six letters B) two vowels C) a blend D) a digraph
