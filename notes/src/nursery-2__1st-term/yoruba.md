### WEEK 1 — Ede: Didaruko nnkan inu yara ikawe / Asa: Ikini ni ile Yoruba / Litireso: Orin keekeekee (imototo, ibawi)

*Depth: school-generated — three strands as printed in the master corpus for N2 F1 W1.*

**You will learn to**

• name things in the classroom in Yoruba
• greet an elder the Yoruba way
• say the words of a small song on cleanliness and respect

**Things to know — Ẹ̀dẹ̀ (naming things in the room)**

• yàrá ìkáwé — the classroom
• tébèèlì — table · ìgáòkó — chair · pátákó — the board
• ìwé — book · ìyàgà/penṣìlì — pencil · ẹlẹ́dọ̀tí — eraser
• bẹ́ẹ̀dì — bed (in the picture) · wọ̀nyí ni nǹkan tí mo rí nínú yàrá — "these are the things I see in the room"
• Pattern of speech: **"Èyí ni ______."** (This is a ______.)
• Naming game: I touch a thing and say its Yoruba name; my friend says it back.

**Things to know — Àṣà (greeting in Yorubaland)**

• In Yorubaland we **greet**; we do not walk past a person in silence.
• **Ẹ́ káàárọ̀** — good morning · **Ẹ́ káàṣánṣá** — good afternoon · **Ẹ́ káàlẹ̀** — good evening
• **Ọ́ dábọ̀** — welcome (to one who has returned) · **Ẹ ṣé** — thank you
• A boy greets an elder by **ìdòbì** (prostration); a girl by **ìkúnlẹ̀** (kneeling). To a king: **"Ẹ̀ kóò"** with the hands on the ground.
• When an elder greets me, I stop what I am doing and answer.
• Greeting is the first lesson of Yoruba manners: **àgànmọ́** (good character).

**Things to know — Lítíréṣọ̀ (the small song)**

• Orin kékeré — a small song. We sing it twice, then we do the actions.
• *"Ìmọ̀tọ̀́ tọ́, ìbàwọ̀n rẹ̀;* — cleanliness is right, and respect goes with it;
• *"ọmọ tí ó mọ́, ni a fiṣọ́ ọwọ́."* — the child that is clean is the one people welcome.
• Words of the song to say aloud: **ìmọ̀tọ̀́** (cleanliness), **ìbàwọ̀n** (respect), **ọmọ** (child), **ọwọ́** (hand).

**Words for my notebook**

Ẹ̀ káàárọ̀ · Ẹ̀ káàṣánṣá · Ẹ̀ káàlẹ̀ · Ẹ́ ṣé. Èyí ni ìwé. — This is a book.

**Let us talk**

1. Point to the chair and say its Yoruba name.
2. How do you greet your teacher at eight in the morning?
3. What does a girl do when she greets an elder?
4. Sing the small song with the actions.

**Worksheet**

1. "Good morning" in Yoruba is A) ẹ̀ káàlẹ̀ B) ẹ̀ káàárọ̀ C) ọ́ dábọ̀ D) ẹ́ ṣé
2. Ìwé means A) chair B) book C) board D) bag
3. A girl greets an elder by A) kneeling B) shouting C) sitting down D) running
4. "Ẹ́ ṣé" is said when somebody A) gives you something B) calls you C) hurts you D) leaves
5. Fill in: The Yoruba name for the classroom is yàrá ____.
6. Say four things in the room in Yoruba.
7. Sing or say the small song of the week to your class.

**My own work**

Write the Yoruba name of five things in this room, and greet three people at home this evening in Yoruba.

Draw five things in your classroom and colour them; say each Yoruba name as you colour it.

### WEEK 2 — Ede: Onka ede Yoruba 1-10 / Asa: Iwa rere / Litireso: Orin ikilo fun omode

*Depth: school-generated — three strands as printed for N2 F1 W2.*

**You will learn to**

• count from one to ten in Yoruba
• name the marks of good character
• say a small warning song for children

**Things to know — Ẹ̀dẹ̀ (counting 1–10)**

• **ọ̀kan** 1 · **èjì** 2 · **ẹ̀ta** 3 · **ẹ̀rin** 4 · **àárún** 5
• **ẹ̀fà** 6 · **ẹ̀je** 7 · **ẹ̀jọ** 8 · **ẹ̀sán** 9 · **ẹ̀wá** 10
• Clap once for ọ̀kan, twice for èjì… ten claps for ẹ̀wá.
• Counting backwards: ẹ̀wá, ẹ̀sán, ẹ̀jọ, ẹ̀je, ẹ̀fà, àárún, ẹ̀rin, ẹ̀ta, èjì, ọ̀kan.
• Ask and answer: **"Meloo ni o ní?"** (How many do you have?) — **"Mẹ́ta ni mo ní."** (I have three.)
• Numbers are used for days, for age and for the market.

**Things to know — Àṣà (good character — ìwà rere)**

• **Ìwà rere** means good character, and it is the greatest wealth of a child.
• A child of good character: tells the **truth** (òtítọ́), is **kind** (àànú), **shares**, **obeys**, **does not steal**, and **does not lie**.
• **"Ìwà lẹ́wàá"** — character is the true beauty; a fine face without good character is nothing.
• A good child greets, says "Ẹ́ ṣé", and returns what he borrowed.
• The Yoruba say: **"Ọmọ dé, a wo bí a ṣe ń hùwà"** — when a child arrives, people watch how he behaves.

**Things to know — Lítíréṣọ̀ (warning song — orin ìkìlọ̀)**

• An **orin ìkìlọ̀** is a song that warns children away from bad things.
• *"Má ṣe kòrò, kí owó má ṣè sọnù;* — do not be idle, so that your money will not sleep;
• *"má ṣe ja, kí ọ̀rẹ́ má ṣè bọ́."* — do not quarrel, so that your friend will not fall away.
• The song has three rules: do not be lazy, do not quarrel, do not tell lies.
• We say it as a rhyme in the morning and sing it once before we sit down.

**Words for my notebook**

ọ̀kan · èjì · ẹ̀ta · ẹ̀rin · àárún · ẹ̀fà · ẹ̀je · ẹ̀jọ · ẹ̀sán · ẹ̀wá. Ìwà lẹ́wàá.

**Let us talk**

1. Count from ọ̀kan to ẹ̀wá.
2. What is the Yoruba word for five?
3. Mention three marks of good character.
4. What does "Ìwà lẹ́wàá" mean?

**Worksheet**

1. The Yoruba word for four is A) ẹ̀ta B) àárún C) ẹ̀rin D) ẹ̀fà
2. ẹ̀wá means A) eight B) six C) ten D) nine
3. "Ìwà lẹ́wàá" teaches that A) food is good B) character is beauty C) clothes are beauty D) money is beauty
4. Which one is NOT good character? A) sharing B) lying C) truth D) kindness
5. Fill in: The Yoruba for five is ____.
6. Count from 1 to 10 in Yoruba, then backwards.
7. Say two rules of the warning song in your own words.

**My own work**

Write the Yoruba names of numbers one to ten in order, and count ten beans into a cup aloud.

Draw ten oranges and colour them while you count them out loud in Yoruba.

### WEEK 3 — Ede: Kika alifabeeti ede Yoruba / Asa: Awon ojo ninu ose / Litireso: Orin idaraya

*Depth: school-generated — three strands as printed for N2 F1 W3.*

**You will learn to**

• say the Yoruba alphabet in order
• name the seven days of the week in Yoruba
• join in a play song with the right actions

**Things to know — Ẹ̀dẹ̀ (the Yoruba alphabet — alifábéẹ̀dẹ̀)**

• The Yoruba alphabet has **twenty-five letters: 18 consonants and 7 vowels**.
• The vowels are: **a, e, ẹ, i, o, ọ, u** — the dots under **ẹ** and **ọ** change the sound.
• The consonants are: **b, d, f, g, gb, h, j, k, l, m, n, ń, p, r, s, ṣ, t, w, y**.
• Yoruba does not use **c, q, v, x, z**; those are only found in borrowed words.
• **gb** and **ṣ** are two letters each: gb as in "bag-bag", ṣ as in "she" but shorter.
• The **ń** (n with a dot) is a nasal sound: ìń ìń — as in "ọ̀nà".
• Three tones mark the voice: **à** (low), **á** (high), **ā/à** with the middle — and the combination marks, e.g. **ǒ, ǐ, ẹ́**.

**Things to know — Àṣà (the days of the week — àwọn ọjọ́ inú ọ̀ṣẹ̀)**

• **Ọjọ́ Àìkú** — Sunday · **Ọjọ́ Ajé** — Monday · **Ọjọ́ Ìṣẹ́gun** — Tuesday
• **Ọjọ́-rúkú-ọ̀jọ́** — Wednesday · **Ọjọ́ Bọ̀ṣẹ̀** — Thursday · **Ọjọ́ Ẹtì** — Friday · **Ọjọ́ Àbámẹ́ta** — Saturday
• Say them in order and clap seven times: the week is complete, then it begins again.
• Our markets have days: Ọjọ́ Àìkú is the day of church and rest; Ọjọ́ Ẹtì is the day of prayer for the Muslims.
• The names teach history: **Ìṣẹ́gun** (victory), **Bọ̀ṣẹ̀** (the market day of the Ibadan people), **Ẹtì** (Friday).

**Things to know — Lítíréṣọ̀ (play song — orin ìdáráya)**

• **Orin ìdáráya** is a song sung while we play, with actions and a ring of children.
• *"Yíyí po, a má yí po;* — going round, we turn round;
• *"ọmọ tí ó bá ṣàn, ni ó máa ṣojú."* — the child that runs fast will be the one chosen.
• How to play: children hold hands in a ring, sing twice, and on the last word one child is chosen to stand in the middle.
• The play song teaches turn-taking and the rhythm of the language.

**Words for my notebook**

a b d e ẹ f g gb h i j k l m n ń o ọ p r s ṣ t w y — twenty-five letters; 7 vowels.

**Let us talk**

1. How many letters are in the Yoruba alphabet?
2. Say the seven vowels.
3. Which day is Ọjọ́ Bọ̀ṣẹ̀?
4. Sing the play song and show the action.

**Worksheet**

1. The Yoruba alphabet has A) 21 letters B) 25 letters C) 26 letters D) 30 letters
2. Which of these is a Yoruba vowel? A) q B) v C) ẹ D) c
3. Ọjọ́ Àìkú is A) Friday B) Sunday C) Wednesday D) Monday
4. The two Yoruba letters with special sounds are gb and A) sh B) ch C) kh D) ṣ
5. Fill in: The seven ____ of the week are called àwọn ọjọ́.
6. Say the days of the week in Yoruba in order.
7. Name the letters of the Yoruba alphabet from a to gb.

**My own work**

Copy the twenty-five Yoruba letters into your notebook, and write your own day of the week under each.

### WEEK 4 — Ede: Isorongbesi laarin akekoo / Asa: Imototo ara eni / Litireso: Orin iwa rere

*Depth: school-generated — three strands as printed for N2 F1 W4.*

**You will learn to**

• speak politely to another pupil in Yoruba
• say the daily acts of keeping the body clean
• sing a song of good character

**Things to know — Ẹ̀dẹ̀ (polite talk among pupils — ìsọ̀rọ̀ńbẹ̀sọ̀rọ̀)**

• **"ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀ dáadáa ni"** — your word is good (a praise of what a friend said).
• Asking: **"Ẹ̀ jọ̀ọ́, fún mi ní penṣìlì."** — please give me a pencil.
• Answering: **"Ẹ́ ṣé o."** — thank you. · When you hurt somebody: **"Ì jọ̀ọ́ ẹ̀, ẹ̀ má bà mi nù."** — please, do not be angry with me.
• Greeting a friend: **"ọ́ rè káàbọ̀"** — welcome, my friend; and asking **"Ṣé àlàáfíà?"** — is it well? Answer: **"Àlàáfíà ni."**
• We do not use rude names; nicknames that pain are not "polite talk".
• Polite words end in the respectful **"ẹ̀"** when speaking to an elder — ẹ̀ jọ̀ọ́, ẹ́ ṣé, ẹ̀ máàdọ́rùn.

**Things to know — Àṣà (cleanliness of the person — ìmọ̀tọ̀́tọ̀ ara ẹni)**

• **Ìmọ̀tọ̀́tọ̀ jẹ́ ìlera** — cleanliness is health.
• Every day: **bìrìkì** (bath), **fífó ẹnu** (washing the mouth), **fífọ́ ọwọ́** (washing the hands).
• **Dídá nǹkan rẹ̀ mọ́** — keeping your own things clean: uniform, shoes, bag.
• Hair combed (ìdà), nails cut (àlàǹtẹ̀), clothes changed daily.
• Do not eat with dirty hands; wash after the toilet; do not share a toothbrush.
• A clean child is welcomed everywhere: **"Ọmọ tí ó mọ́, a fiṣọ́ ọwọ́."**

**Things to know — Lítíréṣọ̀ (song of good character — orin ìwà rere)**

• *"Ìwà pẹ́, ẹ̀ ṣe é;* — let character be long-lived, do it well;
• *"àṣìwà kò dára, ọmọ mi o."* — bad character is not good, my child.
• We sing it standing in a half-circle, one line each, and the class answers "ọmọ mi o".
• The song teaches three things: good character lasts, bad character spoils friendship, and a child should hear this daily.

**Words for my notebook**

Ẹ̀ jọ̀ọ́ · Ẹ́ ṣé · Ẹ̀ má bà mi nù · Àlàáfíà ni. Ìmọ̀tọ̀́tọ̀ jẹ́ ìlera.

**Let us talk**

1. How do you ask a friend for a rubber, politely?
2. What do you say when you hurt somebody?
3. Mention four daily acts of cleanliness.
4. What does "Ìmọ̀tọ̀́tọ̀ jẹ́ ìlera" mean?

**Worksheet**

1. "Ẹ́ ṣé" means A) please B) welcome C) thank you D) sorry
2. "Cleanliness is health" in Yoruba is A) ọmọ dé B) ìwà lẹ́wàá C) ìmọ̀tọ̀́tọ̀ jẹ́ ìlera D) àlàáfíà ni
3. We wash our hands before A) bathing B) playing C) eating D) sleeping only
4. To ask an elder politely we add A) "ẹ̀" B) "i" C) "o" D) "á"
5. Fill in: Fífó ẹnu means washing the ____.
6. Say four polite Yoruba words and what each is for.
7. Sing or say the song of good character.

**My own work**

Use three polite Yoruba words at home today, and write them down with what you said them for.

### WEEK 5 — Ede: Sise nnkan (oro ase) / Asa: Ojuse ebi ninu idile / Litireso: Orin keekeekee ati ijo

*Depth: school-generated — three strands as printed for N2 F1 W5.*

**You will learn to**

• use action words in Yoruba to say what we do
• state the duties of each member of the family
• sing a small song and dance to it

**Things to know — Ẹ̀dẹ̀ (action words — ọ̀rọ̀ àṣe)**

• An action word (verb) tells us **what somebody does**.
• **jẹ** (eat) · **mu** (drink) · ** lọ** (go) · **wá** (come) · **sùn** (sleep)
• **kà** (read) · **kọ** (write) · **ṣe** (do/make) · **fọ** (wash) · **ge** (cut)
• Sentence pattern: **"Mo + action + thing"** → *Mo jẹ ẹ̀fún* (I ate food); *Mo kà á* (I read it).
• With a name: **Bọ́la ń sọ nǹkan di mímọ́** — Bola is making a thing clean.
• Question form: **"Kí ni o ń ṣe?"** — what are you doing? Answer: **"Mo ń kà á."**
• Do not mix the two: **jẹ** is for food, **mu** is for drink.

**Things to know — Àṣà (family duties — ojúṣe ẹbí nínú ídílé)**

• **Bàba** (father): works, provides money, protects the home.
• **Ìyá** (mother): cooks, buys food, cares for the children, teaches the small ones.
• **Àgbà ará ilé** (elder sibling): looks after the little ones, carries messages, leads the smaller ones.
• **Ọmọdé** (the small child): goes to school, obeys, keeps his things, learns to help.
• Everybody has a duty; when each person does his part, the house is peaceful.
• **"Ilé kan ṣoṣo ni a ó gbé, iṣẹ́ pípín ni ọwọ́."** — the house is one, the work is shared.

**Things to know — Lítíréṣọ̀ (small song with a dance)**

• *"Wọ̀ o, wọ̀ o, ọmọdé wọ̀;* — come in, children, come in;
• *"iṣẹ́ ọwọ́ ni a fi ń jẹun."* — it is the work of the hands that feeds us.
• Steps: four steps to the right, four to the left, clap twice, turn once — done with the song twice.
• Actions used in the dance come from work: **fọ** (wash), **ge** (cut), **kọ́** (gather), **gbé** (carry).
• We dance on the quality, not on noise: quiet feet, straight back, smiling face.

**Words for my notebook**

jẹ · mu · lọ · wá · sùn · kà · kọ · ṣe · fọ · ge. Mo jẹ ẹ̀fún. — I ate food.

**Let us talk**

1. Say the Yoruba word for "eat" and for "drink".
2. "Kí ni o ń ṣe?" — answer it with an action you are doing.
3. Mention two duties of a mother and two of a child.
4. Show the four steps of the dance.

**Worksheet**

1. The Yoruba word "mu" means A) drink B) read C) sleep D) eat
2. "Mo ń kà á" means A) I am writing B) I am reading C) I am going D) I am eating
3. The one who provides money and protects the home is A) the neighbour B) the friend C) the father D) the baby
4. "Kí ni o ń ṣe?" is asked to find out A) what you are doing B) your name C) the day D) your age
5. Fill in: The Yoruba for "wash" is ____.
6. Mention the duty of each of these: father, mother, elder sibling.
7. Sing the small song and show two dance steps.

**My own work**

Write five Yoruba action words and use two of them in a sentence each.

### WEEK 6 — Ede: Kika alifabeeti A-GB / Asa: Ikini fun orisirisi igba ati asiko / Litireso: Orin ibawi fun omode

*Depth: school-generated — three strands as printed for N2 F1 W6.*

**You will learn to**

• read the Yoruba letters from a to gb
• greet at different times and on different occasions
• say a song of respect for children

**Things to know — Ẹ̀dẹ̀ (reading a–gb)**

• The first eight letters of the Yoruba alphabet, in order: **a, b, d, e, ẹ, f, g, gb**.
• Sound them, do not name them: **a as in àá (baba), b as in bé, d as in dé, e as in ẹnu, ẹ as in ẹ̀jẹ̀, f as in fẹ́, g as in gé, gb as in gbé**.
• Put them with a vowel: **ba, be, bẹ, da, de, dẹ, ga, ge, gba, gbe**.
• Read the words: **àbá** (cotton) · **ìbà** (yam house) · **ẹ̀dá** (creature) · **gẹ̀gẹ̀** (tall) · **gbà** (to accept).
• Trace the letters in the air with one finger, then on the board, then in your notebook.
• The dot on **ẹ** matters: **bẹ̀** (to press) and **be** are not the same sound.

**Things to know — Àṣà (greetings for times and occasions)**

• Time greetings: **Ẹ̀ káàárọ̀** (morning), **Ẹ̀ káàṣánṣá** (afternoon), **Ẹ̀ káàlẹ̀** (evening), **Ẹ̀ máàdọ́rùn** (good night).
• On returning: **Ọ́ dàbọ̀** — and the answer is **"Ọ́ dábọ̀, ààbọ̀."**
• At work: **Ọ́ nàṣẹ́** — and the reply **"A ṣe é, àṣẹ."**
• After eating: **Ẹ̀ jẹun ni**? — reply **Ìdílé ń jẹun**.
• At a festival or naming ceremony: **Odún rere** / **Ẹ̀ káàbọ̀** for the guest.
• For the sick: **Àlàáfíà ni**? and we bring water, not noise.

**Things to know — Lítíréṣọ̀ (song of respect — orin ìbàwọ̀n)**

• *"Ẹ̀ káàbọ̀, ẹ̀ káàbọ̀; àgbà ń wọlé o."* — welcome, welcome; the elder is entering.
• *"Ojú rẹ̀ pẹ́, ẹnu rẹ̀ títún;* — his eyes are old, his mouth is still good;
• *"ọmọ tí ó bá ń bọ̀wọ́, ni a ń fún ní ọwọ́."* — the child that respects is the one given honour.
• We stand when the elder stands, we sit when he sits, and we do not speak before him.
• The song is sung at the door of the class once, then we sit.

**Words for my notebook**

a b d e ẹ f g gb · Ẹ̀ káàárọ̀ · Ẹ̀ káàlẹ̀ · Ẹ̀ máàdọ́rùn · Ọ́ dàbọ̀.

**Let us talk**

1. Read the letters from a to gb.
2. What do you say to an elder when you are going to bed?
3. What is the reply to "Ọ̀ dábọ̀"?
4. Show me how you sit when an elder enters.

**Worksheet**

1. Which letter comes after e in the Yoruba alphabet? A) g B) d C) f D) ẹ
2. The Yoruba alphabet order begins A) b a d e B) a b d e C) a b c d D) a e i o u

4. The reply to "Ọ́ dàbọ̀" is A) bọ̀ B) àlàáfíà C) ẹ́ ṣé D) ààbọ̀
5. Fill in: "gb" is ____ letter, not two.

7. Sing two lines of the song of respect.

**My own work**

### WEEK 8 — Ede: Kiko alifabeeti / Asa: Imototo ayika / Litireso: Ere osupa sise ni ale

*Depth: school-generated — three strands as printed for N2 F1 W8.*

**You will learn to**

• write the Yoruba letters correctly
• keep the environment clean and say how
• make and play a moonlight game at night

**Things to know — Ẹ̀dẹ̀ (writing the letters — kíkọ́ alifábé)**

• Write on the line: letters sit on it; **b, d, f, g, gb, h, j, k, l** go up, **e, ẹ** stay short.
• Start from the top and go down, not sideways; go from left to right.
• Copy each letter ten times: **a a a … ẹ ẹ ẹ … gb gb gb**.
• Then write the vowels in one row: **a, e, ẹ, i, o, ọ, u**.
• Then write words: **àbà, ẹ̀dá, gẹ̀gẹ̀, gbà, ìbẹ̀**.
• Use a pencil that is not too blunt; do not press so hard that the paper tears.
• What is written is read back the next day: writing that cannot be read is not finished work.

**Things to know — Àṣà (cleanliness of the environment — ìmọ̀tọ̀́tọ̀ àyíká)**

• **Àyíká** is everything around us: the house, the compound, the street, the classroom.
• We keep it clean by: sweeping, putting dirt in the bin, cutting long grass, and keeping the gutter open.
• Dirty water and blocked gutters bring mosquitoes and sickness.
• We do not throw papers, peels or tins on the ground; "ibi tí a tí jẹun, ibẹ̀ ni a ó fọ́" — the place where we ate is the place we clean.
• The toilet is flushed and left as we found it; water is not wasted.
• The school keeps a clean-up day; the class that cleans is the class that is praised.

**Things to know — Lítíréṣọ̀ (moonlight play — ẹ̀rẹ̀ oòṣùpá ní àlẹ́)**

• When the moon is bright, children play outside — that is **ẹ̀rẹ̀ oòṣùpá**.
• Game: **Ìṣekúṣe** (hide and seek) — one child counts, the others hide; "Wá o, wá o, a ó rí i!"
• Game: **"Ẹ̀ ṣe é, ẹ̀ ṣe é, kí ni mo ṣe?"** — a ring game where one child is asked to do an action and the others copy it.
• The rhyme at the start: *"Oòṣùpá ń jẹ́ nǹkan dídùn;* — the moon is eating something sweet; *ọmọdé ń ṣe é, a ó rí wọ́n."*
• Safety at moonlight play: stay inside the compound, no sharp sticks, no going near the road, and come in when called.

**Words for my notebook**

à b d e ẹ f g gb — I write on the line. Ìmọ̀tọ̀́tọ̀ àyíká: sweep, bin the dirt, open the gutter.

**Let us talk**

4. Why is a blocked gutter dangerous?

### WEEK 9 — Ede: Itesiwaju kika alifabeeti H-Q / Asa: Ikini fun ise (ise abinibi) / Litireso: Ewi keekeekee

*Depth: school-generated — three strands as printed for N2 F1 W9.*

**You will learn to**

• read the letters from h up to p
• greet people at work and name occupations in Yoruba
• say a small poem with the right feeling

**Things to know — Ẹ̀dẹ̀ (reading on from h — ìtẹ̀síwájú kíkà)**

• The next letters: **h, i, j, k, l, m, n, ń, o, ọ, p**. (The school's row says H–Q; in the Yoruba alphabet the letters run h to p, for there is no c, q, v, x or z.)
• Sound each with a vowel: **ha, hi, ho · ja, ji, jo · ka, ki, ko · la, li, lo · ma, mi, mo · na, ni, no**.
• Read the words: **ìdí** (reason) · **ọ̀nà** (road) · **ilé** (house) · **omi** (water) · **ọkọ̀** (vehicle) · **inú** (inside).
• Blend trick: say the letter sound, then the vowel sound, then run them together — "k…o… kó".
• Read a full line from the chart without pointing, then read it again with pointing.
• Reading is done aloud, slowly, with the mouth open — not with the eyes only.

**Things to know — Àṣà (greeting at work and occupations — ìkíni fún iṣẹ́)**

• When you meet somebody working, say **"Ọ́ nàṣẹ́"**; the reply is **"A ṣe é, àṣẹ."**
• To a farmer at the farm: **"Ìṣẹ́ gígba ni"** — a harvesting work.
• Occupations in Yoruba: **agbẹ̀** (farmer) · **ọ̀dẹ̀** (hunter) · **akọ́** (weaver) · **oníṣẹ́ ogbó** (blacksmith) · **alakáwó** (baker) · **olùkọ́** (teacher) · **oníṣègùn** (doctor/traditional healer) · **ọkọ̀ ojú irin** (driver).
• The work of the hands is praised, never mocked: **"Ọwọ́ ọmọ ènìyàn ló fi ń ṣe àṣẹ."**
• Every work has its own tool: the farmer's hoe, the tailor's machine, the teacher's chalk.
• We greet a worker so that the work goes well and the person feels respected.

**Things to know — Lítíréṣọ̀ (small poem — ẹ̀wì kékeré)**

• A **ẹ̀wì** is spoken, not sung; it is said with the face and the hands.
• *"Iṣẹ́, iṣẹ́ ni ọ̀rẹ́ ẹni;* — Work, work is a person's friend;
• *" tí a kò ṣiṣẹ́, òkùnkùn ń ṣe é."* — if we do not work, darkness does us.
• Say it three times: slow, then with the hands, then with a loud voice on the last line.
• Meaning we can say: a child who works learns; one who is idle sits in the dark.
• The poem is our closing line every day this week, before we go home.

**Let us talk**
