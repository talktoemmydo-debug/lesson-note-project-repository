### WEEK 1 — TRADITIONAL RULERS IN OUR COMMUNITY

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Nigerian History · Third Term W1.*

**You will learn to**

• say who a traditional ruler is
• tell what a ruler does for the community
• say why we respect the throne
• name two things a ruler keeps safe for the people

**Things to know**

• A **traditional ruler** is the head of the people in his area, chosen from the ruling house by the elders.
• He is **not a man who was voted for** like the chairperson; his family has held the throne for a long time.
• A ruler **keeps the peace**: when two families are fighting, they are taken to him to be settled.
• He is the **custodian of our culture** — the festivals, the land, the names and the customs belong to his keeping.
• People **show respect** to the throne: they stand, they greet well, they do not shout in his presence.
• A ruler **gives titles** to good men and women who have helped the community.
• He speaks for the people to the government and brings news of the village to the town.
• The ruler's **staff, stool or horse** are the things that show the throne is there.
• Where there is no ruler in sight, the **family father** and the **compounds' elders** keep the same work of peace.
• We obey the ruler's good rules the way we obey our father and mother — because they keep us safe.

**Words for my notebook**

A traditional ruler is the head of the people in his area, kept from the ruling house by the elders. He
settles quarrels, keeps our festivals and customs safe, and gives titles to those who help the community.

**Let us talk**

1. Who is the head of the family at home?
2. What does a ruler do when two families are fighting?
3. How do people show respect to their ruler?
4. Name one thing the ruler keeps safe for the people.

### WEEK 2 — EMIR, OBA, IGWE: DIFFERENT RULERS IN NIGERIA

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Nigerian History · Third Term W2.*

**You will learn to**

• name the ruler of the North, the West and the East
• tell one thing each ruler is known by
• say that Nigeria has many thrones, not one
• point to where each ruler's people live

**Things to know**

• In the **North**, the traditional ruler is called an **Emir**; he is known by his **turban** and his **horse**.
• In the **Southwest**, the ruler is called an **Oba**; he is known by his **long coral beads** and his **crown**.
• In the **Southeast**, the ruler is called an **Igwe** or an **Eze**; he is known by his **red cap** and his **staff**.
• Other people have rulers with other names: the **Obi**, the **Olu**, the **Amanyanabo**, the **Shehu**, the **Sultan**.
• Different names do not mean different value — **every throne is as important as the other**.
• A child can say at once which ruler is which, because of the **cap, the beads, the turban and the staff**.
• The ruler of a town may sit in a **palace** with a big yard in front, where people gather on festival days.
• Nigeria has **many kingdoms and many thrones**, and that is one of the things that make the country wide.
• We do not say "my ruler is better than yours"; we say **my ruler is the one my people keep**.
• When two rulers meet, they **greet each other with honour**, and their people learn peace from that day.

**Words for my notebook**

The Emir rules in the North with his turban and horse, the Oba in the Southwest with coral beads and a
crown, and the Igwe or Eze in the Southeast with a red cap and a staff. Nigeria has many thrones, and each
one is honoured by its own people.

**Let us talk**

1. What do we call the ruler in the North?
2. Which ruler is known by long coral beads?
3. Name one thing you can tell a ruler by, without hearing his name.
4. Why is it wrong to say "my ruler is better than yours"?

### WEEK 3 — TRADITIONAL TITLE HOLDERS

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Nigerian History · Third Term W3.*

**You will learn to**

• say what a chief is
• tell how a person is given a title
• name two ways a title holder helps
• say why a title is not a thing we buy

**Things to know**

• A **title holder** is a man or a woman given an **honourary name** by the ruler, to help govern the people.
• A **chief** is a title holder who **speaks for a group of families** and answers for them at the palace.
• Titles are given for **good work**: helping the community, telling the truth, giving time, keeping peace.
• A person may be **chosen by the people of his area** and then **confirmed by the ruler** with drum and blessing.
• A title is **not sold and not bought**; money cannot buy the honour of a good name.
• Some titles are **very old** and have been carried by the same family for many years.
• A title holder **wears what shows his title**: a cap, a bead, a staff, a horse or a special broom.
• He helps by **explaining the ruler's words** to the people and **carrying the people's words** to the ruler.
• A title holder who behaves badly **loses the respect of the people**, even when he keeps the name.
• We greet a titled person well, the way we greet an elder: with the **right hands**, not with a shout.

**Words for my notebook**

A title holder is a man or woman honoured by the ruler to help govern the people. Titles are given for
good work and cannot be bought, and a chief carries the words of the families to the palace and back.

**Let us talk**

3. Name two things a title holder may wear.

### WEEK 4 — BAALE, IYALOJA, OTUNBA: THE WORK OF TITLES

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Nigerian History · Third Term W4.*

**You will learn to**

• say what a Baale, an Iyaloja and an Otunba do
• tell how each one helps the community
• say why the ruler needs helpers
• name one way each title keeps the town working

**Things to know**

• The **Baale** is the **father of the compound or the village street**; he is the oldest and settles small quarrels.
• The **Iyaloja** is the **mother of the market**; she keeps the stalls in order and settles traders' disputes.
• The **Otunba** is a **chief who stands next in line to help the ruler**, and carries messages to the quarters.
• Titles work like the parts of a house: the **roof, the pillars and the floor** each hold something up.
• The ruler **cannot be everywhere at once**, so the title holders see to the streets, the market and the families.
• A title holder **calls meetings**, tells the people news, and **leads them in clean-up and safety work**.
• The Iyaloja knows **the price of yam and rice** and can tell when a seller is cheating a buyer.
• The Baale knows **where every family lives** and who is sick, who is new and who needs help.
• Title holders are to be **respected as institutions**, not as rich persons: the honour belongs to the chair they sit on.
• When the ruler and his chiefs **work together**, roads are watched, markets are open and festivals go well.

**Words for my notebook**

The Baale is the father of the street, the Iyaloja is the mother of the market, and the Otunba helps the
ruler with messages and the quarters. The ruler cannot be everywhere, so titles hold the town up together.

**Let us talk**

1. Who settles small quarrels in the village street?
2. Who keeps order in the market?
3. Why does a ruler need title holders to help him?
4. What does the Iyaloja know about the price of food?

### WEEK 5 — THE LOCAL GOVERNMENT AND ITS CHAIRPERSON

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Nigerian History · Third Term W5.*

**You will learn to**

• say what a Local Government is
• name the head of a Local Government
• tell how a chairperson comes to office
• say why we count votes

**Things to know**

• Nigeria is divided big: **the country**, then **36 states and the FCT**, then **774 Local Governments**.
• A **Local Government** is the **nearest government to the people** — it is the one that touches your street.
• The head of a Local Government is called the **Executive Chairman**.
• A chairman is **chosen by voting**: the people mark the man or woman they want, and **the most votes wins**.
• Voting is how we settle who leads **without fighting** — the box of votes takes the place of the machete.
• To vote, one must be **eighteen years or more**; a child's work is to **behave well at the election**, not to queue.
• The chairman works with **councillors**, each one sent by a ward to speak for that ward.
• The **palace and the council are different**: the ruler sits on the throne, the chairman sits on an elected chair.
• A chairman's time is **limited** — four years, then the people choose again; a throne is for life.
• Our own things come from the Local Government: **the primary school, the health centre, the street drains, the market stalls**.

**Words for my notebook**

A Local Government is the nearest government to the people, and its head is the Executive Chairman, chosen
by voting. The most votes wins, so we settle who leads without fighting, and a chairman's time is limited
to a few years.

**Let us talk**

### WEEK 6 — WHAT A GOOD CHAIRMAN DOES

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Nigerian History · Third Term W6.*

**You will learn to**

• name four services a chairman provides
• tell the difference between a ruler and a chairman
• say what makes a leader good
• tell how a ruler and a chairman work together

**Things to know**

• A chairman **builds and mends the roads**, so that our yam and palm oil can reach the town.
• He **sees to the primary schools** — rooms, benches, books and the teacher's pay.
• He keeps the **health centre** working, with a nurse, drugs and a place for a mother to rest.
• He clears **drains and gutters** before the rains, so that water will not enter our rooms.
• He opens **the market, the borehole and the street lamps**, and lights them at night.
• A **ruler** keeps the customs and settles the people at the palace; a **chairman** keeps the services and signs the papers.
• A ruler is **on the throne for life**; a chairman is **in his office for a fixed time**.
• A **good leader is honest**, listens before he decides, shares work fairly and does not take what is not his.
• When the ruler and the chairman **sit together**, the town gets both wisdom and money.
• If a chairman does not do the work, the people **do not vote for him again** at the end of his time.

**Words for my notebook**

A chairman mends roads, keeps the school and the health centre working, clears drains before the rain and
opens the market. A ruler keeps our customs and sits for life; a chairman keeps the services and stays a
fixed time, and both work together for the town.

### WEEK 8 — WHAT MAKES A GOOD LEADER

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Nigerian History · Third Term W8.*

**You will learn to**

• name five qualities of a good leader
• tell why a leader must listen
• say what fairness looks like in my class
• show responsibility with one small job

**Things to know**

• A **leader** is anybody who **goes in front** — a ruler, a chairman, a monitor, a mother, the one holding the rope.
• The first quality is **honesty**: a leader tells the truth even when the truth will bring him trouble.
• **Truthfulness** means he does not say one thing to the ruler and another thing to the people.
• **Kindness** is a leader's second hand: he corrects without insulting, and helps before he shouts.
• **Fairness** means the same rule for every child, not a sweet for the one he likes best.
• A good leader **listens to both sides** before he decides; a leader who only talks will decide wrongly.
• **Responsibility**: if he is given the key, the key is not lost; if he is given the chalk, the chalk is finished well.
• **Hard work**: a leader is the first to arrive and the last to drop the broom.
• A leader **admits his own mistake**; only a small man hides behind "it was not me".
• A leader **does not use his position to take** the class biscuits for his own brothers.

**Words for my notebook**

A good leader is honest and truthful, kind and fair, listens before he decides, keeps what he is given and
works hard. He owns his own mistake and never uses his position to take what belongs to all.

**Let us talk**

1. Who are the leaders you know besides the ruler and the chairman?
2. What does a fair teacher do with two children who quarrelled?
3. Show me how a leader listens before he decides.
4. What do we call a leader who takes the class things for his brothers?

### WEEK 9 — LEADERSHIP IN MY OWN DAY

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Nigerian History · Third Term W9.*

**You will learn to**

• say what a class monitor does
• tell how a leader leads by example
• name three ways I can lead today
• say how a leader shows respect to others

**Things to know**

• A **class monitor** is a child chosen to **keep order**, to carry a note to the office and to stand at the head of the line.
• A **prefect** does the same for the whole school; he is a leader **before he is a boss**.
• **Leading by example**: if I want the class to be quiet, **my own mouth must be quiet first**.
• If I want others to keep the room clean, **I pick my own paper up** without waiting to be called.
• A child can lead in small ways: **share a chalk**, **help a friend to read**, **line the class up**, **hold the gate**.
• A leader **says please and thank you**, because his position does not make him bigger than anybody.
• A leader **respects those who lead before him** — he greets the monitor, the teacher and the ruler's chief.
• Leadership skills are **learnt young**: the child who keeps one promise becomes the man who keeps many.
• In **role play**, one child sits on the stool as the Baale and another brings him the quarrel of two dolls; the class watches how the case is settled.
• A leader is **known by what he does when nobody is watching**, not by the cap on his head.

**Words for my notebook**

A class monitor keeps order and stands at the head of the line; a prefect does it for the whole school. A
leader leads by example — my own mouth quiet before I ask for quiet, my own paper picked up before I call
to others — and leadership is learnt young.

**Let us talk**

2. What must I do first before I ask the class to be quiet?
3. Name two ways you can lead your small brother or sister today.
4. What does a leader say when he wants somebody to pass him a book?
