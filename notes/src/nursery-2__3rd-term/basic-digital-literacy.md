### WEEK 1 — HEADPHONES, AND HOW WE CARE FOR A MACHINE

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Basic Digital Literacy · Third Term W1.*

**You will learn to**

• say what headphones are for
• wear headphones the right way
• name two machines that use headphones
• say three rules for caring for a computer

**Things to know**

• **Headphones** are two small sound cups on a band; they put sound **into the ears** and nowhere else.
• We use headphones to hear a story, a song or a lesson **without disturbing the rest of the class**.
• Put the band **over the top of the head**, then let the cups **sit on the ears**; never push them inside.
• **Sound kept low** is how a child hears a long time; loud sound makes the ear tired and can hurt it.
• One headphone **for one child** — we do not share the cup that sat on another child's ear.
• Headphones are used with a **computer**, a **phone**, a **radio** and a **tablet**.
• The **wire** is kept free from pulling, from the wheel of a chair and from being stepped on.
• We care for a machine by keeping **food and water away** from it and by **washing the hands** before use.
• A screen is wiped with a **soft dry cloth**, never with a wet one and never with a pencil tip.
• We **hold a keyboard with two hands** when we carry it, and we put the chair back under the table.

**Words for my notebook**

Headphones put sound into our ears and keep it out of the class. We wear them low and clean, we keep food
and water from the machine, and we wipe a screen only with a soft dry cloth.

**Let us talk**

1. What do we hear with on a computer?
2. Why is a low sound better than a loud one?
3. What must stay away from a keyboard and a screen?
4. Show me how you put the band of a headphone over your head.


![A girl sits at a table wearing big headphones with one hand near her ear, with a blank screen, a keyboard and a box on the table, and an open cup of water kept far away on another stool.](assets/img/nursery-2/3rd-term/basic-digital-literacy/week1-headphones-and-care.png)
**Worksheet**

1. Headphones put sound into our ____ . A) nose B) hair C) ears D) shoes
2. Which of these is used with headphones? A) a computer B) a broom C) a cutlass D) a pot
3. A screen is wiped with a ____ . A) wet rag B) rough sand C) pencil tip D) soft dry cloth
4. Before we touch a keyboard we wash our ____ . A) feet B) hands C) ears D) knees
5. Fill in: Loud sound makes the ear ____ and can hurt it.
6. Mention three things we keep away from a computer.
7. Write two sentences on how you wear a headphone the right way.

**My own work**

Draw a pair of headphones with a band and two round cups, then draw a child wearing them with one hand up to an ear.

### WEEK 2 — THE CPU: THE PART THAT THINKS

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Basic Digital Literacy · Third Term W2.*

**You will learn to**

• say what the CPU is
• tell what the CPU does for the whole machine
• name the box where the CPU lives
• say why we do not open the box

**Things to know**

• The **CPU** is the part of the computer that **does the thinking**; it is like the **head** of a person.
• When you press a key, the news runs to the **CPU**, and the CPU decides what the screen will show.
• CPU means **Central Processing Unit** — the words a big man uses; we call it **the thinking part**.
• The CPU lives inside the **big box** under the screen, called the **system unit** or the computer's body.
• The box has **small holes** for air; the CPU gets warm when it works hard, like a leg gets warm when it runs.
• We **do not open the box** and we do not put a finger or a pencil inside it; there is power in there.
• Inside the box the CPU sits on a **flat board** with its little brothers: **RAM** and the **hard drive**.
• Other machines think too: a **phone**, a **television**, a **radio** and a **pump** all have a small CPU.
• A computer with a tired CPU is like a child with no sleep — it works **slowly**.
• We keep the box **on a table**, not on the floor, so that dust and water stay away from it.

**Words for my notebook**

The CPU is the thinking part of a computer, like the head of a person. It lives in the big box under the
screen, it gets warm when it works, and we never open the box or push anything inside it.

**Let us talk**

1. Which part of you does your thinking?
2. What happens when you press a key on the keyboard?
3. Why must we keep our fingers out of the big box?
4. Name two other machines that have a thinking part.


![A boy stands beside a big plain box on a table, one finger raised, with a small square chip resting on top of the box and air lines at its side.](assets/img/nursery-2/3rd-term/basic-digital-literacy/week2-the-thinking-box.png)
**Worksheet**

1. The part of the computer that does the thinking is the ____ . A) shoe B) CPU C) bag D) chair
2. The CPU is like the ____ of a person. A) elbow B) toe C) finger D) head
3. The CPU lives inside the ____ . A) lunch box B) school bag C) big box under the screen D) shoe rack
4. The box has small holes so that the CPU can get ____ . A) air B) water C) sunlight D) sleep
5. Fill in: We must not ____ the big box of the computer.
6. Mention three machines that have a thinking part.
7. Write two sentences on why the CPU gets warm.

**My own work**

Draw a big box with a small square inside it and dots for the air holes, then draw a line from the box to a screen.

### WEEK 3 — RAM: THE MEMORY THAT HOLDS THINGS A LITTLE WHILE

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Basic Digital Literacy · Third Term W3.*

**You will learn to**

• say what RAM is for
• tell how RAM is different from the hard drive
• say what happens to RAM when power goes off
• name one thing we do before we lose our work

**Things to know**

• **RAM** is the computer's **short memory**; it holds what we are working on **just now**.
• RAM is like **carrying things in both hands**: it is fast, but the hands can hold only so much.
• When the machine is **switched off**, what was in RAM is **gone** — the hands become empty.
• The **hard drive** is different: it keeps things **until tomorrow**, like a box in the cupboard.
• If we write a name on the computer and never **save** it, the power failure will take it away.
• More RAM means the computer can hold **more work at the same time**, like two hands and not one.
• RAM lives **inside the big box**, flat on the same board as the CPU, in a long thin strip.
• A **power failure** is a common way to lose work, so we **save as we go**, not only at the end.
• Little things in RAM are **fast**; big things kept in the hard drive are **slower to bring out**.
• A machine with too little RAM **pauses**; a patient child waits and does not hit the keyboard.

**Words for my notebook**

RAM is the computer's short memory, like work held in two hands; it is emptied when power goes off. The
hard drive keeps things until tomorrow, so we save as we go and do not wait for the end.

**Let us talk**

1. What holds your work when the machine is still on?
2. What happens to that work when power goes off?
3. Where does the machine keep a thing until tomorrow?
4. Why do we save before we finish?


![A girl carries three blocks in both hands while four more blocks sit inside an open box with its lid leaning against it.](assets/img/nursery-2/3rd-term/basic-digital-literacy/week3-held-in-two-hands.png)
**Worksheet**

1. The computer's short memory is called ____ . A) the bag B) the box C) the screen D) RAM
2. RAM holds the work we are doing ____ . A) just now B) last year C) next week D) yesterday
3. When the machine is switched off, RAM becomes ____ . A) heavier B) bigger C) empty D) warm
4. The part that keeps our work until tomorrow is the ____ . A) cup B) pencil C) keyboard D) hard drive
5. Fill in: We save our work because a ____ may take it away.
6. Mention two things that make a computer pause or work slowly.
7. Write two sentences on what you do so that your work is not lost.

**My own work**

Draw two hands carrying three blocks each, then draw an empty box beside them with the blocks inside it.

### WEEK 4 — THE HARD DRIVE: THE BOX THAT KEEPS OUR WORK

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Basic Digital Literacy · Third Term W4.*

**You will learn to**

• say what the hard drive keeps
• tell where our picture and our song are kept
• name one thing that hurts a hard drive
• say the word for putting work back into the machine

**Things to know**

• The **hard drive** is the computer's **keeping box**; what goes in there stays until we take it out.
• Our **drawing**, our **song** and the **story we wrote** live in the hard drive.
• **Save** means to put work into the keeping box; **open** means to bring it out again.
• A hard drive can hold **very many** things — a thousand pictures and more — yet it is small.
• Some keeping boxes live **outside** the machine on a **small stick** called a flash drive.
• A **flash drive** is small enough for a pocket and can be carried to another machine.
• A hard drive does not like a **knock** or a **shake** while it is working, so a running machine stays still.
• If the keeping box is **full**, we throw away old songs and pictures the way we clear a shelf.
• The hard drive sits **inside the big box** on the same board as the CPU and RAM.
• We do not pull the plug out of a machine that is still saving, or the work may be lost.

**Words for my notebook**

The hard drive is the keeping box of the computer: our picture, our song and our story live there until we
open them again. A flash drive is a small keeping box that can go into a pocket and to another machine.

**Let us talk**

1. Where does the computer keep your drawing until tomorrow?
2. What do we call putting your work into the keeping box?
3. What is a flash drive like in your house?
4. Why must a machine that is working stay still?


![A boy kneels by an open box and puts a card inside, with a house picture, a disc and a folded paper stored there, and a small drive beside it.](assets/img/nursery-2/3rd-term/basic-digital-literacy/week4-the-keeping-box.png)
**Worksheet**

1. The keeping box of the computer is the ____ . A) hard drive B) school cap C) door D) spoon
2. To put work into the keeping box we say ____ . A) open B) break C) save D) sing
3. A small keeping box we can carry in a pocket is a ____ . A) plate B) cup C) pillow D) flash drive
4. A hard drive does not like ____ while it is working. A) sleep B) a knock or a shake C) a fan D) a cover
5. Fill in: If the keeping box is full, we ____ away old pictures.
6. Mention three things we may keep in a hard drive.
7. Write two sentences on the last thing you saved on a machine.

**My own work**

Draw a box with a picture, a note and a song card inside it, then draw a small stick with its own lid beside it.

### WEEK 5 — THE MOTHERBOARD: THE FLOOR EVERY PART SITS ON

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Basic Digital Literacy · Third Term W5.*

**You will learn to**

• say what a motherboard is
• name three parts that sit on it
• tell how parts pass news to one another
• say why water is the enemy of a board

**Things to know**

• The **motherboard** is the **big flat board** inside the box that **joins all the parts together**.
• It is like the **floor of a house**: the CPU, the RAM and the hard drive all sit on it and are connected.
• Thin **lines** run over the board like **roads**, and news travels along them from one part to another.
• A **port** is a little door on the board where a wire from the keyboard or the mouse is put in.
• The **cable** is the wire that carries news and power from one thing to another.
• If one part is loose, the news cannot pass, and the machine **refuses to work** — like a road with a hole.
• **Water** is the enemy of a board: it leaves spots that break the little roads.
• A **spilled cup** on a laptop can finish the whole machine, so drink sits on the other table.
• We never touch the board of an opened machine; the power inside can **give a child a shock**.
• Small **metal pins** hold parts to the board; they are for a grown-up's tool, not for a finger.

**Words for my notebook**

The motherboard is the big flat board inside the computer's box. The CPU, the RAM and the hard drive sit on
it, and news passes between them along thin lines like roads, which is why water must never touch a board.

**Let us talk**

1. What is the board inside the box called?
2. Which three parts sit on that board?
3. What do we call the little door where a wire goes in?
4. Why must a cup of water stay away from a machine?


![A girl crouches beside a big flat board with four small boxes on it joined by straight lines, and little square doors on its edge.](assets/img/nursery-2/3rd-term/basic-digital-literacy/week5-the-joining-board.png)
**Worksheet**

1. The big flat board that joins the parts is the ____ . A) table cloth B) wall C) motherboard D) curtain
2. Which of these sits on the motherboard? A) the CPU B) a shoe C) a plate D) a ball
3. The little door on the board where a wire is put in is a ____ . A) port B) gate C) pocket D) lid
4. News travels between parts along thin lines called ____ . A) rivers B) roads like lines C) ropes D) belts
5. Fill in: The enemy of a board is ____ .
6. Mention three parts that sit on the motherboard.
7. Write two sentences on what a spilled cup can do to a machine.

**My own work**

Draw a big flat square board, put three small boxes on it and join them with straight lines, then draw a circle for a port.

### WEEK 6 — POWER SUPPLY: THE PART THAT BRINGS THE MACHINE TO LIFE

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Basic Digital Literacy · Third Term W6.*

**You will learn to**

• say what a power supply does
• name where the power for a machine comes from
• tell how a machine keeps working when light goes off
• say two safety rules about electricity

**Things to know**

• The **power supply** is the part inside the box that **takes electricity in** and gives it to the other parts.
• Without power the CPU cannot think, the screen cannot shine and the fan cannot turn.
• Power for a machine comes from the **socket in the wall**, from an **inverter**, a **generator** or a **battery**.
• A **plug** goes into the socket; the **cable** carries the power along to the machine.
• A **power bank** or **battery** keeps a phone or a small lamp working when NEPA takes the light off.
• A machine uses a **button** to be switched on and off — we press it **once**, gently, with a finger.
• We do **not** pull the cable to switch a machine off; we press the button and then remove the plug.
• **Electricity can kill**: never put a finger, a pin or water into a socket, and never touch a cut cable.
• A **surge** from lightning can spoil a machine, so during a storm we take the plug out of the wall.
• The power supply has a **fan** too, because the work of carrying power makes it **warm**.

**Words for my notebook**

The power supply takes electricity from the wall and gives it to the parts of the machine. We press the
button to switch on and off, never pull the cable, and keep fingers, pins and water far from a socket.

**Let us talk**

1. Where does a computer get its power from?
2. What part gives power to the other parts?
3. What do we do with the plug during a storm?
4. Name one thing that must never go into a socket.


![A plug sits in a wall socket and a cable runs across the floor to a box on a table, while a boy presses one finger on a single round button.](assets/img/nursery-2/3rd-term/basic-digital-literacy/week6-power-from-the-wall.png)
**Worksheet**

1. The part that brings power into the machine is the ____ . A) shoe box B) power supply C) lunch cup D) broom
2. A plug goes into the ____ in the wall. A) socket B) window C) shelf D) curtain
3. Which of these can keep a small lamp working when light goes off? A) a stone B) a leaf C) a battery D) a cup
4. To switch a machine off we ____ . A) pull the cable hard B) press the button C) pour water on it D) hit the screen
5. Fill in: During a storm we take the ____ out of the wall.
6. Mention three places power for a machine can come from.
7. Write two sentences on the safety rules for a socket.

**My own work**

### WEEK 8 — COOLING: HOW A MACHINE KEEPS FROM SWEATING

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Basic Digital Literacy · Third Term W8.*

**You will learn to**

• say why a machine needs cooling
• point to the places air goes in and out
• tell where a machine must stand to stay cool
• name one thing that stops a fan from turning

**Things to know**

• A machine **works hard**, and hard work makes **heat** — the same way running makes your legs warm.
• **Too much heat** will spoil the CPU, so the machine has a **cooling fan** inside it.
• A **fan** is a small spinning blade, the same idea as the **electric fan** in your class.
• Air goes **in at one side** of the box, passes over the hot parts and goes **out at the other side**.
• The **holes** you see on the box are the machine's nose; if we **cover them**, the machine gets too hot.
• A machine must stand on a **hard flat table**, not on a **soft mattress** or a cloth that shuts the holes.
• **Dust** and **hair** can choke a fan; a clean machine is a cool machine.
• A laptop on a **bed** can become hot because the bed closes the air way underneath it.
• Some big machines have **air like a room cooler**; a phone just rests and cools itself.
• If a machine becomes **too hot**, it **switches itself off** to rest — let it cool before you press again.

**Words for my notebook**

Hard work makes a machine hot, so it keeps a small fan and holes at the sides for air. We set the machine
on a hard flat table, leave the holes free, and let a hot machine rest before we switch it on again.

**Let us talk**

1. What makes your legs warm at playtime?
2. What part inside the box blows the heat away?
3. Why must we not cover the holes on the box?
4. Where is a bad place to keep a laptop, and why?


![A box stands on a hard table with rows of holes on one side and a four-bladed fan on the other, arrows showing the air going through, and a folded cloth left on the floor below.](assets/img/nursery-2/3rd-term/basic-digital-literacy/week8-air-in-and-out.png)
**Worksheet**

1. A machine gets hot because it ____ . A) works hard B) sleeps long C) sings D) waits
2. The part that blows heat away is a ____ . A) spoon B) fork C) cooling fan D) plate
3. Air goes in and out of the box through the ____ . A) holes at the side B) screen C) keyboard D) mouse
4. A bad place to put a machine is on a ____ . A) wooden table B) hard shelf C) stone slab D) soft bed
5. Fill in: If a machine becomes too hot, it ____ itself off.
6. Mention two things that can choke a fan.
7. Write two sentences on where you would keep a machine so it stays cool.

**My own work**

### WEEK 9 — HOW THE PARTS WORK TOGETHER

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Basic Digital Literacy · Third Term W9.*

**You will learn to**

• tell the story of a letter from the keyboard to the paper
• name an input device and an output device
• say what hardware and software mean
• put three steps of computer work in order

**Things to know**

• **Input** means things that send news **into** the machine: a **keyboard**, a **mouse**, a
  **microphone**, a **scanner** and a **webcam**.
• **Output** means things that bring news **out**: a **screen**, a **printer**, a **speaker** and **headphones**.
• The story of one letter: you **press a key**, the news runs to the **CPU**, the CPU shows it on the
  **screen**, and it is kept in the **hard drive**.
• To get the letter on paper, the machine sends it out at the **printer**.
• **Hardware** is every part we can **see and touch** — the box, the board, the keyboard, the mouse.
• **Software** is the work inside we **cannot touch** — the writing program, the picture program, the game.
• A machine with hardware but no software is like a **mouth with nothing to say**.
• Order of work: **put the news in**, **let the machine think**, **bring the news out**, **keep it**.
• All the parts need the **motherboard** to sit on and the **power supply** to feed them.
• The parts are not one big boss; they are like a **class at work** — one writes, one reads out, one keeps the book.

**Words for my notebook**

Input things send news into the machine, output things bring news out, and the CPU thinks between the two.
Hardware is what we touch, software is what we cannot touch, and every part sits on the motherboard and
eats from the power supply.

**Let us talk**

1. Which part sends news into the machine when you want to write a name?
2. Which part brings the name out on paper?
3. Say two things that are hardware and two that are software.
4. Put this in order: think, print, type, keep.


![A keyboard, a tall box, a blank screen and a printer with a sheet of paper stand in a row joined by straight lines, while a boy presses one key.](assets/img/nursery-2/3rd-term/basic-digital-literacy/week9-parts-in-a-row.png)
**Worksheet**

1. A keyboard and a mouse send news ____ the machine. A) into B) under C) behind D) above
2. Which of these brings news out of the machine? A) the CPU B) the keyboard C) the screen D) the mouse
3. The parts we can see and touch are the ____ . A) soft words B) power C) air D) hardware
4. The work inside the machine we cannot touch is ____ . A) software B) the box C) the cable D) the stand
5. Fill in: First we type, then the ____ thinks, then the screen shows.
6. Mention two input devices and two output devices.
7. Write two sentences on what happens to a letter you press on the keyboard.

**My own work**

Draw a keyboard, a box, a screen and a printer in a row, then join them with three straight lines.
