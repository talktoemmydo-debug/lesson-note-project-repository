### WEEK 1 — THE PLACE WE LIVE, AND THE WEATHER THAT CHANGES

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Social and Citizenship Studies · Third Term W1.*

**Main content**

• A **bin with a cover** is where paper, peels and wrappers go; the ground is not a bin.
• A **wrapper or a tin left on the ground** is called litter; it makes the place ugly and it can cut a foot.
• When the gutter is choked with litter, rain water has no way out and the **water comes into the compound**.
• A broken fence, a deep hole or a pool of dirty water is **told to a grown-up** so that it is mended.
• Weather is what the sky does today: **sun, rain, wind, hot and cold**.
• **Climate** is the weather a place has over many years — we know rain will come in its season.
• When rain, heat and wind change from what they used to be, we call it **climate change**.
• Too much heat dries the soil and the young plants die; too much rain brings a **flood**; a long time
  without rain is a **drought**.
• Our own part is small but real: **plant a tree**, shut the tap when water is not wanted, keep our place clean.

**Words for my notebook**

We keep our place clean by putting waste in a covered bin and never on the ground, and a broken or dangerous
thing is told to a grown-up. Weather is what the sky does today — sun, rain, wind, hot or cold — and when it
changes for many years we call it climate change; a tree planted and water saved both help.

**Let us talk**

1. Where do paper, peels and wrappers go?
2. What happens when a gutter is choked with litter?
3. Name three kinds of weather you know.
4. What are two things you can do when the heat grows too much?

### WEEK 2 — THE ANIMALS THAT LIVE BY THEMSELVES

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Social and Citizenship Studies · Third Term W2.*

**Main content**

• **Wildlife** are the animals and birds that live in nature by themselves, in the bush, by the river and on
  the grassland.
• Animals we find in Nigeria are the **monkey, antelope, bushbird, lizard, snake and butterfly**.
• A monkey lives in the **trees of the forest**; an antelope feeds on the **grassland**; a snake keeps to the
  long grass and the holes in the ground.
• Fish and frogs live in or beside the **river**, and a butterfly goes from flower to flower for the drink in
  the flower.
• Animals are **part of nature**: the bird eats the insect that would spoil our farm, and the bee helps a
  flower become a fruit.
• Some animals are **nearly gone** — that is called endangered — because their homes are cut down or they are
  killed for food.
• **Conservation** means keeping nature and its animals safe, so that a child yet to come will still see a
  monkey in a tree.
• We do not **hurt them or pull down their homes**: no burning the bush, no cutting every tree, no stone
  thrown at a bird.
• A **zoo** is a place where animals are kept and fed so that people may learn about them; there we look
  quietly and we do not throw anything in.

**Words for my notebook**

Wildlife are animals that live in nature by themselves — a monkey in the tree, an antelope on the grassland,
a fish in the river. Conservation means keeping them and their homes safe: we do not burn the bush, cut
every tree or stone a bird.

**Let us talk**

1. What does wildlife mean?
2. Where does a monkey live, and where does an antelope feed?
3. Name two animals you have seen with your own eyes.
4. Why should we not burn the bush?

### WEEK 3 — THE MONEY WE USE IN NIGERIA

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Social and Citizenship Studies · Third Term W3.*

**Main content**

• **Money** is the thing we use to buy what we need: food, cloth, a pencil and a bus ride.
• The money of **Nigeria is the naira**, and its mark is the letter **N with two strokes** through it.
• Round money made of metal is a **coin**; the ₦1 and the ₦2 coins are still carried in a pocket.
• Money made of paper is a **note**: ₦5, ₦10, ₦20, ₦50, ₦100, ₦200, ₦500 and ₦1000.
• A note carries a **number, a face and a shining strip**, and one note is not the same colour as another.
• Money has **value**: a ₦100 note will buy more than a ₦5 note buys.
• Money is **got by work** — the grown-up who works is paid money for the work done.
• A note is not folded into a plane and not torn: it is kept **clean, flat and shut** in a purse or a box.

**Words for my notebook**

The money of Nigeria is the naira. Round metal money is a coin and paper money is a note; money is got by
work, it has value, and it is kept clean, flat and shut.

**Let us talk**

1. What do we use money for?

4. Why is a note not for folding into a plane?

### WEEK 4 — SPENDING WISELY AND SAVING A PART

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Social and Citizenship Studies · Third Term W4.*

**Main content**

• Money buys **food, cloth and shoes**; it pays for **school**, for the **bus**, and now and then for a toy.
• A **need** is a thing we must have — garri, oil, a uniform. A **want** is a thing that would be nice — a
  second toy.
• Buying what we need **first** is spending wisely; when the money is finished there is nothing left for food.
• **Saving** is keeping part of the money for later use — a piggy bank, a box with a slit, or the bank.
• Money saved little by little grows: **two coins today and two coins tomorrow** make a heavier box.
• Money that is not ours is **never taken** — not from a bag, a drawer, a table or a friend's pocket.
• If we find money on the floor we **give it to the person who lost it**, or take it to the class teacher.
• Being **honest with money** is worth more than any toy bought with money that is not ours.
• We **thank** the grown-up who buys for us and we do not cry for everything we see in the shop.
• In the market we **count what we are given** and say thank you; a buyer and a seller are both doing a work.

**Words for my notebook**

Money buys what we need first — food, cloth, school and the bus — and a part of what is left is saved in a
box or a bank. Money that is not ours is never taken, and we thank the grown-up who buys for us.

**Let us talk**

1. Name three things money buys for a family.
2. What is the difference between a need and a want?
3. Where can money kept for later be put?
4. What do you do if you find money on the floor?

### WEEK 5 — BEING A GOOD CITIZEN

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Social and Citizenship Studies · Third Term W5.*

**Main content**

• A **citizen** is a person who belongs to a country; every child born in **Nigeria is a citizen of Nigeria**.
• A good citizen is a **good person in the place where they live**: they greet, they keep the rules, they help.
• Our **rights** are these: to learn, to play, to be safe, to be treated fairly, and to be fed and cared for.
• Our **responsibilities** are the other half: to be kind, to help others, to keep the rules and to respect
  everybody.
• A right is mine and it is my friend's too, so I do not push to the front of the line.
• **Fairness** means a girl and a boy, a small child and a big one, a Yoruba child and a Hausa child all get
  the same turn.
• When a thing is wrong — a child being bullied, a tap left running — we **speak up** and tell a grown-up.
• We take care of the community: the bin is used, the bench is kept clean and the classroom is swept.
• Being **proud to be Nigerian** means knowing the colours of the flag, greeting in the tongue of our people
  and singing our song well.
• A good choice is a small thing done many times: sharing a pencil, saying sorry, holding a door.

**Words for my notebook**

A citizen is a person who belongs to a country, and we are all citizens of Nigeria. A good child has rights —
to learn, to play, to be safe, to be treated fairly — and duties too: to be kind, to help, to keep the rules
and to respect everybody.

**Let us talk**

2. Name three rights of a child.
3. What is the other half that comes with a right?
4. When is the right time to speak up?

### WEEK 6 — OUR COMMUNITY: THE TOWN AND THE VILLAGE

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Social and Citizenship Studies · Third Term W6.*

**Main content**

• A **community** is people living and working together in one place: the houses, the shops, the school, the
  market and the stream.
• Our community is **part of who we are** — where we are from is known by our tongue and by our food.
• A **town** is a busy place: many buildings, many cars, big markets and street lights.
• A **village** is a quiet place: farms around the houses, animals going about, and nature close by.
• The two are **different but connected** — the village sends food to the town, and the town has the big
  hospital, the good road and the larger school.
• When a flood comes, the town sends cloth and food to the village and the village sends baskets of yam
  back; **communities help one another**.
• The values of a good community are **sharing, helping and working together** — a meeting, a road swept by
  all, a neighbour taken to the clinic.
• Our own part: greet an elder, keep the path clear, help with a heavy load, and do not fight over a piece of
  land.
• No community is better than another; one is **busy** and one is **quiet**, and both are needed.

**Words for my notebook**

A community is people living and working together in one place. A town is busy with many buildings and a
village is quiet with farms near the houses, and both need each other: food comes from the village and the big
school and hospital are in the town.

**Let us talk**

3. What does a village send to the town?
4. Mention two ways people in a community help one another.

### WEEK 8 — THE LEADERS IN OUR COMMUNITY

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Social and Citizenship Studies · Third Term W8.*

**Main content**

• A **leader** is a person who guides people and **serves** them, not one who only shouts orders.
• The qualities of a good leader are these: **honest, kind, fair, brave, helpful and a good listener**.
• **Traditional leaders** in our land are the **Oba**, the **Emir**, a **chief** and a **village head**.
• **Religious leaders** teach people the way of their faith: a **pastor** or an **imam**.
• **Community leaders** keep order in a place: a **Baale**, a ward head or the **chairman** of the local
  government.
• **School leaders** are the **head teacher**, the **class captain** and the **prefects**, and **family
  leaders** are a father, a mother and a grandparent.
• We **respect and support** our leaders by listening to them, keeping their rules and greeting them well.
• Leaders serve the community by settling quarrels, calling the people to work on the road and looking after
  the school.
• A child can lead in small ways: a **line leader**, giving out the books, saying the prayer, or helping two
  friends make peace.
• A child's leadership skills are **speaking clearly, listening to others, being fair** and helping to settle
  a quarrel.

**Words for my notebook**

A leader guides people and serves them. We have traditional leaders like an Oba or an Emir, religious leaders
like a pastor or an imam, community leaders like a Baale or a chairman, school leaders like a head teacher and
a class captain, and family leaders at home.

**Let us talk**

2. Name two traditional leaders in our land.

4. What are two ways a child can lead in the class?

### WEEK 9 — THE BIG WORLD WE ARE ALL IN

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Social and Citizenship Studies · Third Term W9.*

**Main content**

• We learn about **people, places and how they live** — our family, our community, our country and the world.
• Learning about others helps us **know ourselves better** and stops us laughing at what is different.
• Nigeria has **many cultures and many tongues**: Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa, Tiv, Kanuri, Efik and about four
  hundred more.
• Our foods are many too: **amala and efo, garri, tuwo, pounded yam and pepper soup**, and each people has
  their own.
• A greeting is different as well — "ẹ káàárọ̀", "good morning", "sannu" and "eto" all say respect; say your
  own one well.
• Nigeria is one country in a **world of many countries**, and other children go to school, work at home and
  play, just as we do.
• We are all part of the world: the rain that falls here, the air we breathe and the sea are **shared** with
  people far away.
• We **value what is like ours and what is not**: a friend from another place may eat a different food and
  still be a friend.
• Asking a question and then looking for the answer — in a book, from an elder, by watching — is how we learn
  the world.

**Words for my notebook**

We learn about people and places so that we can live well with them. Nigeria has many cultures and many
tongues, and it is one country in a world of many countries where children go to school and play as we do.

**Let us talk**
