### WEEK 1 — JESUS CHRIST OUR SAVIOUR

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · CRS · Third Term (pp 576–578).*

**You will learn to**

• say what a saviour is
• tell why Jesus came into the world
• say what Jesus did for me on the cross
• tell the Easter story in my own words

**Things to know**

• A **saviour** is somebody who **saves** us or **rescues** us out of danger.
• A **sin** is the wrong thing we do — **fighting**, **lying**, **stealing**, refusing to obey.
• Everybody does wrong things sometimes; the Bible says **all have sinned** (Romans 3:23).
• Sin puts a **wall** between us and God, because God is holy and cannot be pleased with wrong.
• We cannot pull the wall down ourselves; **Jesus came to take it down for us**.
• Jesus **died on the cross** for us. He did no sin, yet He took the punishment that was ours.
• His death shows **how much God loves us**: God so loved the world that He gave His only Son (John 3:16).
• On the **third day** Jesus **rose from the dead** — the women came and the stone was rolled away.
• Jesus is **alive in heaven today**, near God the Father, and He still hears us when we speak.
• Because Jesus died and rose again, we can be called **God's children** and **live with God for ever**.
• To **accept** Jesus is to say: *You are my Saviour; I will follow You.*

**Words for my notebook**

A saviour is one who saves. We all do wrong, and sin puts a wall between us and God, so Jesus died on
the cross for us and rose again on the third day that we may be God's children.

**Let us talk**

1. What does the word saviour mean?
2. Name one wrong thing the Bible calls sin.
3. On which day did Jesus rise from the dead?
4. What do you say when you accept Jesus as your Saviour?

### WEEK 2 — JESUS CALLS US FRIENDS, BROTHERS AND SISTERS

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · CRS · Third Term (pp 576–578).*

**You will learn to**

• say what Jesus calls us
• tell who my brothers and sisters in God's family are
• show a friend what love looks like
• say how we prove that we are Jesus' friends

**Things to know**

• Jesus said, "**You are My friends**" (John 15:15) — He did not call those who follow Him strangers.
• A **friend** is one who stays near, tells the truth, shares and does not hurt you.
• Jesus is a friend who **never leaves**, even when other children go home.
• In God's family, everybody in the class is my **brother or my sister**.
• That is why we do not say "that child is not my relation, I will not help him".
• Jesus wants us to **love one another**; this is the sign by which people will know us.
• We show that we are Jesus' friends by **obeying** what He taught, not only by singing.
• A friend is known in what he **does**: sharing a bench, giving a drink, carrying a heavy book.
• Little children can be Jesus' friends too — He did not wait for us to grow tall.
• When I hurt my friend, I go back and say **"I am sorry"**, because friends do not keep anger.

**Words for my notebook**

Jesus calls us friends and brothers and sisters in God's family. We prove we are His friends by loving
and obeying one another, and by saying sorry when we hurt a friend.

**Let us talk**

1. What does Jesus call the ones who follow Him?
2. Who is your brother or sister in God's family?
3. What did you do for a friend today?
4. How do we show that we are Jesus' friends?

### WEEK 3 — JESUS COMMANDS US TO LOVE

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · CRS · Third Term (pp 576–578).*

**You will learn to**

• say the greatest commandment of the law
• tell who my neighbour is
• show love to God in three ways
• show love to people by what I do

**Things to know**

• The **greatest commandment**: **love the Lord your God with all your heart** (Matthew 22:37).
• The **second** is like it: **love your neighbour as yourself** (Matthew 22:39).
• Jesus said, "**Love one another as I have loved you**" (John 13:34).
• Love is the **most important** thing Jesus taught; all other teaching hangs on these two.
• We show love to God by **praying**, by **thanking** Him, by **worshipping** and by **obeying**.
• **Who is my neighbour?** Everybody around me — my family, my class, my teacher, the baby at home.
• My neighbour is also the **stranger** at the gate and the child who is **different** from me.
• Love is a **verb**: helping a friend up, sharing an orange, giving water to the old woman.
• Love does not **push**, does not **scold**, does not **take** what is not mine.
• A kind word is love; a kept promise is love; a divided biscuit is love.
• It is easy to love those who love me; Jesus asks me to love even the child who annoys me.

**Words for my notebook**

Jesus gave two great commands: love God with all my heart and love my neighbour as myself. My neighbour
is everybody around me, and love is known by what I do — helping, sharing and being kind.

**Let us talk**

1. Which is the greatest commandment of the law?
2. Who counts as your neighbour?
3. Name one way you showed love at home this week.
4. How do we show love to God without leaving this class?

### WEEK 4 — JESUS WANTS US TO OBEY GOD'S COMMANDMENTS

*Depth: NERDC Primary 2 · CRS · First Term (pp 699–701), and Exodus 20 in child's words.*

**You will learn to**

• say what a commandment is
• name five of God's rules for living
• tell why we obey God
• say who brought God's rules to the people

**Things to know**

• **God's rules for living are called commandments** — they are not punishments, they are guidance.
• God gave His rules through **Moses** on the mountain; they were written on **two tables of stone**.
• We keep them in our hearts: **love God only**, **do not make an image to worship**, **do not steal**, **do not lie**, **do not be jealous of what others have**, and **give honour to father and mother**.
• We obey God because **He loves us** and **knows what is best** for us.
• A child who obeys keeps himself out of a great deal of sorrow.
• **Jesus obeyed God perfectly** all His life, even when obeying was painful.
• Jesus did not obey only when He felt like it; that is what "perfectly" means.
• Obeying God **makes us happy** and **pleases Him**, like a good song pleases the one who hears it.
• We cannot obey in our own power: we **ask Jesus to help us** obey every morning.
• To **lie**, to **take what is not mine**, to **hit a smaller child** — each one breaks a rule we know.

**Words for my notebook**

Commandments are God's rules for living, given through Moses on two tables of stone. We obey because God
loves us and knows what is best, and Jesus obeyed God perfectly so that we may learn from Him.

**Let us talk**

1. What do we call God's rules for living?
2. Who brought the commandments to the people?
3. Which rule keeps my mouth true?
4. Why is it good to obey God?

### WEEK 5 — THE MEANING OF PRAYER

*Depth: NERDC Primary 2 · CRS · First Term W1–2, prayer (pp 699–701).*

**You will learn to**

• say what prayer is
• name four places I can pray
• tell who listens when I pray
• pray in two different ways

**Things to know**

• **Prayer is talking to God** — with my mouth, or only in my heart where nobody can hear.
• Prayer is like talking to a **father** or a **friend**: I say what I did, what I need and what I thank for.
• **God listens** when I pray; He is not far and He is not busy.
• **God loves** it when His little children speak to Him, and He does not mind small words.
• I can pray **anywhere**: at home, in class, in the church, on the playground, in the bus.
• I can pray **any time**: in the morning, in the afternoon, at night, and between two lessons.
• We pray **out loud** when we are together, and **silently** in our hearts when we are many.
• We also pray by **singing** to God, and by **reading** the prayers He gave us in the Bible.
• **Folding the hands** and **closing the eyes** are not what makes God hear; they help me to think on Him.
• Nobody has to be tall to pray: **a child's short prayer is heard** the same as the pastor's long one.
• Prayer is our **conversation** with God — He speaks to me in His word, I speak to Him in prayer.

**Words for my notebook**

Prayer is talking to God like talking to a father or a friend. I can pray anywhere and any time, out loud
or in my heart, and God listens to me.

**Let us talk**

1. What is prayer?
2. Where did you pray yesterday?
3. Who listens when you pray?
4. What is the difference between praying out loud and praying in your heart?

### WEEK 6 — WHEN TO PRAY

*Depth: NERDC Primary 2 · CRS · First Term W3–4, when to pray (pp 699–701).*

**You will learn to**

• name the special times we pray every day
• say what we ask God in the morning and at night
• thank God before a meal
• tell when else it is good to pray

**Things to know**

• There is **no wrong time** to pray; we can pray any time we want.
• In the **morning** we thank God for the night that kept us safe, and we ask for **protection** for the day.
• **Before a meal** we thank God for the food; that short prayer is called **saying grace**.
• At **night** we thank God for the day and ask for **peaceful sleep**.
• **Before school** we ask God for wisdom to understand and safety on the road.
• When we are **happy**, we pray and thank God; when we are **sad or afraid**, we ask for help and comfort.
• When somebody is **sick**, we pray for their healing; when a friend is hurting, we pray with them.
• **Before a class test** we ask God to open our minds, not to give us another child's work.
• The Bible says, "**Pray without ceasing**" (1 Thessalonians 5:17) — that means keep talking to God all day.
• A child who prays at these four times — **morning, food, school, bed** — is learning to keep God near.
• Praying **with the family** at home is good, and praying **alone** is good too.

**Words for my notebook**

We pray in the morning, before a meal, before school and at night. We also pray when we are happy, sad,
afraid, or when somebody is sick, for the Bible says we should pray without ceasing.

**Let us talk**

1. What do you thank God for in the morning?
2. What do we say before we eat?
3. Name one other time it is good to pray.
4. What does "pray without ceasing" mean for a child?

### WEEK 8 — HOW TO PRAY

*Depth: NERDC Primary 2 · CRS · First Term W1–2, how to pray (pp 699–701).*

**You will learn to**

• show three positions we can pray in
• tell why we fold the hands
• pray a short prayer with the class
• say what Jesus did when He prayed

**Things to know**

• We may pray **standing**, **sitting**, **kneeling** or simply with the **head bowed**.
• **Kneeling and bowing the head** are ways of showing God honour, not rules that make the prayer work.
• Jesus Himself **knelt** when He prayed in the garden (Luke 22:41).
• **Folded hands** keep the fingers from fidgeting; **closed eyes** keep the mind from wandering.
• When we pray **together**, one voice speaks and the rest say **"Amen"** at the end, meaning *so be it*.
• A prayer can be as **short** as "Thank You, God, for my mother", and as **long** as a whole song.
• We **thank** first, then we **ask** for what we need, then we **ask God to wash away** the wrong we did.
• It is right to **pray for somebody else**: my friend who is sick, my teacher, my father and mother.
• Prayer is not only words from the mouth; the **heart** must go with the mouth.
• After praying, I **listen** quietly for a moment, because God also speaks to me through His word.
• Do not **copy another child's prayer** in a loud voice; God is not hearing the class, He is hearing you.

**Words for my notebook**

We can pray standing, sitting or kneeling, with folded hands and bowed head. We thank God first, then
ask, then own our wrong, and we may pray for other people as well as for ourselves.

**Let us talk**

1. Show me a position you like to pray in.
2. Why do we fold our hands when we pray?
3. What do we say at the end of a prayer together?
4. Put your own short prayer in this order: thank, ask, own what you did wrong.

### WEEK 9 — PRAYING EVERY DAY

*Depth: NERDC Primary 2 · CRS · First Term W3–4, keeping on in prayer (pp 699–701).*

**You will learn to**

• keep a daily order of prayer
• use the pattern Jesus taught
• pray for four kinds of people
• tell how God speaks back to my prayer

**Things to know**

• A **daily order** keeps prayer from being forgotten: **morning**, **before food**, **before school**, **bed**.
• Jesus taught His friends a pattern: "**Our Father in heaven**, may Your name be honoured; give us our
  food today; forgive us as we forgive others; keep us from evil" (Matthew 6:9–13).
• That pattern has four parts: **praise God**, **ask for daily needs**, **own wrong and forgive**, **ask for keeping**.
• We pray for the **sick**, for our **parents and guardians**, for our **teachers and the class**, and for our
  **country** and its leaders.
• We also pray for the person who **annoys** us; that is the hardest and the best prayer a child can say.
• God speaks to us **through the Bible**, through a good thought, and through what a grown-up tells us.
• God replies in three kinds of way: **"yes"**, **"wait"**, and **"I have something better"**.
• A prayer is not better because it is **long**; the widow's two coins were smaller than the rich man's
  basket, yet they were noticed.
• Keeping a small **mark on a paper chart** each morning is a way to see the order grow — one tick a day.
• If I forget a time, I do not start all over in shame; I simply **begin again at the next time**.

**Words for my notebook**

I keep a daily order of prayer — morning, before food, before school and at bed — using the pattern Jesus
taught: praise God, ask for needs, own my wrong and forgive, and ask to be kept. God replies yes, wait,
or something better.

2. What are the four parts of the pattern Jesus taught?
3. Name somebody outside this class you will pray for tonight.
4. What do you do when God's reply to your prayer is "wait"?
