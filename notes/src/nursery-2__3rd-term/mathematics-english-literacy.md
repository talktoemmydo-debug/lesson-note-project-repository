### WEEK 1 — THE WORDS WE KNOW AND THE SENTENCES WE BUILD

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy · Third Term W1.*

**Main content**

• A **sight word** is a word we know at once, the way we know a face: **I, a, the, is, am, and, it, in, at,
  on, he, she, we, me, my, to, go, no, up, can**.
• More we add this term: **see, look, here, there, come, have, like, play, run, jump**.
• A sentence gives a whole talk: **"I can see the dog."** — five words, one full stop.
• Three things make a sentence neat: a **big letter** first, a **space** between every word, a **full stop**
  at the end.
• The word **the** is the one we meet most; say it without spelling it out.
• Read with your finger along the line, and do not jump to the next line before this one is finished.
• A picture and a sentence are friends: for a boy with a ball, we may write **"He can play."**
• The same words can make different sentences: **"She is here."** and **"Here is she."** — the first one is the
  way we say it.
• When we write, we sit well, hold the pencil lightly, and keep the line under the words.

**Words for my notebook**

The sight words of this term: I, a, the, is, am, and, it, in, at, on, he, she, we, me, my, to, go, no, up, can,
see, look, here, there, come, have, like, play, run, jump. A sentence begins big, has room between its words,
and ends with a full stop.

**Let us talk**

1. Which word do we meet most in a sentence?
2. Read the line "I can see the red ball." with your finger on it.
3. Name the three things that make a sentence neat.
4. What could we write under a picture of a girl with a book?

**Worksheet**

### WEEK 2 — FIVE-LETTER WORDS AND WORDS LONGER STILL

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy · Third Term W2.*

**Main content**

• **plant** has five letters: **p-l-a-n-t**; we say the five sounds and join them at once.
• Words can begin with **two consonants joined**: **pl**ant, **br**ush, **st**amp, **st**and, **dr**ink,
  **fr**ost — the two run together, they are not pulled apart.
• Two letters can also make **one sound** between them: **sh** in sheep, **th** in three and think, **ch** in
  chain, **wh** in while, **qu** in queen.
• Say them slowly, then fast: **p… l… a… n… t** — **plant**; **sh… ee… p** — **sheep**.
• Five-letter words we read this term: **plant, brush, stamp, stand, drink, frost, sheep, three, chain,
  queen, while, think, trend**.
• A longer word is only a short word with more sounds; nothing in it is frightening.
• Put the new word in a line you know: **"I put the plant in the sun."**
• Word hunt: when we see one of our words in a page, we touch it and say it out.
• Writing a long word: say it, count the sounds on your fingers, then write one letter for each sound.

**Words for my notebook**

Five-letter words: plant, brush, stamp, stand, drink, frost, sheep, three, chain, queen, while, think. Two
consonants at the start run together, and sh, th, ch, wh and qu are two letters that make one sound.

**Let us talk**

1. How many letters has "brush"? Count them on your fingers.
2. Which two letters make one sound in "sheep"?
3. Say the sounds of "plant" slowly, then say the word fast.
4. Make a sentence with the word "drink".

**Worksheet**

1. The word "stamp" has ____ letters. A) four B) five C) six D) three

3. The two letters at the start of "brush" say ____ . A) two long sounds apart B) no sound C) one joined sound D) a full stop
4. "Sheep" is spelled s-h-e-e-p: how many sounds joined make it? A) two B) three C) five D) six
5. Fill in: In "rain + bow = rainbow" the two parts join to make one ____.

### WEEK 3 — TWO WORDS JOINED: COMPOUND WORDS

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy · Third Term W3.*

**Main content**

• Two words can hold hands and become **one word**: this is a **compound word**.
• **sun + set = sunset** — the time the sun goes down; **rain + bow = rainbow** — the colour band after rain.
• **foot + ball = football** — the game the foot plays; **cup + cake = cupcake** — a small cake baked in a
  cup shape.
• Places are named this way: **bed + room = bedroom**, **class + room = classroom**,
  **play + ground = playground**, **note + book = notebook**.
• Position words too: **out + side = outside**, **in + side = inside**.
• The morning plate has a name made the same way: **break + fast = breakfast** — the meal that breaks the fast
  of the night.
• Say the parts first and the whole after: **sun — set — sunset**.
• The whole word often means more than its parts: a **playground** is not only a ground that plays.
• Some joined words keep two beats of the voice; clap the word: **bed-room** is two claps, **plant** is one.
• Make your own and test it on the class: **pan + cake = pancake**, **snail + shell = snailshell** — the
  class says whether the word is used or only made.

**Words for my notebook**

A compound word is two words joined into one: sun + set = sunset, rain + bow = rainbow, foot + ball =
football, bed + room = bedroom, note + book = notebook, break + fast = breakfast.

**Let us talk**

1. Which two words make "rainbow"?
2. What is a bedroom?
3. Clap "classroom" — how many claps?
4. Say a joined word you made yourself and what it would mean.


![Four small scenes: the sun half gone behind the hills, a rainbow over the clouds, a ball at a girl's shoes, and a bed with its lamp in a room.](assets/img/nursery-2/3rd-term/mathematics-english-literacy/week3-four-scenes-one-word.png)
**Worksheet**

1. "Sun + set" joined makes ____ . A) sunset B) set C) sun D) Sunday
2. Which two words make "notebook"? A) note + bake B) node + book C) note + book D) note + bookcase
3. A playground is a place we ____ . A) sleep in B) play in C) cook in D) wash in
4. "Breakfast" is the meal that ____ the fast of the night. A) doubles B) keeps C) breaks D) buys
5. Fill in: rain + bow makes one word called a ____ word.
6. Part these words into two: bedroom, cupcake, outside.
7. Write two sentences, each with one joined word in it.

**My own work**

Draw a sunset with a rainbow over it, a ball at the foot of a child, and a bed inside a room.

### WEEK 4 — READING A STORY AND SAYING WHAT YOU HEARD

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy · Third Term W4.*

**Main content**

• A story has three parts: the **beginning**, the **middle** and the **end**.
• The **characters** are the people or animals the story is about; the **setting** is where and when it
  happens.
• Our story for this week, read line by line: **"The hen goes out. She sees a corn. She eats the corn. The
  rain comes. The hen runs home."**
• Three questions will open nearly any story: **Who?** (the hen) **What?** (she found corn) **Where?** (by the
  fence, then home).
• Beginning — the hen goes out; middle — she sees and eats the corn; end — the rain comes and she runs home.
• Retell it in your own words: it is not the same as reading it; your line may be shorter or longer.
• Say what you liked and why: **"I like the part with the corn because the hen is happy."**
• Tie it to your own day: **"When the rain came, I ran home too."**
• Look for the words we learnt inside the story — **the, she, goes, sees, comes, runs, home, out**.
• A story you have read is a story you can draw: three boxes, three parts, in order.

**Words for my notebook**

A story has a beginning, a middle and an end. The characters are who it is about and the setting is where it
happens. Who? What? Where? are the three questions we ask.

**Let us talk**

1. Who is in our story, and what did she find?
2. Where was the hen when the rain came?
3. Which part of the story happened first?
4. What part did you like, and why?


![Our hen in three boxes: she walks out at the fence, she eats five grains on the ground, and she runs home with rain falling and a leaf above her.](assets/img/nursery-2/3rd-term/mathematics-english-literacy/week4-the-hen-in-three-boxes.png)
**Worksheet**

1. The people or animals a story is about are its ____ . A) setting B) characters C) letters D) pages
2. "Where and when a story happens" is its ____ . A) middle B) end C) setting D) title
3. In our hen story, what happened last? A) The hen runs home. B) She sees a corn. C) The hen goes out. D) She eats the corn.
4. The part of the story that comes first is the ____ . A) end B) middle C) beginning D) last line
5. Fill in: When we tell a story in our own words, we say it is a ____.
6. Answer in a whole line: what did the hen do when the rain came?
7. Write one sentence about a time the rain came while you were outside.

**My own work**

### WEEK 5 — WORDS THAT TELL US WHAT A THING IS LIKE

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy · Third Term W7 (Nursery floor).*

**Main content**

• A word that tells us what a thing is **like** is a **describing word**.
• Colour words: **red, blue, green, yellow, black, white, brown, pink, orange, purple**.
• Size words: **big, small, tall, short, long, fat, thin**.
• How a thing is: **good, bad, hot, cold, happy, sad, fast, slow**.
• The describing word usually comes **before** the thing: **"The big dog."** — **"A red ball."**
• Two things can share one word: a **tall** man and a **tall** tree; a **hot** pot and a **hot** day.
• Say the pair out loud and hear the difference: **"a big stone"** and **"a small stone"**.
• A line with one describing word is worth a line without: **"The dog runs."** and **"The fast dog runs."**
• Match the word to the picture: for a snail, **slow**; for a running child, **fast**.
• Do not pile them up: one good describing word is enough in a small sentence.

**Words for my notebook**

A describing word tells what a thing is like — its colour, its size, or how it is. It usually comes before the
thing: the big dog, a red ball, a cold drink.

**Let us talk**

1. What colour is your bag today?
2. Say one big thing and one small thing in this room.
3. Which word goes with a snail — fast or slow?
4. Make the line "The pot is hot" into a line with the word before the thing.

### WEEK 6 — ACTION WORDS: THE WORDS THAT MOVE

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy · Third Term W8 (Nursery floor).*

**Main content**

• A word that tells what somebody or something **does** is an **action word**.
• Our first list: **run, jump, walk, sit, stand, eat, drink, sleep, play, read, write, draw**.
• More to add to it: **sing, dance, swim, fly, hop, skip, clap, throw, catch, kick**.
• "I can run" — run is the action; "Birds fly" — fly is the action.
• Say an action word and **do** it: the class is the picture of the word.
• One picture, one word: children with hands up clapping — **clap**; a boy and a ball in the air —
  **throw** and **catch**.
• Action words change a little with who does them: **I run**, but **she runs**.
• Write a line about your own day: **"I jump at play. I read in class. I sleep at night."**
• Guessing game: one child acts, the class names the action word.
• A story without action words would not move at all; that is why we keep them in mind when we write.

**Words for my notebook**

An action word tells what somebody does: run, jump, walk, sit, stand, eat, drink, sleep, play, read, write,
draw, sing, dance, swim, fly, hop, skip, clap, throw, catch, kick.

**Let us talk**

1. What action word is this picture of two hands together?
2. Say a line that begins "I can" and ends with an action word.
3. Which word changes when we say "she" instead of "I"?
4. Act an action word and let the class guess it.

### WEEK 8 — WRITING A STORY OF OUR OWN

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy · Third Term W9.*

**Main content**

• A story we write ourselves can be **three lines long** — that is already a story.
• First line: **who** and **where**. Second line: **what happened**. Third line: **how it ended**.
• Use what you can write: the sight words, the action words, and the describing words.
• Our three pictures this week: a boy **finds** a ball; the boy **kicks** the ball over a fence; a girl
  **gives** the ball back to him.
• A story from those boxes: **"I have a ball. I kick the ball. It goes over the fence. Ada sees the ball. She
  gives it to me. We play."**
• Say the line in your head first, then write it; do not leave the space out between the words.
• Begin every new sentence with a big letter and end it with a full stop.
• If a word will not come, draw it in the box above the line and ask for its letters after.
• Give your story a name: **"The Ball"**, **"Over the Fence"**, **"My Friend Ada"**.
• When you read it out, point at each word and let the class look at your picture boxes.

**Words for my notebook**

My story has a beginning, a middle and an end. I use the words I know, I begin big, I leave room between
words, and I end each line with a full stop.

**Let us talk**

1. What is happening in the first box of our three pictures?
2. Which line of your story tells how it ended?
3. What do you do with a word you cannot write yet?
4. What name will you give your story?

### WEEK 9 — THE MARKS THAT HELP A READER

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy · Third Term W10.*

**Main content**

• A **big letter** (a capital) begins every sentence: **"The dog runs."**
• A name takes a big letter too: **Ada**, **Tunde**, **Bola**, **Musa**, and the name of our own town.
• A **full stop** closes a sentence and tells the reader to breathe: **"I can jump."**
• A **question mark** opens a asking line: **"What is it?"** — it stands where the full stop would.
• An **exclamation mark** is for a strong call or a surprise: **"Look out!"**, **"What a big ball!"**
• A sentence never carries two of these marks at once; it is either told, asked or cried.
• Room between words is part of writing: **"Ican seeit"** is hard, **"I can see it"** is read at once.
• Neat writing is slow writing done twice: once to say it, once to make the letters sit on the line.
• Read your own page back with your finger; a missing full stop is felt before it is seen.
• The marks are not ornaments — they are the traffic lights of the page: stop, ask, shout.

**Words for my notebook**

A sentence begins with a big letter and ends with a full stop. A question mark ends an asking line and an
exclamation mark ends a strong call. Names take a big letter, and every word keeps its own room.

**Let us talk**

1. Which mark tells the reader that the sentence is finished?
2. What would you put at the end of "Where is my bag"?
3. Say your own name and tell me which letter in it is big.
4. Read this line the untidy way and then the neat way: "Icanrun."
