### WEEK 1 — PLACES WE PLAY AND THINGS WE PLAY WITH

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · PHE · Third Term W1 (pp 558–563).*

**Main content**

• A **sport facility** is the **place or the building** where a game is played.
• A **sport equipment** is the **thing you hold or wear** to play the game.
• Places we know: the **playground** for running and football, the **court** for basketball and tennis, the
  **field** for the big matches, and the **pool** for swimming.
• Equipment we know: a **ball**, a **whistle**, a **stopwatch**, **bibs** to mark the two teams,
  **boots**, **shin guards**, **nets**, **goal posts**, **cones** to mark a line and **mats** to fall on.
• Facilities **make the game possible** and keep us from being hurt by a hole or a stone.
• Equipment **must be used well**: we do not kick a football at a window, and we do not sit on the mat stack.
• After play, things go **back to their box or their store**, and the monitor **counts them**.
• A thing that is **torn or broken is reported**, not hidden under the bench.
• Not every school has a pool or a gym; a good class **plays well with what it has** — a rope, four stones
  for a goal, and a flat ground.

**Words for my notebook**

A facility is the place we play in and an equipment is the thing we play with. We use both well, put every
thing back in the store after play, and report the one that is torn or broken.

**Let us talk**

1. Where does this school play football?
2. Name two things you hold when you play.
3. Why do we put the ball back in the box?
4. What do we do if a net is torn?

### WEEK 2 — FOOTBALL: KICK IT, KEEP IT, SCORE IT

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · PHE · Third Term W2 (pp 558–563).*

**Main content**

• Football is played with a **round ball** and **two teams**; the team that puts the ball in the goal wins.
• **Passing** is kicking the ball to a mate; the **inside of the foot** is the truest pass a child can make.
• **Dribbling** is running the ball along while you keep it **close to your feet** and look up to see your mates.
• **Shooting** is kicking at the goal — not the loudest kick, but the **steady** one.
• **No hand** for a player in the field; only the **goalkeeper** may use his hands, and only inside his own box.
• A goal is made of **two posts and a bar**, with a net behind to catch the ball.
• What a player wears: a **jersey**, **shorts**, **socks**, **boots** and **shin guards** inside the socks.
• The **referee** carries the **whistle**; one short blast means play, a long one means a foul was done.
• When we **lose**, we shake hands; when we **win**, we do not mock the other side — that is sportsmanship.
• A small field, a size-3 ball and two piles of stones for posts make a real match for our class.

**Words for my notebook**

In football we pass with the inside of the foot, dribble with the ball kept close, and shoot steadily at the
goal. Only the goalkeeper may touch the ball with his hands, and a good team shakes hands after the game.

**Let us talk**

1. Which part of the foot makes the truest pass?
2. Who alone may use his hands in the game?
3. What does the referee blow?
4. What do we do to the other team when we have beaten them?

### WEEK 3 — BASKETBALL: BOUNCE IT AND THROW IT IN

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · PHE · Third Term W3 (pp 558–563).*

**Main content**

• Basketball is played with a **big bouncy ball** and **two teams**; the ball is **thrown**, not kicked.
• The hoop is **raised on a board**, and into it the ball must go to give a point to the team.
• **Dribbling** is bouncing the ball with the **finger tips of one hand**, keeping it **low**, and looking up.
• The ball is **not carried** while the feet run: **bounce it or pass it** — that is the first rule a child learns.
• A **chest pass** is pushed from the chest with both hands, thumbs ending down.
• A **bounce pass** is thrown to the floor so the ball jumps up into your mate's hands.
• To shoot, we **balance, keep our eyes on the hoop, bring the elbow under the ball and follow through** with
  the wrist.
• A **foul** is pushing, holding or hitting the other child's arm; two free throws are given for it.
• In our class the hoop is **put low**, and a ring painted on the wall will do just as well.
• Basketball makes the legs springy, the hands quick and the eyes steady.

**Words for my notebook**

In basketball the ball is bounced with the finger tips, passed from the chest with two hands and thrown at a
raised hoop. We do not run while we hold the ball — we bounce it or we pass it.

**Let us talk**

1. Which part of the hand pushes the ball down when we dribble?
2. What do we do instead of running with the ball in our hands?
3. Where must the ball go to give our team a point?
4. What is a foul in this game?

### WEEK 4 — TABLE TENNIS: THE BALL THAT MUST NOT DROP

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · PHE · Third Term W4 (pp 558–563).*

**Main content**

• Table tennis is **like tennis on a table**, and that is how it got its name.
• It is played by **two children** or by **four**, two on each side of the net.
• The **bat** is held like a **handshake** — the flat part in the palm, the handle between the fingers.
• To **serve**, the ball is thrown up lightly and hit **underhand**, so that it bounces on **your own side
  first** and then on the other.
• During play the ball may **bounce only once** on a side; the second bounce is a point for the other pair.
• We hit **forehand** on the side the hand holds out, and **backhand** across the body.
• What the game needs: a **table with a net across it**, **two bats** and a **small light ball**.
• A table needs **no big ground**, so it can be played **in the rain or in the shade** — one of its kindnesses.
• The eye must stay on the ball the whole time; a child who watches the ball **cannot miss it for long**.
• The ball is **not blown at** to make it move, and the bat is **not raised above the table edge** to hit down
  at the other child's face.

**Words for my notebook**

Table tennis is tennis on a table. We hold the bat like a handshake, serve so the ball bounces once on our
side and once on theirs, and hit it back before it bounces twice.

**Let us talk**

1. What is the small net on the table for?
2. Show me how you hold a bat like a handshake.
3. How many times may the ball bounce on one side during play?
4. Why can this game be played when it is raining?

### WEEK 5 — CHESS: THE GAME PLAYED WITH THE HEAD

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · PHE · Third Term W5 (pp 558–563).*

**Main content**

• **Chess** is a **thinking game** for **two players**; nobody runs and nobody shouts.
• It is played on a **checkered board** of **light and dark squares**, and each player takes the **light
  corner on his right hand**.
• Each side begins with the same family of men: **a king, a queen, two castles, two jumpers and eight small
  men in front**.
• The **king** is the most important man on the board; the game is ended when he **cannot escape**.
• The **queen** is the mightiest: she goes as far as the board is long, straight or crooked.
• The **castle** goes straight, the **jumper** leaps over a man in an **L** shape, and the small **man** goes
  one square at a time.
• We learn chess for **thinking ahead**, for **patience** and for **sitting still to look well**.
• **Touching a man means playing it** — a child who has touched must move, so look before the hand goes out.
• If a man is moved by mistake, we **say nothing** and play it; the game is also a school for truth.
• Chess is played all over the world, in houses, in clubs and in big halls where grown-ups sit very quiet.

**Words for my notebook**

Chess is a thinking game for two on a board of light and dark squares. Each side keeps a king, a queen, two
castles, two jumpers and eight small men, and the game ends when the king cannot get away.

**Let us talk**

1. Does chess need running or thinking?
2. Which man on the board must be kept safe above all others?
3. What do the two colours of squares look like?
4. What must you do before your hand goes out to a man?

### WEEK 6 — VIDEO GAMES: FUN IN LIMITS

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · PHE · Third Term W6 (pp 558–563).*

**Main content**

• A **video game** is a game played on an **electronic thing**: a computer, a tablet, a phone or a box joined
  to the television.
• Video games are **indoor play** — the child sits, and the fingers work.
• Games a small child may play: a **card game** where the cards are matched, a **running game** where the
  figure jumps the holes, a **puzzle** of pictures, a **counting** or **word** game, and a football game on
  the screen.
• Some games are made for **big young men**, with fighting and bad language; **a child of five does not play
  those**, and if he sees them on a screen he says to a grown-up at home.
• A screen is **fun in a little amount**; it is **not** the first thing for the day.
• **Outside play comes first**: running, jumping, throwing, and helping at home — then a short time on the
  screen, if it is allowed.
• Eyes get tired and the head becomes stiff when we sit long; that is why the time is **limited**.
• We do **not** take a screen to bed, and we do not play while food is on the table.
• If our school or house has no machine, we are not less: a rope, a ball and two lines on the ground carry the
  same joy.

**Words for my notebook**

A video game is played on a computer, a tablet or a phone, and it is indoor fun kept in a small amount.
Outside play, lessons and sleep come first, and games made for big people are not for a child of five.

**Let us talk**

1. What do we call a game played on a tablet?
2. Which comes first, the screen or play outside?
3. Why is our time on a screen kept short?
4. What do you say when a big brother shows you a fighting game?

### WEEK 8 — MOVING OUR BODY PARTS

*Depth: NERDC Primary 1 · PHE · motor skills, moving our body parts (part 1).*

**Main content**

• The parts we name: **head, neck, shoulders, chest, back, waist, arms, hands, fingers, legs, feet and toes**.
• **Nodding** the head means yes; **shaking** it means no; both move the **neck**.
• **Shrugging** lifts the shoulders up to the ears and drops them — the way a man carries a heavy load.
• **Waving** moves the whole arm from the shoulder, with the open hand going left and right.
• **Stretching** the arms above the head makes the body long, like a plant reaching for the sun.
• Bending to touch the **toes** stretches the back and the legs; the knees stay straight, and we do not jerk.
• Turning the **waist** from side to side with the hands on the hips wakes up the middle of the body.
• Shaking out the **hands** after writing makes the fingers ready again.
• In a **body song** we do what the song says — head, shoulders, knees and toes — and we get faster each time.
• Every movement is done **slowly at first**, on a **clear space**, with **no child within reach** of your legs.

**Words for my notebook**

We name the parts of the body and move them one at a time: nodding the head, shrugging the shoulders,
waving the arms, stretching up and bending to the toes, all done slowly in a clear space.

**Let us talk**

1. Point to your waist and then to your neck.
2. What movement do we do when we want to say yes?
3. Why must nobody stand near you when you stretch your legs?
4. Sing the body song once with the actions.

### WEEK 9 — MOVING WELL IN EVERYDAY WORK

*Depth: NERDC Primary 1 · PHE · motor skills, daily activity movements.*

**Main content**

• To pick up a thing from the floor, **bend the knees** and keep the back near straight — do not fold the back.
• A thing is held **close to the body** when carrying; the further it is from you, the heavier it feels.
• Two small loads are **easier than one big one**: carry in two trips rather than fall with one.
• **Pushing** is better than pulling for a heavy box: push with the **legs and the shoulder**, not the back.
• When we **pull**, the hands come in low and the body leans **forward**, so we will not be dragged down.
• On the **stair** we go **one after another**, hold the rail, and do not look at the child below.
• Nothing is **thrown down the stair** and no child **slides the rail**.
• An **obstacle course** trains the body: walk the line, step over the low bar, crawl under the rope, jump the
  two marks, and run back to touch the last child's hand.
• In a **relay**, the stick or the bean bag is passed **in the zone**, not thrown from far.
• These same moves keep us safe at home — for a child who lifts well **spills less, drops less and aches less**.

**Words for my notebook**

We pick things up by bending the knees, hold the load close, push with the legs and lean forward when we
pull. On the stair we go one after another and hold the rail, and in a race we pass the bag in the zone.

**Let us talk**

1. Which part of you should do the work when you lift a bucket — the back or the legs?
2. Why is two small loads better than one big one?
3. Show me how you hold the rail on the stair.
4. Where do we pass the stick in a relay?
