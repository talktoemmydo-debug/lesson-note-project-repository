### WEEK 1 — THE PATTERNS WE ALREADY KNOW: BLENDS, DIGRAPHS, THREE-LETTER SOUNDS

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy (Language Domain) · Third Term W1.*

**Main content**

• A **blend** is two consonants that keep their own sounds and still run together: **bl**ue, **fl**ag,
  **cl**ap, **gr**een, **pl**ant, **tr**ip, **sk**ip, **st**amp, **sn**ail, **sw**im.
• A **digraph** is two letters that make **one** sound between them: **sh**op, **ch**air, **th**ree,
  **wh**en, **ph**one, and the **ck** at the end of clock and duck.
• A **trigraph** is three letters held to one sound: the **tch** in catch and match, the **air** in hair and
  chair, the **are** in share and square, the **ure** in pure.
• Say them slowly and then at once: **sh… irt** — **shirt**; **c… l… o… c… k** — **clock**;
  **h… air** — **hair**.
• Our three sight words for this week are **shirt**, **clock** and **hair**; each one hides a pattern we
  know — the **sh** in shirt, the **cl** and **ck** in clock, the **air** in hair.
• Read the lines with your finger on them: **"The child in the red shirt looks at the clock."** —
  **"She has long hair and a new brush."**
• Fluency is the same words read again, only smoother: once with the finger, once with the eyes.
• Word hunt in the class: a thing with **sh**, a thing with **ck**, a thing with **air** — say it and put it in
  the box on the board.
• Do not guess a word from its first letter; sound the whole of it and then say it.

**Words for my notebook**

A blend keeps both sounds: fl, gr, st, sn. A digraph is two letters, one sound: sh, ch, th, wh, ph, ck. A
trigraph is three letters, one sound: tch, air, are, ure. Our words this week: shirt, clock, hair.

**Let us talk**

1. Which sound do the two letters at the end of "clock" make?
2. Name a thing in this room that begins with a blend.
3. Say the three-letter sound in "hair" and find it in one more word.
4. Read the line about the red shirt, once slow and once smooth.

**Worksheet**

1. Two consonants that keep their own sounds and run together are called a ____ . A) digraph B) sight word C) blend D) full stop
2. The letters **sh** in "shirt" make ____ . A) one sound B) two sounds apart C) no sound D) a long line
3. The three-letter sound in "catch" is ____ . A) cat B) tch C) atc D) ach
4. Which word has the **air** sound in it? ____ . A) clock B) shirt C) dress D) chair

### WEEK 2 — NAMING WORDS: THE WORDS THAT CALL THINGS BY NAME

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy (Language Domain) · Third Term W2.*

**Main content**

• A **noun** is a **naming word** — it is the word we call a person, a place, a thing or an animal by.
• People: **boy, girl, teacher, man, woman**. Places: **school, home, park, shop**. Things: **book, pen,
  car, chair**. Animals: **dog, cat, bird, fish**.
• A **proper noun** is the name of one single person or place — **Ada**, **Tunde**, **Lagos**,
  **Friday** — and it always begins with a **big letter**.
• Test a word by putting "the" before it: **the book**, **the dog**, **the school** work; "the run" does
  not, because run is not a naming word.
• One **singular** noun is one thing; more than one is **plural**, and we usually add **s**: cat / cats,
  book / books, pen / pens.
• Some plurals change a little and we do not add s to them: one child, two **children**; one foot, two
  **feet**; one tooth, three **teeth**.
• Our three sight words are **old**, **young** and **should**; old and young are each other's opposite, and we
  put them before a naming word: **an old man**, **a young boy**.
• **Should** tells us a thing is right to do: **"You should greet your teacher."**
• Sort a page of words into two hoops: naming words in one, all the others in the other.

**Words for my notebook**

A noun names a person, a place, a thing or an animal. A name like Ada or Lagos is a proper noun and begins with
a big letter. One cat, two cats; one child, two children. Old and young are opposites, and should tells us what
is right.

**Let us talk**

1. Name four things in this room and say what kind of noun each one is.
2. Which words in this list are proper nouns: friday, Lagos, book, Ada?
3. Say the plural of child, and of foot.
4. Make a line with "old" and one with "young" before a naming word.


![Four boxes, four kinds of naming word: a person standing, a school with its plain flag, a wooden chair, and a goat with horns and a short tail.](assets/img/nursery-2/3rd-term/mathematics-english-language/week2-four-kinds-of-noun.png)
**Worksheet**

1. A noun is a word that ____ . A) names a thing B) tells a size C) ends a line D) shows time
2. Which of these is a proper noun? A) school B) river C) Bola D) chair
3. "One child, two ____" . A) childs B) children C) childes D) childrens
4. The opposite of "an old man" is ____ . A) a young boy B) a tall tree C) a big house D) a new book
5. Fill in: A naming word that begins with a big letter is called a ____ noun.
6. Sort these into people, places, things and animals: teacher, park, pen, goat, woman, shop.
7. Write two lines, one with the word "should" and one with the word "young".

**My own work**

Draw four boxes and put in them one person, one place, one thing and one animal, then write the naming word
under each box.

### WEEK 3 — DOING WORDS: THE WORDS THAT MOVE THE LINE

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy (Language Domain) · Third Term W3.*

**Main content**

• A **verb** is a **doing word**: it tells what somebody or something does — **run, jump, walk, sit, eat,
  drink, sleep, play, read, write, sing, dance**.
• More we add today: **swim, fly, hop, skip, catch, throw, kick, clap**.
• Find it by asking **what is happening**: in "The baby sleeps", the doing word is **sleeps**.
• Say the word and **do** it: the class becomes the picture of the word, and the body remembers what the ear
  heard.
• When we speak of one person — he, she, Ada, the dog — the doing word usually takes an **s**: **I run**, but
  **she runs**; **they play**, but **he plays**.
• **What** is our asking word: **"What do you do at play?"** — and we answer in a line, not one word:
  **"I jump and I clap."**
• **Upon** is the old-time word for **on**: "the book is upon the table" says the same thing.
• **Little** tells a size before the naming word: **a little cat**, **a little bird**.
• Write three lines about your own day, each carrying one doing word.

**Words for my notebook**

A verb is a doing word: run, jump, eat, sing, read, write, hop, skip, catch, clap. We say "I run" but "she
runs". What is the asking word, little tells a small size, and upon means on.

**Let us talk**

1. What is the doing word in "The dog barks"?
2. Show us "hop" and "skip" with your body — are they the same?
3. Why does "run" become "runs" when we say "she"?
4. Ask the class a line that begins with "What" and let one child answer fully.


![Four children, four doing words: one hops on a foot, one skips with a rope, one sings with a note in the air, and one dances with an arm up.](assets/img/nursery-2/3rd-term/mathematics-english-language/week3-four-doing-words.png)
**Worksheet**

1. A verb tells us what somebody ____ . A) does B) owns C) looks like D) is called
2. Which of these is a doing word? A) green B) table C) clap D) small
3. We say "I sing" but "she ____" . A) sings B) sing C) singing D) singed
4. "Upon" means much the same as ____ . A) under B) upon the old C) on D) off
5. Fill in: The asking word that begins "____ do you do?" is What.
6. Name eight doing words you can show with your body.
7. Write three lines about today, each with one doing word in it.

**My own work**

Draw four children in four boxes doing four different things, then write the doing word for each under its box.

### WEEK 4 — A NAMING WORD AND A DOING WORD MAKE A SENTENCE

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy (Language Domain) · Third Term W4.*

**Main content**

• A sentence needs **two** things: who or what it is about (the naming word) and what happens
  (the doing word).
• **The dog runs.** — the dog is who, runs is what. **The girl jumps.** — same shape.
• A line with only one of the two is not finished: "The happy child" is not a sentence, but "The happy child
  **laughs**" is.
• Pairing matters: a **bird flies**, a **fish swims**, a **teacher teaches**, a **farmer farms**, a
  **driver drives**.
• Find the two parts in any line: ask **who?** then ask **what did they do?**
• Our three sight words are **yellow**, **there** and **your**: yellow is a colour word, there shows a place,
  your tells that a thing belongs to somebody.
• Put all the kinds together: **"Your yellow bird flies."** — that line has your, yellow, bird and flies.
• Do not mix **there** with **their**: there shows a place, their says a thing belongs to somebody.
• Read your built sentence aloud and count the words on your fingers; a sentence may be two words:
  **"Dogs run."**

**Words for my notebook**

Every sentence has a naming word and a doing word: the dog runs, the girl jumps. A bird flies, a fish swims, a
teacher teaches. Our words: yellow is a colour, there is a place, your means it belongs to you.

**Let us talk**

1. What two parts must a line have before it is a sentence?
2. Say the doing word that belongs to a bird, and the one that belongs to a teacher.
3. Read "Your yellow bird flies" and point out the colour word and the naming word.
4. Make a two-word sentence and then make it longer.


![A bird flies in the first box, a fish swims under three waves in the middle, and a goat stands on the bank with tufts of grass at its feet.](assets/img/nursery-2/3rd-term/mathematics-english-language/week4-the-bird-the-fish-the-goat.png)
**Worksheet**

1. A sentence must have a naming word and a ____ word. A) doing B) colour C) long D) plural
2. Which doing word goes with "bird"? A) swims B) flies C) teaches D) drives
3. "____ yellow sun hat" — the word that shows it belongs to you is ____ . A) There B) Your C) They D) Then
4. Which of these is NOT yet a sentence? A) Dogs run. B) The happy child. C) The girl jumps. D) Fish swim.
5. Fill in: "There" is a word about a ____, not about who owns a thing.
6. Match five naming words to the doing word that suits them.
7. Write one line that carries a colour word, a naming word and a doing word.

**My own work**

Draw a bird in the air, a fish in water and a goat on the bank, and let one line of words go under each

### WEEK 5 — LONGER LINES: WHO DOES WHAT, AND WHY

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy (Language Domain) · Third Term W7.*

**Main content**

• Grow the sentence to **naming + doing + naming**: **The boy kicks the ball** — who, does what, to what.
• Add one describing word and the line gets a picture in it: **The big boy kicks the red ball.**
• Two lines can be joined with **and**: **I run and I jump.** — each half could stand alone.
• **Because** carries the reason: **I sleep because I am tired**; **She smiles because she won.**
• Ask and answer about the line: **"What does the cat do?"** — **"The cat sleeps."**
• **Them** is the small word we use instead of the people we already named: "I see the boys" becomes
  **"I see them."**
• Our three sight words are **because**, **them** and **put**; put is a doing word: **"Put the book on the
  shelf."**
• Word order is not free in English: **The dog bites the man** and **The man bites the dog** are both correct
  lines with different meanings, and only one of them is true.
• Check your line with the marks we learnt: big letter first, room between the words, full stop at the end, a
  question mark if it asks.
• A long line is only two short lines that learned to hold hands; say the short one first, then add to it.

**Words for my notebook**

A line can grow: the boy kicks the ball; the big boy kicks the red ball. And joins two lines, because gives the
reason, and them stands in for people we already named. Put is a doing word.

**Let us talk**

1. Make the line "The girl eats" into one with three parts.
2. Which word carries a reason — and or because?
3. Change "I see the goats" so that one small word stands for the goats.
4. Say one line about yourself with "because" in it.


![A boy puts a ball in the basket, a girl lifts a book onto the shelf, and two children sit down under a tree because their legs are tired.](assets/img/nursery-2/3rd-term/mathematics-english-language/week5-put-things-away.png)
**Worksheet**

1. "The boy kicks the ball" has a naming word, a doing word and ____ . A) another naming word B) no other word C) only a colour D) a plural mark
2. The word that brings a reason is ____ . A) and B) because C) there D) them
3. "I see the boys" can be said as "I see ____" . A) there B) put C) them D) they're
4. Which line is joined with "and"? A) I run and I jump. B) I run fast. C) Why do I run? D) I run now.
5. Fill in: "____ the cup on the table" — the doing word we need is Put.
6. Grow this line twice: "The dog barks." — add a describing word, then add a second thing.
7. Write two lines about your evening, one with "because" in it.

**My own work**

Draw three small scenes — a boy putting balls in a basket, a girl putting a book on a shelf, two children
sitting because they are tired — and write one line under each.

### WEEK 6 — READING ON OUR OWN, AND SAYING WHAT WE HEARD

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 2 · Literacy (Language Domain) · Third Term W8.*

**Main content**

• Our page for this week: **"Sam has a small boat. He puts the boat in the water. The wind blows and the boat
  goes fast. Ada claps her hands and runs to the shore."**
• Five questions open a story: **Who?** (Sam and Ada) **What?** (a boat on the water) **Where?**
  (at the shore) **When?** (a windy day) **Why?** (the wind blew).
• Read with the eyes, not the finger, and stop at the full stop so the listener can breathe.
• The **main character** is the person the page is mostly about; the **events** are the things that happen in
  order.
• Pick out the naming words in the first line, then the doing words; a page is built from nothing else.
• The sight words we learnt sit in every page: **has, in, and, to, he, she, there, what, should** — find
  them before you sound them.
• Guess ahead: cover the last line and say what you think Ada will do; a reader who predicts is a reader who
  is thinking.
• Tie it to yourself: **"When the wind blew my boat away, I ran after it."**
• Retell the page in your own words afterwards — your line may be shorter than the book's, and that is right.
• Long pages get easier the way a long walk does: a little further each day, read a page more than yesterday.

**Words for my notebook**

We ask who, what, where, when and why about any page. The main character is who the page is mostly about, and
the events are what happens in order. I can guess the next line before I read it.

**Let us talk**

1. Who is our page mostly about, and what does he have?
2. Where does the story happen, and when?
3. Find two doing words in the second line.
4. What do you think happens after the last line?


![Our boat story: a boy sets the boat in the water, the wind lines push it fast over the waves, and a girl claps for it at the shore.](assets/img/nursery-2/3rd-term/mathematics-english-language/week6-the-boat-in-three-boxes.png)
**Worksheet**

1. The person a story is mostly about is its ____ character. A) main B) small C) last D) quiet
2. Which question asks for the reason? A) Who? B) When? C) Why? D) Where?
3. In "The wind blows", the doing word is ____ . A) wind B) blows C) the D) air
4. Before we turn the page we can ____ what will happen. A) guess B) forget C) erase D) count
5. Fill in: The things that happen in a story, in order, are its ____.
6. Read the page about the boat twice — once slowly, once smooth — and say which time was better.
7. Write two lines about a time the wind took something from you.

**My own work**

Draw our boat page in three boxes — the boat in the water, the wind blowing it fast, Ada clapping at the shore
— and write one line under each box.

### WEEK 8 — WRITING A STORY OF OUR OWN, AND THE WORDS THAT START ONE

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy (Language Domain) · Third Term W9.*

**Main content**

• Stories often open with the old door-word: **"Once upon a time…"** and **once** is our sight word for that.
• Three lines make a story: what was the start, what happened, how it ended.
• **Want** is a doing word for a wish in the heart: **"They want their toys."** — that line has both want and
  their in it.
• **Their** tells that things belong to people: **their toys**, **their books**; it is the family of "they".
• **There** shows a place: **the ball is there**. The two sound the same and are not the same word.
• Put a describing word in the middle line and the listener sees the picture: **the small dog**,
  **the long road**.
• Write about your own life too, and it is already a story: **"What I did"**, **"My family"**,
  **"My favourite toy"**.
• Do not stop at the first line: a story may be wrong in its letters and still be yours; we fix marks after,
  not while.
• If a word will not come, draw it in the box above and ask for its letters when you finish.
• Read your story out with your finger under each word; the class may ask you one question about it.

**Words for my notebook**

Once begins a tale. Want is a wish word, and their means things belong to people — there is about a place and
sounds the same. My story has a beginning, a middle and an end.

**Let us talk**

1. What word often opens a story?
2. Say a line with "want" and "their" in it.
3. Which one is about a place — there or their?
4. What will you write about: your day, your family, or your favourite toy?

**Worksheet**

1. "____ upon a time" begins a story with which word? A) Once B) One C) Only D) Over
2. "They ____ their shoes" — the wish word is ____ . A) there B) want C) their D) when
3. The words that mean things belong to the people spoken of are ____ . A) there B) they C) their D) then
4. "The ball is there" — there tells us ____ . A) a place B) a colour C) a number D) a reason
5. Fill in: "there" and "their" sound the same but do not mean the same ____ .
6. Write the three lines of your story, each with one naming word and one doing word.
7. Add one describing word to your middle line and read it to the class.

**My own work**

### WEEK 9 — THE WHOLE OF IT ON ONE PAGE

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Literacy (Language Domain) · Third Term W10.*

**Main content**

• Our last page carries all of it at once: **"Kemi finds a little black cat under a bench. She takes it home
  and gives it milk. The cat drinks, jumps up and sleeps upon her bed."**
• Sounds hidden in it: the **bl** and **ck** of black, the **nd** of and, the **sh** in she, the **tch** in
  bench, the **air** we met in hair.
• Name the kinds of word: **Kemi, cat, bench, home, milk, bed** are naming words; **finds, takes, gives,
  drinks, jumps, sleeps** are doing words; **little, black** describe.
• Marks to check on every line of yours: big letter first and on every name, room between words, full stop at
  the end, question mark if it asks.
• Proofreading is reading your own work **slowly with your finger**, looking for the four things above and
  nothing else.
• The sight words we took all term are here too: **upon, little, what, there, their, should, old, young,
  because, them, put, once, want, your, yellow, shirt, clock, hair**.
• Reading on and on without stopping is a muscle, not a trick: a page more each day, and the arm does not tire.
• When a word will not come, say its sounds, count them on your fingers, and write one letter for each.
• Read your page to a friend and let them ask you **who, what, where, when and why** about it.
• What you can do on the last day of this term — read a page, name the kinds of word, write three clean lines —
  is what the next class will build on.

**Words for my notebook**

On one page I can find the naming words, the doing words and the describing words, and the sounds we learnt. I
check my own lines for a big letter, room between words and the right mark at the end. That is what I take to
Primary 1.

**Let us talk**

1. Read Kemi's page once with your finger and once with your eyes.
2. Name three doing words and two describing words in it.
3. Which four things do you look for when you proofread your own line?
4. What will you still want to be better at when you are in Primary 1?
