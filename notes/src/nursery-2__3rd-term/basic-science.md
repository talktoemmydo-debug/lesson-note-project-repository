### WEEK 1 — WATER: HOW WE KEEP IT CLEAN AND SAFE

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Basic Science and Technology · Third Term W2 (pp 497–502).*

**Main content**

• **Water keeps best** in a container that is **clean** and has a **lid**.
• A lid keeps out **dirt**, **dust**, **flies** and **small insects** that want to fall in.
• Containers we use for water: a **covered bucket**, a **kettle with a lid**, a **water tank**, a
  **jerry can**, a **clay pot (agolo)**, a **bottle**.
• A container used for water should **never** be used for paraffin, insecticide or soap.
• Before water goes in, the container is **washed out with clean water** first.
• We **dip a cup or a ladle** into the water; we do not put our hands, our legs or a dirty sponge in it.
• Stored water can still become **dirty**: an open bucket collects dust, and a bucket on the floor
  collects dirt from the feet of everybody who passes.
• Keep the container on a **stand** or a **bench**, covered, away from the cooking place and the toilet.
• Once a week the container is **emptied, washed and rinsed**, then filled again.
• **Rain water** is caught in a clean tank or drum with a cover; **borehole, well and tap water** come
  from the ground or the pipe.

**Words for my notebook**

We store water in a clean covered container so that dirt and insects cannot get in. The container is
washed before use and kept on a stand, away from the floor and the cooking place.

**Let us talk**

1. Which container keeps water cleaner — the open bucket or the bucket with a lid?
2. What falls into water that is left uncovered?
3. Show me how you carry water home without spilling it.
4. Why is a jerry can a good container for water?

### WEEK 2 — WATER-BORNE DISEASES: SICKNESS THAT COMES WITH DIRTY WATER

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Basic Science and Technology · Third Term W3 (pp 497–502).*

**Main content**

• A **water-borne disease** is a sickness that comes to us from **dirty water** or from water with
  germs in it.
• The ones we must know: **diarrhoea**, **cholera**, **typhoid fever** and **dysentery**.
• **Diarrhoea** means passing loose water many times a day; the body loses water and becomes weak.
• In **cholera** the stomach pains, the child vomits and passes watery stool again and again.
• **Dysentery** is diarrhoea with blood and pain in the belly.
• **Typhoid fever** brings long heat (fever), a bad head and a tummy that will not work well.
• Germs enter the body when we **drink dirty water**, **wash food in dirty water**, or touch food with
  hands that have not been washed after the toilet.
• The signs to watch for: **stomach pain**, **vomiting**, **loose stool**, **weakness**, **fever**.
• Prevention is better than cure: **boil or treat the water**, **wash hands with soap**, **cover the
  water**, **use the toilet**, and **wash the food** before it is eaten.
• A child with diarrhoea must **drink plenty of clean water** and the **ORS water** the clinic gives,
  and must be **taken to the clinic** the same day if the belly pain or fever does not stop.

**Words for my notebook**

Water-borne diseases — diarrhoea, cholera, typhoid and dysentery — come from dirty water. Clean water,
washed hands and covered containers keep us away from them.

**Let us talk**

1. What is the danger in drinking water straight from a stream?
2. Name one sign of a child who has diarrhoea.
3. When must we wash our hands with soap?
4. Which water is safer for drinking: boiled water or water from the barrel?

### WEEK 3 — TECHNOLOGY: SECURITY GADGETS THAT KEEP US SAFE

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Basic Science and Technology · Third Term W4 (pp 497–502).*

**Main content**

• **Security** means keeping **people** and **property** safe from thieves, fire and danger.
• A **security gadget** is a machine or a tool that helps people stay safe.
• **Locks and keys** keep doors and gates shut so that a stranger cannot walk in.
• A **burglar alarm** makes a loud sound when somebody forces a door or a window.
• A **CCTV camera** watches the gate or the shop and **records what happens**, so the police can see it.
• A **security light** turns bright at night so that nobody can move in the dark unseen.
• A **smoke alarm** warns the house when there is **fire or smoke**, long before the fire is big.
• A **security man** uses a **torch**, a **whistle**, a **radio**, a **uniform** and a **gate book** to
  know who is coming and going.
• When an alarm sounds: **leave the house at once**, go to the place your family has agreed on, and
  **tell a security person or a grown-up at the gate** — never hide under the bed and never go back for a toy.
• Gadgets help, but **they do not think**: a gate left open and a key under the mat undo a good lock.

**Words for my notebook**

Security gadgets — locks, alarms, cameras, lights and smoke detectors — help keep people and property
safe. When an alarm sounds we leave the house and tell the person at the gate.

**Let us talk**

1. What keeps a gate shut at night?
2. What does a camera on the wall of a shop do?
3. What sound does a smoke alarm make, and what do we do when we hear it?
4. Name one thing a security man carries.

### WEEK 4 — ANIMALS AND THE HOMES THEY LIVE IN

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Basic Science and Technology · Third Term W7 (pp 497–502).*

**Main content**

• Every animal has a **home** where it **sleeps, hides its young ones and stays safe**.
• Names we must know: **dog → kennel**, **chicken → coop**, **cow → shed or barn**, **horse → stable**,
  **bee → hive**, **bird → nest**, **lion → den**, **rabbit → burrow**, **ant → anthill**,
  **spider → web**, **fish → water**.
• A **nest** is built by the bird out of **twigs, grass, mud and feather**.
• A **web** is spun by the spider out of its own thread to catch small flies.
• An **anthill** is dug by ants under the ground; the loose soil is what we see heaped up outside.
• A **burrow** is a hole in the ground; the rabbit and the groundnut live in one.
• **Wild homes** are not made by people: the **forest**, the **cave**, the **river**, the **tree hole**,
  the **rock**.
• **Homes people make** are for the animals we keep: the **kennel**, the **coop**, the **shed**, the
  **stable**, the **aquarium**.
• An animal chooses or builds its home for **shade**, **safety**, **rest** and **young ones**.
• If a home is destroyed, the animal must **move, find food elsewhere or die** — that is why we do not
  burn bushes carelessly or cut every tree.

**Words for my notebook**

Animals live in homes: a dog in a kennel, a bird in a nest, a cow in a shed, a fish in water. Some homes
are made by nature; some are made by people who keep the animals.

**Let us talk**

1. Where does your neighbour's dog sleep at night?
2. What does a bird use to build its nest?
3. Which animal lives in a web?
4. Name an animal whose home is in the ground.

### WEEK 5 — ANIMALS AND THEIR YOUNG ONES

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Basic Science and Technology · Third Term W8 (pp 497–502).*

**Main content**

• A **young one** is the baby of an animal; most young ones **look like their parents**, only smaller.
• The names we must know: **dog → puppy**, **cat → kitten**, **cow → calf**, **horse → foal**,
  **sheep → lamb**, **goat → kid**, **chicken → chick**, **duck → duckling**, **lion → cub**,
  **elephant → calf**, **bear → cub**.
• Some young ones do **not** look like the parent at first: the **frog's** young one is a **tadpole**
  that swims with a tail and has no legs; the **butterfly's** young one is a **caterpillar** that crawls.
• The growth from tadpole to frog and from caterpillar to butterfly is a **change of shape**.
• A mother animal gives **milk** to its young, keeps them **warm**, **cleans** them and **warns** them.
• A father animal may **guard** the young, as the dog guards the pups in the kennel.
• Birds **feed** the chick by carrying food in the mouth to the nest.
• The young one of a hen hatches from an **egg**; the young one of a cat is **born alive**.
• Animals that lay eggs: **hen, duck, tortoise, fish, snake, butterfly**. Animals that are born alive:
  **dog, cat, cow, goat, horse, rabbit**.
• We are kind to young animals: we do not chase them, we do not take them from the nest, and we do not
  tie a rope round a kid for play.

**Words for my notebook**

The young of a dog is a puppy, of a cat a kitten, of a hen a chick, of a cow a calf. Some, like the
tadpole and the caterpillar, change shape before they grow into the adult.

**Let us talk**

1. What do we call the baby of a hen?
2. Which animal's young one is called a kid?
3. Show me how a mother cat keeps her kittens warm.
4. What does a cow give its calf to drink?

### WEEK 6 — TECHNOLOGY: SIMPLE MACHINES USED IN THE HOME

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Basic Science and Technology · Third Term W9 (pp 497–502).*

**Main content**

• A **machine** is anything people make that **makes work easier, faster or better**.
• A **simple machine** has few parts and is moved by my own strength.
• In the kitchen: a **broom** sweeps the floor, a **cutlass** and a **knife** cut, a **spoon** and a
  **fork** carry food to the mouth, a **plate** holds the food, a **pot** cooks it.
• For the hands: a **bottle opener** and a **can opener** lift lids, a **hammer** knocks a nail, a
  **screwdriver** turns a screw, a **spanner** holds a bolt.
• For the body and the house: an **iron** presses clothes, a **fan** moves air, a **torch** gives
  light, a **bucket** carries water, a **wheelbarrow** carries loads.
• A machine makes work easier but it still needs **care**: a knife cuts the hand that holds it badly, a
  hot iron burns, a fan blade hurts a finger put inside it.
• Some machines are **for grown-ups to use**, and I only watch: the cutlass, the sharp knife, the hot
  iron, the blender that is plugged in.
• After use a tool is **cleaned, dried and put back in its place**; a knife is never left on the edge of
  a table and never carried about by its blade.
• Never touch a **plug, socket or wire** with **wet hands**, and never pull a cable to bring a machine down.
• Keeping tools in good order is part of using them: a blunt cutlass is more dangerous than a sharp one,
  because the hand slips.

**Words for my notebook**

A machine makes work easier. At home we use a broom, a knife, a spoon, a can opener, a hammer, an iron
and a fan. Every one of them needs care and a safe place.

**Let us talk**

1. Which machine in your house makes the clothes smooth?
2. What do we use to sweep the compound?
3. Which tools must a child not hold?
4. Where should a knife be kept after use?

### WEEK 8 — TECHNOLOGY: SIMPLE MACHINES USED IN SCHOOL

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Basic Science and Technology · Third Term W10 (pp 497–502).*

**Main content**

• A **pencil sharpener** cuts the wood away so the lead comes out to a point; turn the pencil, not the
  blade.
• **Scissors** cut paper for art: hold the paper with the fingers **away** from the line the blade follows.
• A **stapler** joins two or three sheets with a small pin; press flat, never staple the desk.
• A **hole puncher** makes a round hole so the page can go into a file.
• A **ruler** measures in centimetres and draws a **straight line**; it is not a throwing stick.
• An **eraser** (a **duster** for the board) removes pencil marks; chalk and a board duster keep the
  board clean for the next writing.
• The **bell** or the **gong** calls the class in and out; one person rings it at the agreed time.
• A **dustpan and brush** carry the sweepings out of the room — the brush sweeps into the pan, never at
  another child.
• On the playground the **swing** and the **slide** are simple machines too: the swing is a **wheel and
  axle with a rope** and the slide is an **inclined plane** (a slope that saves the lifting).
• Every machine in the class is **school property**: it is carried with two hands, used for its own
  work, counted before it is returned to the cupboard, and **reported at once when it is damaged**.

**Words for my notebook**

At school I use a sharpener, scissors, a stapler, a hole puncher, a ruler, an eraser, a duster, a bell
and a dustpan. They are school things; I use them for their own work and return them in good order.

**Let us talk**

1. What do you use to make the pencil sharp again?
2. Which machine joins two sheets of paper?
3. Who rings the bell in our school?
4. What should we do when a scissors is broken?

### WEEK 9 — EXPLORING OUR ENVIRONMENT

*Depth: NERDC Primary 1 · Basic Science · First Term W1–2 (pp 624–625), the year's ending look at the
child's own surroundings.*

**Main content**

• My **environment** is **everything around me**: the room I sit in, the compound, the street, the
  market, the church or mosque, the stream, the trees and the people.
• I learn about my environment with the **five senses**: I **see** colours and shapes, **hear** the
  seller's bell and the generator, **smell** cooking and dirty water, **touch** rough wall and smooth
  table, and sometimes **taste** the salt air or the dust.
• In the **classroom**: table, bench, board, duster, cupboard, fan, poster, my bag and my book.
• In the **school compound**: the block, the office, the toilet, the bin, the assembly ground, the
  playground, the water tank, the guard's chair.
• At **home**: the living room, the kitchen, the bedroom, the bathroom, the compound, the gutter, the
  bin, the gate.
• **People** are part of my environment too: my family, my teacher, my friends, my neighbours, the
  seller, the driver, the person who sweeps our street.
• Things that make an environment **unpleasant**: a blocked gutter, refuse in the gutter, a toilet with
  no water, a stream of dirty water, broken glass, an open drain.
• Things that make it **pleasant**: a swept compound, a covered bin, grass cut, trees planted, clean
  water, a painted wall, a classroom with light in it.
• On a **nature walk** I look, I listen, and I say what I found; I do not pick unknown fruit, and I do
  not put anything in my mouth.
• I can **keep my place clean**: refuse goes in the bin, the gutter is cleared, the tap is closed after
  use, and I wash my hands after the toilet.

**Words for my notebook**

My environment is everything around me — the room, the compound, the street, the people. I know it with
my five senses, and I help to keep it clean.

**Let us talk**

1. Name three things you can see from our classroom window.
2. What sound can you hear outside this room right now?
3. Which place in your compound must be kept cleanest, and why?
4. What did you find on our walk round the school?
