### WEEK 1 — MY STATE AND MY TOWN

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · General Knowledge · Third Term W1.*

**You will learn to**

• say the name of my state and the town where I live
• put my state on the map of Nigeria, in a big first look
• name the famous things of my state: its food, its festivals and its places
• draw a picture map of my town with the five places we all know

**Things to know**

• A **state** is a big piece of the country with its own head; Nigeria has **thirty-six states** and one
  capital territory, and the capital territory is called **Abuja**.
• Our state is **Lagos**, and it sits at the **south-west** of Nigeria, where the land meets the sea.
• The big city of our state is called **Ikeja**; the town or village where my house stands is **my own town**.
• On the map we look for three things first: the **sea** at the bottom, the **bridge** that goes over the water,
  and the **lagoon** that lies between.
• Famous places in our state: the **beach** with its soft sand, the **harbour** where big ships stand, and the
  **bridge** long enough to cross the whole water.
• Famous food of our people: **àmàlà with ẹ̀fọ́**, and by the sea we also eat **fish** that came in a boat.
• Festival of our town: **Eyo** — the day the masked people walk in white through the streets and the whole town
  comes out to see.
• Five places every town has: the **market**, the **motor park**, the **school**, a **church or a mosque**, and
  the **stream or the water point**.
• A town is not only houses: it is the **people** in them — the seller, the driver, the guard, the teacher and
  my own family.

**Words for my notebook**

My state is Lagos, in the south-west of Nigeria, where the land meets the sea. My town has a market, a motor
park, a school, a church and a mosque, and a stream that runs past it.

**Let us talk**

1. What is the name of your state, and which town is your house in?
2. Point to the sea on the map and then point to our state.
3. Name two foods of our people and one food that comes from the sea.
4. Which four places did we say every town must have?

**Worksheet**

1. Our state in the south-west of Nigeria is ____ . A) Kano B) Lagos C) Borno D) Enugu
2. The big city of Lagos state is called ____ . A) Ikeja B) Zaria C) Lokoja D) Calabar
3. The festival where masked people walk in white is ____ . A) Durbar B) Ọ̀ṣun C) Eyo D) New Yam
4. On the map of Nigeria, the sea is at the ____ . A) top B) right side C) middle D) bottom
5. Fill in: Nigeria has thirty-six states and one capital territory called ____ .
6. Name the five places that a town has.
7. Write two sentences on the town where your house is.

**My own work**

Draw a picture map of our town: put the market on one road, the school on another, the motor park by the wide
road, and the stream running past behind them; colour the roads.

### WEEK 2 — WATER AND AIR: WHY THEY MATTER

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · General Knowledge · Third Term W2.*

**You will learn to**

• name where water comes from and what we use it for
• say why air matters even though we cannot see it
• keep water clean and not waste it
• do three small tests with water and air

**Things to know**

• Water comes to us from the **rain**, the **tap**, the **well**, the **stream** and the **river**.
• We use water to **drink**, to **bath**, to **cook**, to **wash** cloth and plates, and to give the **plants**.
• Air is **everywhere**; it is inside the room, inside the bag, and inside the ball we blow up.
• We cannot see air, but we can **feel it as wind**, and wind can move a leaf, a kite and a small boat.
• Living things **breathe**: we take air in and let it out; a plant takes in the part we throw out.
• Water stays clean when the vessel is **covered**, the cup is washed, and the hand does not go inside.
• Water for drinking is **boiled** by a grown-up and then kept in a clean covered keg.
• We do not waste it: the tap is **shut** while we rub the teeth, and one cup of water is enough for that.
• Test one — **float or sink**: a leaf floats, a stone sinks, a bottle top may do either.
• Test two — **bubbles**: blow water with a straw and the air comes out as round balls.
• Test three — a **wind chime**: hang shells on a thread by the window and the air plays the tune.

**Words for my notebook**

Water comes from rain, the tap, the well, the stream and the river; we drink it, bath in it, cook with it and
give it to plants. Air is everywhere, we breathe it, and wind is air that is moving.

**Let us talk**

1. Name four places water comes from.
2. What do we do with water every morning before school?
3. How can we know air is there when we cannot see it?
4. Say two ways we keep water clean and one way we waste it.

**Worksheet**

1. A stream and a river are places we get ____ from. A) air B) water C) fire D) sand
2. Wind is really ____ that is moving. A) rain B) dust C) air D) smoke
3. Before a child drinks it, water for the house is ____ . A) boiled B) frozen C) buried D) coloured
4. In the float-and-sink test, the thing that goes down is ____ . A) a dry leaf B) a feather C) a small stone D) a bottle top
5. Fill in: We shut the ____ while we rub our teeth, so that water is not wasted.
6. Say five things water is used for at home.
7. Write two sentences on what you saw in the bubble test.

**My own work**

Draw a tall glass of covered water, and beside it six small things — a leaf, a stone, a feather, a bead, a
bottle top and a bit of cloth — then ring the ones you think will float.

### WEEK 3 — THE SKY: SUN, MOON, STARS, DAY AND NIGHT

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · General Knowledge · Third Term W3.*

**You will learn to**

• say what the sun gives us and when it is up
• name what we see in the sky at night
• tell the parts of the day in order
• sort things into the day and the night

**Things to know**

• The **sun** gives us **light** and **warmth**; it is the reason the day is bright.
• The **daytime** is when we work, learn and play; the **night** is when the world rests and we sleep.
• At night we see the **moon** and the **stars**, and sometimes the moon is full and round.
• The parts of the day in order: **morning**, **midday**, **afternoon**, **evening**, **night**.
• In the **morning** the sun rises; at **midday** it is high and hottest; in the **evening** it goes down.
• The **sky** is everything above us; the clouds live in it and the **rain** comes down out of it.
• A cloud is a big crowd of very small drops of water; when they grow heavy, they fall as rain.
• Day and night are not the same: in the day we see colour, at night we see light from lamps, the moon and fire.
• Sorting game: a kite, a school bag and a market basket go to the **day**; a torch, a mosquito net and a night
  lamp go to the **night**.
• We do not look straight at the sun; its light is too strong for the eye.

**Words for my notebook**

The sun gives light and warmth and makes the day. The moon and the stars come with the night, when we rest. The
sky is above us, and the clouds and the rain live in it.

**Let us talk**

1. What two things does the sun give us?
2. Which two things do we see in the sky only at night?
3. Say the parts of the day, from morning to night.
4. Where does rain come from?

**Worksheet**

1. The thing that gives us light and warmth in the day is ____ . A) the moon B) the lamp C) the sun D) the star
2. At night we see the moon and the ____ . A) rainbow B) stars C) clouds at midday D) shadow at noon
3. The part of the day when the sun is high and hottest is ____ . A) morning B) evening C) night D) midday
4. Rain comes down out of the ____ . A) mountain B) well C) sky D) market
5. Fill in: When the sun goes down in the ____, we light the lamp.
6. Put two things in the day box and two things in the night box.
7. Write two sentences on what you do between evening and sleep.

**My own work**

Fold a page in two. In one half draw the sun and three things we do in the daylight; in the other half draw the
moon, three stars and three things we do at night.

### WEEK 4 — SIMPLE MACHINES AND TOOLS AROUND ME

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · General Knowledge · Third Term W4.*

**You will learn to**

• name the tools we use at home and at school
• say what each tool is for
• tell which things have wheels and what a wheel does
• say how a simple machine takes work off our hands

**Things to know**

• Tools at home: **spoon** for eating, **fork** for holding food, **knife** for cutting, **broom** for the
  floor, **rake** for the yard, **bucket** for water.
• Tools at school: **pencil** for writing, **crayon** for colouring, **ruler** for drawing a straight line,
  **scissors** for cutting paper.
• A **knife** and a pair of **scissors** are sharp, so they are carried with the point **down** and passed to a
  friend **by the handle**.
• Things with wheels: **bicycle**, **car**, **wheelbarrow**, and the **cart** at the market; a wheel **rolls**,
  it does not slide.
• A wheel makes a heavy thing easy to move; that is why a load goes in a wheelbarrow and not on the head
  alone.
• A **ramp** is a slope: a plank laid from the ground to a lorry lets a heavy bag slide up instead of being
  lifted.
• A **lever** is a strong stick: put one end under a stone, rest the middle on a small stone, and the big stone
  comes up.
• A tool does not do the work for us; it **takes part of the work off our hands**.
• Touch-and-tell: things go in a bag, a hand goes in, and the class names the tool without looking.
• Every tool has its place: the broom on the wall, the scissors in the box, the pencil in the purse.

**Words for my notebook**

Spoon, fork, knife, broom, rake and bucket are the tools of the house; pencil, crayon, ruler and scissors are
the tools of the class. A wheel rolls, a ramp is a slope, and a lever is a strong stick that lifts a stone.

**Let us talk**

1. Name four tools of the house and what each one is for.
2. How do you pass a pair of scissors to a friend?
3. What does a wheel do that makes a load easy?
4. Which two tools are kept out of the way of small hands?

**Worksheet**

1. The tool used for sweeping the floor is the ____ . A) rake B) bucket C) broom D) spoon
2. A wheelbarrow carries a load because it has a ____ . A) rope B) wheel C) lid D) blade
3. When we give a pair of scissors to somebody we hold it by the ____ . A) blades B) point C) spring D) handle
4. A plank laid from the ground up to a lorry is a ____ . A) ramp B) lever C) basket D) keg
5. Fill in: A strong stick used to lift a stone is called a ____ .
6. Match six tools to their work by drawing a line.
7. Write two sentences on the tool you use most in class.

**My own work**

Draw a box divided into six squares and put one tool in each square. Cut along the outside line with the
scissors, then match the tools to their work with a line.

### WEEK 5 — WE ARE ONE NIGERIA: BIG FAMILY, MANY PEOPLE

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · General Knowledge · Third Term W5.*

**You will learn to**

• say that Nigeria is one country of many peoples
• name foods and tongues that are different among us
• treat every person with the same respect
• take part in the class celebration of one Nigeria

**Things to know**

• Nigeria is **one country** made of **many peoples**; all of us are **Nigerians**.
• The country took its name from the **river Niger** that runs through it; the Niger and the **Benue** are the
  two big rivers, and they meet in the middle of the land.
• Among our peoples are the **Yoruba**, the **Igbo**, the **Hausa**, the **Tiv**, the **Edo**, the **Efik**, the
  **Ijaw**, the **Kanuri** and many more.
• Our foods are different and all of them are good: **àmàlà with ẹ̀fọ́** in the west, **pounded yam with
  soup** in the east, **tuwo with soup** in the north, and **fish** brought in a boat by the coast.
• Our tongues are different, but a smile, the word "hello" and a shared pencil need no translation.
• The flag is our one sign: **green, white, green** in three tall strips — the green of our land, the white of
  peace.
• **Respect** is for everybody: the old and the young, every tribe, boys and girls, a child who learns fast and
  a child who takes time.
• Kindness is a thing we **do**: sharing a biscuit, carrying a bag for a smaller child, saying "thank you" and
  "sorry".
• For the class celebration each child brings one thing of their people — a food, a cloth, a song — and we lay
  them on one table and walk round it singing **"One Nigeria, one people."**
• Different is not wrong; different is **more**. A pot with only one pepper in it has no taste.

**Words for my notebook**

Nigeria is one country of many peoples, many foods and many tongues. The flag is green, white and green. I
respect everybody the same, because different is not wrong — different is more.

**Let us talk**

1. What two rivers are the big rivers of Nigeria?
2. Name four peoples of Nigeria.
3. What are the colours of our flag, and how are they arranged?
4. Say three kind things you did this week and to whom.

**Worksheet**

1. Nigeria takes its name from ____ . A) a city B) a river C) a mountain D) a king
2. The colour in the middle of our flag is ____ . A) red B) green C) white D) gold
3. Our two big rivers, the Niger and the Benue, meet ____ . A) in the middle of the country B) at the sea C) in the north only D) at the market
4. Which of these is a people of Nigeria? ____ . A) the Zulu B) the Yoruba C) the Maya D) the Inuit
5. Fill in: Different is not wrong; different is ____ .
6. Name four foods of the different peoples of Nigeria.
7. Write two sentences on one kind thing you can do for a child who is different from you.

**My own work**

Draw the flag on a pole — green, white and green in three tall strips — and under it draw four children, each
holding one food of their people.

### WEEK 6 — COUNTING, SORTING AND EVERYDAY KNOWLEDGE

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · General Knowledge · Third Term W6.*

**You will learn to**

• count things about us from one to twenty
• sort things by size, colour, shape and kind
• say the days of the week and the routine of a day
• play the sorting games of the class

**Things to know**

• Counting is done with the **finger on the thing**: one bottle top, two bottle tops … twenty bottle tops, and
  then we put them in **two lines of ten**.
• Things in the house we can count: **cups, plates, spoons, chairs, the windows, the goats in the yard**.
• Sorting by **size**: big gari basin, small bowl; long cloth, small handkerchief.
• Sorting by **colour**: everything red in one heap, everything white in another, everything black in the last.
• Sorting by **shape**: a plate is **round**, a book is **square**, a ball is a **sphere**, a cone hat is a
  **cone**.
• Sorting by **kind**: fruits on one side — banana, mango, watermelon; vegetables on the other — ugu, spinach,
  carrot.
• The seven days come in order: **Sunday, Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday**.
• The routine of my day: **wake, wash, dress, eat, school, learn, play, bathe, eat, sleep** — and it is the
  same order every day, that is why the day feels easy.
• Games of the week: **shop play** (the seller, the buyer, the coins), **counting songs**, and a **sorting
  race** — who can put ten mixed beads into their colours first.
• A number is not only a word: five is five cups, five fingers, five beats of a drum.

**Words for my notebook**

I can count from one to twenty and put twenty into two lines of ten. I can sort by size, colour, shape and
kind. Sunday, Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday are the seven days of the week.

**Let us talk**

1. Count ten things on your table, out loud.
2. I have a plate, a book and a ball: which one is round?
3. Which day comes after Tuesday, and which comes before Sunday?
4. Say five things you do every day, in order.

**Worksheet**

1. Twenty bottle tops, put in two equal lines, make lines of ____ . A) five B) eight C) ten D) fifteen
2. A book is like which shape? ____ . A) a circle B) a square C) a cone D) a ball
3. The day that comes after Tuesday is ____ . A) Monday B) Thursday C) Sunday D) Wednesday
4. Which of these is a vegetable, not a fruit? ____ . A) ugu B) mango C) banana D) watermelon
5. Fill in: The day that comes right after Friday is ____ .
6. Sort these into fruits and vegetables: mango, carrot, banana, tomato, ugu, orange.
7. Write the five things you do before you come to school, in order.

**My own work**

Draw twenty bottle tops in two lines of ten and circle the tens. Under them, draw two baskets: write "fruit" on
one and "vegetable" on the other, then draw three things of each kind inside.

### WEEK 8 — PEOPLE WHO DID GREAT THINGS

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · General Knowledge · Third Term W8.*

**You will learn to**

• name the work-a-day people who keep a town going
• hear two stories of people of the past
• say what a hero does that a child can do too
• tell the class who you want to be when you grow up

**Things to know**

• The **farmer** rises before the sun and the whole town eats because of that work.
• The **teacher**, the **nurse**, the **driver**, the **cleaner**, the **seller** and the **guard** are the
  people who make a town move; none of them is a small work.
• In the old stories, **Ìjà̀pá the tortoise** is small and slow but he out-thinks the whole bush; cleverness is
  a thing he **uses**, and the stories also show where it takes him somewhere he did not want to go.
• **Queen Amina of Zaria** is remembered in the story as a **warrior queen**: the story says she rode with her
  people, made the roads safe for traders, and put up walls round the towns so that nobody could enter unasked.
• Old **kings and queens** — the Oba, the Emir, the Obi — sat on thrones, but the stories we love are the ones
  where they **listened** to an old woman at the market.
• The people who help **many** people are the **doctor**, the **nurse**, the **firefighter**, the **teacher** and
  the ones who dig a **well** in a village that had none.
• **Our own parents** are in this list too: they work, they cook, they pay school fees, they get up at dawn.
• What a hero does that a child can also do: **tell the truth, help the smaller one, share, stand up when
  somebody is being bullied, finish the work begun.**
• Show and tell this week: each child says **"When I grow up I want to be ____, because ____."**

**Words for my notebook**

The farmer, the teacher, the nurse, the driver and the seller keep a town going. Ìjà̀pá is the clever one of the
stories, and Queen Amina of Zaria is remembered as a warrior queen who made the roads safe. A hero tells the
truth and finishes the work begun.

**Let us talk**

1. Which worker feeds the whole town?
2. What did the story say Queen Amina did for the traders and the towns?
3. Which things does Ìjà̀pá use to get on in the stories?
4. Say who you want to be when you grow up and why.

**Worksheet**

1. The person whose work makes sure the whole town eats is the ____ . A) farmer B) driver C) guard D) barber
2. In the stories of our people, the small animal that out-thinks the bush is ____ . A) the elephant B) the tortoise C) the lion D) the bird
3. Queen Amina of Zaria is remembered as ____ . A) a warrior queen B) a market seller C) a boat man D) a drum maker
4. Which of these is a thing a child can do and be a hero for it? A) running fast home B) hiding a mistake C) telling the truth D) taking a big share
5. Fill in: The people who put out a fire are the ____ .
6. Name six workers who keep a town going.
7. Write two sentences on the work you want to do when you grow up and why you want it.

**My own work**

Draw the helper you want to be when you grow big — a nurse, a farmer, a driver, a teacher — and give the figure
one thing that person carries; colour the whole drawing.

### WEEK 9 — END-OF-YEAR QUIZ GAMES AND SHOW AND TELL

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · General Knowledge · Third Term W9.*

**You will learn to**

• answer quiz questions about what we have learnt all year
• play "Who am I?" and "Where does it live?"
• stand and show one thing to the class
• say what we are going to do next year

**Things to know**

• The quiz cards of this year carry five pictures: the **flag**, an **animal**, a **colour**, a **food** and a
  **helper**.
• In **"Who am I?"** the class is told three things and guesses: *"I give light and warmth in the day"* — the
  sun.
• In **"Where does it live?"** an animal is named and the child says its home: monkey — the **forest**;
  fish — the **water**; antelope — the **grassland**; hen — the **house yard**.
• **Show and tell**: each child brings **one thing** — a stone from the road, a fruit of the season, a photo of
  the family, a basket from the market — and says three lines about it.
• Three lines are enough: **"This is ____. It comes from ____. I like it because ____."**
• We sing the year's songs once more in a ring, and we say the rhymes in the order we learnt them.
• In the **GK games** the class takes two teams; a team that answers quietly gets the point, and a team that
  shouts does not.
• At the end we say it together: **"I am ready for Primary 1."** — new bag, new book, bigger school.
• What we take with us: the greetings, the counting, the names of the tools, the water rule, the flag, and the
  way we treat people who are different from us.

**Words for my notebook**

I answered the quiz about the flag, the animals, the colours, the foods and the helpers. I stood and showed one
thing to the class and said three lines about it. I am ready for Primary 1.

**Let us talk**

1. What do we say when a team wants a point in the quiz game?
2. Name an animal and say where it lives.
3. What are the three lines of a show-and-tell?
4. Say two things you will take with you into Primary 1.

**Worksheet**

1. In "Where does it live?", a monkey lives ____ . A) in the sea B) in the trees of the forest C) in a house D) at the motor park
2. The first line of a show-and-tell is ____ . A) This is … B) I am sorry C) Good morning D) How are you
3. Which of these is NOT one of our five quiz pictures? ____ . A) the flag B) a colour C) a planet D) a helper
4. A team gets the point in the GK game when it ____ . A) shouts first B) answers quietly C) takes the card D) sings last
5. Fill in: At the end of the day we say, "I am ready for Primary ____ ."
6. Name five things the quiz cards of this year carried.
7. Write your three show-and-tell lines about one thing you brought.

**My own work**

Make one quiz card: on one side draw an animal of our land, on the other side write its name, where it lives
and one thing it eats. Colour the animal and read your card to the class.
