### WEEK 1 — MAKING CARDS: PAPER THAT CARRIES A MESSAGE

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Creativity · Third Term W9 (pp 572–575).*

**Main content**

• A **card** is a small piece of stiff paper we **fold** and give to somebody to carry a **message**.
• A card says what the mouth finds hard to say: **thank you**, **I am sorry**, **well done**, **get
  well soon**.
• Kinds of card we know: a **birthday card**, a **thank-you card**, a **get-well card**, a
  **congratulations card**, an **invitation card** and a **holiday card**.
• We make the card by **folding** the paper once, in the middle, so it has a **front**, an **inside**
  and a **back**.
• The **front** carries a drawing or a picture; the **inside** carries the words.
• Words for the inside are kept **short**: "Happy birthday, Mama." "Thank you for the rice."
• We decorate with **crayon**, **colouring**, **cut-out paper**, **stickers**, a **ribbon** or a
  little **glitter**.
• Glitter and paste go on the **front**, never inside where the words are written.
• A card is **given with two hands** and is opened by the person it was written for.
• A card is art and speech together: the picture shows the feeling, the words say it.

**Words for my notebook**

A card is folded stiff paper that carries a short message and a picture. We make a birthday, a
thank-you or a get-well card, then write inside and decorate the front.

**Let us talk**

1. Whose birthday did you recently celebrate?
2. What would you write inside a get-well card?
3. Which part of the card carries the drawing — the front or the inside?
4. Show me how you fold one paper into a card.


![A girl holds open a folded card with a cake drawn on the front; scissors, a glue stick and a ribbon lie on the table.](assets/img/nursery-2/3rd-term/cultural-and-creative-arts/week1-making-a-card.png)

3. The words of a card are written on the ____ of it. A) inside B) back C) edge D) fold
4. A card carries a message and a ____ . A) price B) name of a shop C) picture D) key
5. Fill in: The words of a card are written on the ____ of it.
6. Mention two kinds of card you know.
7. Write two sentences on a card you made and the person you gave it to.

**My own work**

### WEEK 2 — COLOUR SPLASH AND FEATHER ART

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Creativity · Third Term W10 (pp 572–575).*

**Main content**

• **Splash art** is colour thrown from a **wet brush** onto paper; the drops make marks no hand could draw.
• To splash, hold the brush **over** the paper and tap it gently with a finger — never at your neighbour.
• A **comb** or a toothbrush combed through the wet colour makes a **fine spray** of little dots.
• Cover the table with newspaper first; splash art goes where it is **meant** to go, not everywhere.
• More colour on the brush makes a **heavy** splash; a nearly dry brush makes a **light**, thin one.
• **Feather art** uses soft feathers stuck on paper with paste to make a picture.
• Feathers give a picture **texture** — it feels soft and raised, not flat like crayon.
• Pictures we can make from feathers: a **bird**, a **peacock** with a long tail, an **angel's wing**, a
  heap of **grass** in the wind.
• A feather stuck **on its edge** looks like a line; stuck **flat** it looks like a wing.
• Feathers are **natural things**, so we handle them gently and do not pull them from a living bird.

**Words for my notebook**

Splash art is colour tapped off a wet brush, and feather art is soft feathers pasted flat on paper to
give a picture texture and a raised edge.

**Let us talk**

1. What falls on the paper when you tap a wet brush?
2. How do we keep splash off the table and our clothes?
3. Does a feather feel smooth, rough or soft?
4. Which bird can we make from feathers on paper?


![A boy taps a toothbrush over paper so dots of paint fall on it, with three feathers and a jar of paste beside it.](assets/img/nursery-2/3rd-term/cultural-and-creative-arts/week2-splash-and-feathers.png)
**Worksheet**

1. We get a fine spray of colour by tapping a wet brush over a ____ . A) cup B) floor C) comb D) shoe
2. A picture with feathers feels ____ to the finger. A) soft and raised B) hot C) wet D) sharp
3. Feathers are best stuck on paper with ____ . A) water B) mud C) a stone D) paste
4. Heavy splash colour comes from a brush that is ____ . A) dry B) very wet with colour C) clean D) small
5. Fill in: Tapping a wet brush over paper makes ____ art.
6. Mention two pictures we can make with feathers on paper.
7. Write two sentences on how you kept the colour off the table.

**My own work**

Splash colour on one half of a paper, then paste three feathers on the other half to make a bird's tail.

### WEEK 3 — WHAT ART IS AND WHAT WE MAKE IT WITH

*Depth: NERDC Primary 1 · Cultural and Creative Arts · First Term W1 (pp 662–663).*

**Main content**

• **Art** is making something **beautiful** or something that **shows what we feel or think**.
• Art is all around us: on **cloth**, on **walls**, on **pots**, on **doors**, on the **floor mats**.
• Kinds of art we do in class: **drawing**, **painting**, **colouring** and **craft** (making things by hand).
• Materials: **pencils**, **crayons**, **chalk**, **water colours**, **brushes**, **paper**, **cardboard**,
  **glue** and **scissors**.
• A **crayon** gives a soft thick line; a **pencil** gives a thin light one; **chalk** works on the floor.
• A **brush** is held near the metal ring, not at the end, so the hand can steer it.
• Colours used for a picture: **red**, **blue**, **yellow**, **green**, **brown**, **black**, **white**.
• A **smiling face** drawn big and round shows joy; a face with a dropped mouth shows sadness.
• Looking at an artwork means saying **what we see**, then **what it reminds us of**.
• Art is not only for the quick child; every finished picture in this class is kept and looked at.

**Words for my notebook**

Art is making beautiful things with our hands and eyes — drawing, painting, colouring and craft — with
pencils, crayons, brushes, colour, paper and scissors.

**Let us talk**

1. Point to something in this room that was made beautiful on purpose.
2. Which art material makes a soft thick line?
3. Draw a face that looks happy and a face that looks sorry.
4. What do you do with a brush before you dip it in colour?


![Crayons in a box, a brush in a jar, a folded paper fan and a clay pot on a shelf, with a girl holding up her drawing.](assets/img/nursery-2/3rd-term/cultural-and-creative-arts/week3-art-materials.png)
**Worksheet**

1. A picture drawn with a pencil is made with the hand and the ____ . A) nose B) feet C) ears D) eyes
2. Which of these is a craft? A) singing B) folding paper into a boat C) running D) sleeping
3. The material that gives a soft thick line is a ____ . A) ruler B) needle C) crayon D) spoon
4. To show joy we can draw a face that is ____ . A) sleeping B) crying C) blank D) smiling
5. Fill in: Drawing, painting, colouring and ____ are kinds of art.
6. Mention three materials you use for art.
7. Write two sentences on a picture you saw on cloth or on a wall.

**My own work**

Draw two faces on one paper — one happy, one sad — and colour the happy one with two bright colours.

### WEEK 4 — SCRIBBLING, LINES AND SHAPES

*Depth: NERDC Primary 1 · Cultural and Creative Arts · First Term W2 (pp 662–663).*

**Main content**

• **Scribbling** is the hand moving the pencil freely; it makes the hand strong for writing.
• First we scribble **all over** the paper; then we scribble **only inside** a drawn ring.
• A **straight line** goes from one place to another without bending — like a piece of stick.
• A **curved line** bends like a smile, like a road going round a hill, like a banana.
• A **zigzag line** goes up and down sharply, like teeth, like the mountain in the distance.
• A **circle** is a round line whose ends meet; we say "one round line, no corner".
• A **square** has **four corners** and four like sides; a **triangle** has **three corners** and three sides.
• Things with a circle in them: a **plate**, a **ball**, the **sun**, a **clock**.
• Things with a square or a rectangle in them: a **window**, a **book**, a **mat**, a **box**.
• To **join dots**, the pencil starts at one dot and walks to the next without lifting off the paper.

**Words for my notebook**

We scribble first on the whole paper, then only inside a line. Straight, curved and zigzag lines make
circles, squares and triangles, and dots joined with no lift finish the picture.

**Let us talk**

1. Show me a zigzag line in the air with your finger.
2. How many corners does a triangle have?
3. Name two round things you saw on your way here.
4. Which is easier to draw, a circle or a square?


![A straight line, a curved line and a zigzag above a circle, a square and a triangle, with dots joined into a fish.](assets/img/nursery-2/3rd-term/cultural-and-creative-arts/week4-lines-and-shapes.png)
**Worksheet**

1. A line that goes up and down sharply is a ____ line. A) zigzag B) flat C) quiet D) long
2. A circle is a round line whose ends ____ . A) stay apart B) cross twice C) meet D) point
3. How many corners does a square have? A) one B) four C) two D) three
4. Before joining dots, the pencil must not ____ . A) touch paper B) hold chalk C) be black D) lift off
5. Fill in: A square has ____ corners and four like sides.
6. Mention one round thing and one square thing you saw today.
7. Write two sentences on the lines you drew — straight, curved and zigzag.

**My own work**

Fill one paper with scribble, then draw a big circle, a square and a triangle inside it and colour only the triangle.

### WEEK 5 — MAKING LETTERS AND NUMBERS BEAUTIFUL

*Depth: NERDC Primary 1 · Cultural and Creative Arts · First Term W3 (pp 662–663).*

**Main content**

• A letter can be **art** as well as writing: we draw it **big** and leave it **hollow** to colour in.
• To make a hollow letter, trace a big letter twice, once beside itself, then join the tops and the feet.
• We decorate the outline with **dots**, **small circles**, **zigzags** or **little leaves** drawn on it.
• Colour stays **inside** the letter; the line of the letter is left whole and dark.
• Numbers can be decorated too: **1** with a hat, **2** with a boat's sail, **8** with two round plates.
• A **pattern** is marks that **repeat**: dot, line, dot, line — or 1, 2, 1, 2, 1, 2.
• A **collage** letter is made by **pasting** small bits of coloured paper, seeds or beads on the letter.
• Paste is spread **thin** with a finger or a stick, and the paper is **pressed flat**, not rubbed.
• Our own name is the best letter to decorate, because we can read every one of its letters.
• Artistic writing means the letter is still **readable** after we have made it beautiful.

**Words for my notebook**

We draw big hollow letters and numbers, colour inside them, decorate the edge with dots and lines, and
paste paper bits to make a pattern that can still be read.

**Let us talk**

1. Which letter of your name is the roundest?
2. What repeats in the pattern: dot, line, dot, line?
3. Show me how you press a pasted paper bit flat.
4. Which number looks like two plates on top of each other?


![A boy presses torn bits of paper onto a big star outline, with a jar of paste and a heap of paper scraps beside him.](assets/img/nursery-2/3rd-term/cultural-and-creative-arts/week5-paper-collage.png)
**Worksheet**

1. A hollow letter is one we can ____ inside. A) tear B) colour C) fold D) erase
2. Dots on a line of a letter are there to make it ____ . A) heavy B) wet C) beautiful D) new
3. A pattern of dot, line, dot, line is made of marks that ____ . A) melt B) disappear C) stand still D) repeat
4. A letter covered with pasted paper bits is called a ____ . A) collage B) song C) plate D) dream
5. Fill in: A pattern is made of marks that ____ .
6. Mention two ways to decorate the line of a letter.
7. Write two sentences on the letter you decorated and the colour you used.

**My own work**

Draw the first letter of your name big and hollow, colour inside it, then put dots all along its line.

### WEEK 6 — COLOURS: THE THREE THAT MAKE THE OTHERS

*Depth: NERDC Primary 1 · Cultural and Creative Arts · First Term W4 and W7 (pp 662–663).*

**Main content**

• A **colour** is what makes things look **red**, **blue**, **yellow** or **green** to the eye.
• The **primary colours** are **red**, **blue** and **yellow**; no two colours can be mixed to make them.
• Two primary colours mixed give a **secondary colour**.
• **Red + yellow = orange** (the colour of a ripe palm fruit and of sunset).
• **Yellow + blue = green** (the colour of a fresh leaf and of the grass).
• **Red + blue = purple** (the colour of a ripe plum and of some beans).
• Other colours we use: **black**, **white**, **brown**, **pink** and **grey**.
• In our surroundings: the **sky** is blue, the **grass** is green, the **sun** is yellow, a **ripe tomato**
  is red, the **soil** after rain is brown.
• Much red with a little white makes **pink**; much black with a little white makes **grey**.
• Before two colours are mixed on paper, the brush is **rinsed** in the water cup, or the mix turns muddy.

**Words for my notebook**

Red, blue and yellow are primary colours. Mixing two of them gives orange, green or purple, and the rest
of the colours we see are around us in the sky, the leaves and the soil.

**Let us talk**

1. What colour is the sky at noon today?
2. Which two colours must I mix to get green?
3. Point to something brown in this room.
4. What colour is a ripe orange — the fruit?


![A girl mixes paint in a small dish with a brush; three open jars, their lids and a cup of water stand in a row.](assets/img/nursery-2/3rd-term/cultural-and-creative-arts/week6-mixing-colours.png)
**Worksheet**

1. Which colour is a primary colour? A) green B) orange C) purple D) yellow
2. Red and yellow mixed together give ____ . A) green B) orange C) black D) blue
3. The colour of a fresh leaf is ____ . A) green B) white C) red D) grey
4. How many primary colours are there? A) one B) eight C) three D) two
5. Fill in: Red and blue mixed together give ____ .
6. Mention the three primary colours.
7. Write two sentences on colours you saw outside on your way to school.

**My own work**

### WEEK 8 — CRAFTS: MAKING THINGS WITH OUR HANDS

*Depth: NERDC Primary 1 · Cultural and Creative Arts · First Term W8 (pp 662–663).*

**Main content**

• A **craft** is a thing we **make with our hands**, usually something we can use or wear.
• Crafts we know in class: **paper craft**, **clay modelling** and **bead work**.
• Materials for craft: **paper**, **cardboard**, **glue**, **scissors**, **clay**, **thread**, **beads**,
  **old bottle caps**.
• Folding a paper once, corner to corner or edge to edge, gives **two halves**; folding again gives
  **four quarters**.
• A crease is made by running a **fingernail** along the fold, so the paper keeps the shape.
• A **paper boat**, a **hat** and an **aeroplane** are all made from one folded paper.
• **Clay modelling**: roll clay on the table into a **snake**, then coil it into a **pot**; keep the clay
  cool and covered with a damp cloth.
• **Bead work**: beads are threaded on a strong thread, one after another, to make a **necklace** or a
  **bracelet**.
• Scissors are carried with the **blades closed** and the **handles forward** to the person you hand them to.
• Scissors **cut paper and cloth**; they do not cut hair, thread on a garment, or a neighbour's work.

**Words for my notebook**

A craft is a thing made with our hands: paper folding, clay modelling and bead work. We fold halves and
quarters, press every crease, and carry scissors closed with the handles forward.

**Let us talk**

1. What did you make with clay last time?
2. How many quarters do you get when a paper is folded twice?
3. Which part of the scissors do you hold when you pass them to a friend?
4. Name one thing that scissors must never cut.

### WEEK 9 — DECORATION: MAKING PLAIN THINGS BEAUTIFUL

*Depth: NERDC Primary 1 · Cultural and Creative Arts · First Term W10 (pp 662–663).*

**Main content**

• **Decoration** means making a plain thing **beautiful** by adding colours, patterns or shapes to it.
• We decorate the **classroom walls**, our **notice board**, our **books**, our **name tags** and the **corners** of a drawing.
• A **pattern** repeats: dots, then lines, then small circles; a **border** is a pattern that runs round the edge.
• A **paper flower** is made by cutting **petals** round a circle, folding it, and pressing a **button centre** in it.
• A **garland** is made by stringing **paper cups**, **leaves** or **beads** one after another on a thread.
• For a garland, two **shapes** are placed back to back and the thread runs between them.
• Our country's colours, **green** and **white**, can be used in a garland for a school day.
• A **name tag** carries the name written **big and clear**, with a pattern round the edge.
• Decoration must not **cover what we need to see**: the name stays readable, the border stays outside it.
• Everything used for decoration is **what we find**: paper, leaves, seeds, old cloth, caps, string.

**Words for my notebook**

Decoration makes plain things beautiful. We repeat a pattern to draw a border, cut paper flowers, string
a garland of paper cups, and write our name big on a tag with a pattern round the edge.

**Let us talk**

1. Which wall in this room could use a decoration?
2. What shape do we cut for a paper flower's petals?
3. What repeats in the border you drew today?
4. Why must a name tag still be easy to read?
