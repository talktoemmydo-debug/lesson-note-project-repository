### WEEK 1 — THE TOOLS WE WORK WITH, AND WHERE THEY REST

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Prevocational Studies · Third Term W1.*

**You will learn to**

• name six common farm tools
• say what each tool is used for
• tell the difference between a spade and a hoe
• say where tools rest when work is over

**Things to know**

• A **spade** digs and lifts soil; a **hoe** opens the ground and clears small weeds; a **cutlass** cuts
  grass and stick.
• A **rake** pulls soil smooth and gathers dry leaves; a **watering can** carries water to the young plant;
  a **basket** and a **wheelbarrow** carry what we have cut or dug.
• A spade has a **flat wide blade** and a long handle; a hoe has a **short flat blade** set at an angle.
• A rake has **teeth**; a spade has none — that is how you know them at a glance.
• Tools are **for work**, not for play: a cutlass and a hoe are for a grown-up's hand, and a child carries
  the small watering can or the light basket.
• After work, a tool is **cleaned of mud**, dried, and **hung on the rack** or laid on a shelf with the
  blade down.
• A tool left on the ground in the path is a **trap for the next foot**; a tool on the rack is a tool kept.
• Rust spoons an edge, so tools are kept **dry** and under a roof, not on the wet soil overnight.
• A **damaged handle** is not carried back to the rack; it is shown to a grown-up and mended first.

**Words for my notebook**

A spade digs, a hoe clears weeds, a cutlass cuts, a rake smooths, a watering can brings water, and a basket
or a wheelbarrow carries. After work every tool is cleaned, dried and hung on the rack with its blade down.

**Let us talk**

1. Which tool do we use to pull dry leaves together?
2. Show me how a hoe's blade is different from a spade's.
3. Where does a tool rest when the work is over?
4. Which two things may a child carry on the farm?

### WEEK 2 — WHAT WE MEAN BY SAFETY

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Prevocational Studies · Third Term W2.*

**You will learn to**

• say what safety means
• name three rules we keep on the farm
• tell why the ground is cleared before work
• say what we do with a thing that can hurt

**Things to know**

• **Safety means keeping ourselves and other people from being hurt while we work or play.**
• Safety is not a thing we wear once; it is the **way we do everything** — walking, carrying, putting down.
• Before work begins we **look at the ground**: a stone, a stick, a hole or a peel can send a person down.
• A tool is **pointed away from people** and never swung over a head; the space behind is looked at first.
• Nothing that can cut is **thrown, pushed into the soil, or left in the grass** where a foot will find it.
• When we carry something sharp, a grown-up carries it; the child's hands hold the **watering can, the cup,
  the basket**.
• **Reporting** is part of safety: a broken thing, a spill, or a wasps' nest by the fence is told, not hidden.
• The **store and the fire** have rules too: the insect medicine stays shut and high, and never in a bottle
  that once held water to drink.
• A safe farm is a **working farm**: a cut hand keeps a family from the market for a week.

**Words for my notebook**

Safety means keeping ourselves and others from being hurt. We clear the ground before we work, we point
a tool away from people, we carry sharp things with a grown-up, and we report what is broken or dangerous.

**Let us talk**

1. What does the word safety tell you?
2. What do we look at before we start work on the ground?
3. Who carries a cutlass, and who carries the watering can?
4. Name one thing you would report to a grown-up at once.

### WEEK 3 — WHY SAFETY MATTERS SO MUCH

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Prevocational Studies · Third Term W3.*

**You will learn to**

• say three good things that come from safe work
• tell what one carelessness can cost
• name the people who are hurt by our carelessness
• say why a careful child is trusted with more

**Things to know**

• Safe work **keeps the body whole** — eyes for reading, hands for holding, legs for running.
• It **keeps the work going**: a farm with a hurt worker loses days, and a kitchen with a burnt pot loses a meal.
• It **saves money**: a cloth for a cut and medicine for a fever are dearer than the care that prevented them.
• It **keeps the family glad**: a child who comes home unhurt is the reason the day was good.
• Carelessness does not hurt only the careless one: a swung hoe can reach a smaller child standing behind.
• A careful child is **trusted with more** — first the basket, then the small rake, then bigger work.
• Being quick is good; being **quick and careless** is the slowest way to do a thing.
• Safe work is also **neat work**: things in their place, the ground cleared, the tool cleaned after use.
• We do not brag about a danger we have passed; we tell the class the rule that kept us whole.

**Words for my notebook**

Safety keeps the body whole, keeps the work going, saves money and keeps the family glad. Carelessness can
hurt the one standing behind, so a careful child is the one who is trusted with more work.

**Let us talk**

1. Name two good things that come from working safely.
2. Who else can be hurt by your carelessness?
3. What does a family lose when a worker hurts a hand?
4. Which comes first, being quick or being careful?

### WEEK 4 — WHAT HAPPENS WHEN A TOOL IS MISUSED

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Prevocational Studies · Third Term W4.*

**You will learn to**

• name four dangers on a farm or in a home
• tell what a cracked handle can do
• say why a hole in the ground is marked
• tell what we do with a tool that is broken

**Things to know**

• A **cracked or loose handle** can slip off while the tool is swinging, and the blade goes where nobody
  meant it.
• A **tool left in the grass** is seen by nobody until a foot steps on it; a blade turned down in the soil is
  the same trap.
• An **open hole** — a post hole, a burrow, a washed gully — turns an ankle; the rule is to fill it or
  **mark it with stones or a stick** until it is filled.
• A **wet floor, a peel, or a stone in the path** is a fall waiting; the quick child picks it up, not jumps it.
• A **blade used for the wrong work** — a cutlass to split wood, a knife to open a tin — chips, and a chipped
  blade cuts worse and slips more.
• **Fire and hot ash** are not moved by a child; a pot handle is turned in on the stove, not out into the path.
• A broken tool is **taken up and told about**, not hidden under the rack; the mending is small, the hiding is
  not.
• First aid for a small cut: **clean water on it, a clean cloth over it, and a grown-up told** — no dust and no
  leaf pressed into the wound.

**Words for my notebook**

A cracked handle flies off, a tool in the grass waits for a foot, and a hole in the ground turns an ankle.
We mark the hole, pick up the stone, take the broken tool to be mended, and wash and cover a small cut.

**Let us talk**

1. What can a loose handle do while a man is swinging?
2. What do we do with a hole we cannot fill today?
3. Why is a chipped blade more dangerous than a good one?
4. What are the three steps for a small cut?


![A boy points at a hoe with a crack across its wooden handle, and beside them a hole in the ground is ringed with stones so that no foot falls in.](assets/img/nursery-2/3rd-term/prevocational-studies/week4-the-cracked-handle.png)
**Worksheet**

1. A tool with a cracked handle may ____ . A) lose its handle while it swings B) work faster C) keep better D) stay clean longer
2. A hole in the ground that cannot be filled today is ____ . A) left for the goats B) marked with stones C) hidden with grass D) jumped by everyone
3. A chipped blade is dangerous because it ____ . A) shines less B) is heavier C) slips and cuts worse D) costs more
4. The three steps for a small cut are clean water, a clean cloth and ____ . A) a leaf pressed in B) dust from the floor C) the finger hidden D) a grown-up told
5. Fill in: A broken tool is taken up and ____ , not hidden.

### WEEK 5 — THE KITCHEN, AND WHY A GROWN-UP IS NEEDED

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Prevocational Studies · Third Term W5.*

**You will learn to**

• say what a kitchen is for
• name four dangers found in a kitchen
• tell which jobs need a grown-up
• say how a child can still help in the kitchen

**Things to know**

• The **kitchen** is the room where food is kept, washed and **cooked**; it feeds the whole house.
• Its dangers are quick: **fire, hot water, a hot pot, a sharp knife, a wet floor, and a covered pot that
  spits when the lid is lifted**.
• A **hot surface does not wait** for a small hand; the pot's handle is turned **in**, so a sleeve will not
  catch it.
• Knives are **laid on a shelf or in a rack**, blade away from the edge, never in a basin of water you cannot
  see through.
• The jobs of **fire, boiling, cutting and lifting a hot pot belong to a grown-up**; a child stands back and
  carries what is cool.
• A child helps well by **washing vegetables, carrying a cool cup, setting things on the table, sweeping, and
  fetching** what is asked for.
• **A grown-up is needed** not because a child is slow but because a spill, a burn or a cut is bigger than a
  child's hand can put right.
• If a fire starts in a pot, **the lid is put on and the air taken away**; nobody carries a burning pot
  across the room.
• The floor is wiped at once; **a wet floor sends a person to the wall** — and the wall sends the pot down.

**Words for my notebook**

The kitchen cooks the food and keeps it, and it holds fire, hot water and sharp knives. Those jobs belong to
a grown-up; a child washes, carries what is cool, sweeps, and keeps the floor dry.

**Let us talk**

1. What do we do in a kitchen?
2. Name two things in a kitchen that can hurt quickly.
3. Which kitchen jobs must a child wait for a grown-up to do?
4. Name two ways you help in the kitchen today.

### WEEK 6 — THE RULES WE KEEP WHEN TOOLS ARE ABOUT

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Prevocational Studies · Third Term W6.*

**You will learn to**

• state five rules we keep with tools
• show how a sharp tool is carried
• say why we watch the space behind us
• tell how tools are shared in a class

**Things to know**

• **Never run with a tool**; walk, and keep the blade or the teeth **pointing down**.
• **Never throw a tool** — even to a friend who is near; it is **put into the hand** and the hand is waited for.
• **Never swing a tool over anybody.** Before you swing, look behind you — the handle comes round too,
  and it can hit a person standing there.
• **One tool, one work**: a hoe for the soil, a cutlass for grass, a knife for food.
• **A tool is passed by the handle**, so the receiver's hand is where the grip goes.
• Space is kept: at least **the length of two arms** between anybody working and anybody watching.
• A tool is **not stood in the soil** where it can fall on a foot; it is laid down or hung up.
• In a class we **take turns with the watering can** and never grab it mid-pour.
• Medicine for insects, kerosene and soap are **kept shut, kept high and kept apart from drink and food**.
• A rule is only good when it is **said out loud to the one who forgot it**, kindly and before the accident.

**Words for my notebook**

We walk with a tool and point it down, we pass it by the handle, we put it into a hand instead of throwing
it, and we look behind before we swing. One tool does one work, and two arms' space is kept from anybody
watching.

**Let us talk**

1. Which way should a tool point when it is carried?
2. Why must we look behind before we swing a hoe?
3. How do we give a tool to another person?
4. What do we say when a friend forgets a rule?

### WEEK 8 — CLOTHES AND THINGS THAT PROTECT THE BODY

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Prevocational Studies · Third Term W8.*

**You will learn to**

• name four things worn to keep us safe at work
• say what each one protects
• tell when they must be worn
• say how a child dresses for the farm

**Things to know**

• The things we wear to keep the body safe are called **protective gear**: **gloves, boots, a hat and an
  overall** (the big loose cloth that covers the clothes).
• **Gloves** keep the hands from thorns, blisters from a rough handle, and wet medicine off the skin.
• **Boots** keep the feet from a nail, a stone, water in the gutter, and a tool that slips.
• A **hat** keeps the sun off the head and neck, so the head does not become hot and painful.
• An **overall** keeps the school clothes free of mud and oil, and loose cloth keeps branches from a hand.
• Gear is worn **before** the work begins, not after the first scratch.
• It is **clean and whole**: a glove with a hole lets in what the glove was for.
• A child on the farm wears a **cap, sandals or shoes, and clothes that are not too long**, so the feet will
  not trip on the hem.
• Gear is **not a toy to play with**; the gloves and the hat are for work and are put back on the peg.
• Eyes are kept safe too: nobody looks at the sun, and nobody points a stick where a face is.

**Words for my notebook**

Gloves for the hands, boots for the feet, a hat for the head, and an overall for the clothes are the gear we
put on before work. The gear is clean and whole, and it goes back on its peg when the work is over.

**Let us talk**

1. Which thing keeps a nail or a stone from the foot?
2. Why must a glove have no hole in it?
3. What does a hat keep off the head?
4. What do we do with the gear when work is finished?

### WEEK 9 — PUTTING THINGS AWAY, TELLING WHAT IS BROKEN, AND FIRST AID

*Depth: NERDC Nursery 3 · Prevocational Studies · Third Term W9.*

**You will learn to**

• say how tools and chemicals are stored
• tell who is told about a broken thing
• give first aid for a small cut and for a burn
• say what a store place must keep out

**Things to know**

• A **store** is a dry place, off the ground, where tools, cans and baskets are kept **with the blade down**
  and the sharp edges in.
• Tools are **cleaned of mud and dried** before they go in, or the mud will keep the rust alive.
• Insect medicine, kerosene and acid are kept **shut, high, and marked**; they never go into a bottle that a
  child drinks from.
• A **broken thing is reported** at once to the grown-up in charge, and it is **not used again** until it is
  mended.
• A **small cut**: wash with clean water, press a clean cloth on it, keep the hand up; a grown-up puts a
  cover on it. Dust, leaf and ash are never rubbed into a wound.
• A **small burn**: hold the place under **cool clean water** for a while, cover loosely with a clean cloth,
  and tell a grown-up. No oil, no paste and no leaf is put on a burn.
• A **nosebleed**: sit up, lean **a little forward**, and pinch the soft part of the nose; the head is not
  thrown back.
• First aid is what we do **before** the clinic or the hospital; a deep cut, a bad burn or a fall where the leg
  will not bear weight is taken to a grown-up and to the clinic at once.
• The store and the first-aid box are kept **clean, closed and known** — everybody in the house can point to
  both of them.

**Words for my notebook**

Tools are cleaned, dried and stored with the blade down; medicine is kept shut and high; a broken thing is
reported and not used. A cut is washed and covered, a burn is cooled with clean water, and a grown-up is
told at once.

**Let us talk**

1. What is done to a tool before it is put on the rack?

3. What is put on a small burn first?
4. Why is a nosebleed child made to lean forward?
