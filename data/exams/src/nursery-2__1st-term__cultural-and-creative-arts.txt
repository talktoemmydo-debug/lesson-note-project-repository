# Cultural and Creative Arts — Nursery 2 · First Term · term paper
subject: Cultural and Creative Arts
class: Nursery 2
term: 1st Term

[objective]
The drawing of a car has these four parts || body, windows, two wheels and a headlight || wings, feathers, a beak and a tail || petals, roots, a stem and seeds || pages, a cover, a spine and words
What do wheels do? || They help the car to move || They help the car to sing || They keep the car warm || They light the car inside
We use crayons to || colour inside the lines of a drawing || wash the grease off our hands || cut our hair into shapes || cook rice in the pot
Paint is kept in a || small tin or can || big cooking pot || soft pillow || school shoe
Before we paint, the surface must be || clean and dry || wet and muddy || hot from the sun || covered in dust and sand
Thumb-printing is done with the || thumb of the hand || knee of the leg || tip of the ear || point of the elbow
The pattern made by pressing a thumb on paper is called a || thumb print || hand shake drawing || footprint rubbing || wall shadow
A Barney is a character we || draw and colour || eat || wear on the head || keep in water
The lines that show where a drawing ends are called || outlines || deep shadows || rhyming lines || story verses
In "Barney's environment" we colour the things || around Barney — his home and his friends || only the inside of Barney's big mouth || only what is hidden under Barney's bed || the moon and all the far away stars
A pig has a short tail that is || curly like a spring || feathered like a bird || long like a rope || flat and square
The foot of a pig is called a || trotter || paw with claws || hoof of a horse || fin
The pig, goat and cow are drawn in our lesson because they are || animals kept at home || wild animals of the bush || animals of the deep sea || toys made of plastic
Strands of dried grass or wheat used for making jewellery are || straw || stones || leaves || bones
A craft pattern that goes red, blue, red, blue is called a || repeating pattern || one-off pattern || song pattern || straight line
Which of these can be made out of straw? || A bracelet, a ring and a small cap || A cup filled with cold water || A pencil carved from hard wax || A bed made of iron
An umbrella protects us from || falling rain and hot sunshine || the snake in the bush || our own homework || the noise of the market
The strong cloth stretched over an umbrella is called the || canopy || carrying handle || rubber tip || box it came in
The frame of wire that holds the canopy is called the || skeleton || soft pillow || market basket || car wheel
Vegetable printing uses || the cut surface of an okra || only a painting brush || a wooden eating spoon || a long sewing needle
Before printing with a vegetable, we spread paint on a || flat tray or plate || bucket of water || school shoe || wall mirror
The print left by an okra looks like || a little star or a flower || a long straight road || a plain round ball || a tall storey building
Plasticine, which we mould with our hands, is a soft || coloured clay || sweet red fruit || running tap water || clean cotton cloth
A small ball and a rolled snake-like piece are the first || shapes for modelling || songs for singing || dances for jumping || sums for adding
To join two pieces of plasticine we || press them hard together || blow air on them || drop them twice || sew them with thread
Keeping paint off your uniform while you work is part of || taking good care of materials and tools || wasting the paint and materials || hiding the materials from friends || breaking the tools in anger
A drawing that shows a thing as it really looks is made with || patience and careful shading || only a long ruler || a clean soft rubber || two eyes kept closed
The noise dance we do with simple instruments keeps the body || moving the feet and hands in time with the beat || sitting completely still on the chair || quietly asleep on the mat || making no sound at all
Which of these is NOT a way of making a pattern? || Shouting loudly in class || Repeating the same colours || Alternating shapes and lines || Printing with a leaf
A good artist at the end of an art lesson || washes the brush and lays the work out to dry || leaves the wet paint pot on the floor || hides the crayons under a bench || tears the finished drawing in two
