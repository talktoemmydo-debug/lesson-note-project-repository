[objective]
A leader is somebody who || guides other people and shows them what to do || sells other things to other people || drives a car to the market by himself || cooks the food of the family at home
The leader of my class in school is the || teacher, with the class captain || the watchman at the gate || the cleaner of the corridor || a visitor from town

The leader of a street or community is called the || baale || prefect || monitor || pupil
The leader of a state is the || governor || chairman || king only || mayor of another town
A quality of a good leader is being || kind, fair, honest and helpful || rude, lazy and greedy || noisy, proud and quick to anger || silent, far and forgetful

Titles given to leaders include chief, emir, oba, igwe, chairman and || president || prisoner || passenger || watchman

A duty of a parent is to give food, clothes, education, love and || protection || punishment only || money for games || a motor car
The advice of the grandparents is heavy because they || have seen more of life, and we answer them with respect || give us money at the end of every single month || are older than everybody else in the street || stay in another state with our relations

A community is people living together in || one area — a street, a village or a town || one house with many storeys only || one class in the school building || one market stall on a table
A small settlement is a village; a bigger one is a || town || country || state || ocean
The name of the place I live is said like this: || "I live in ______" || "I sleep in ______" || "I eat at ______" || "I run to ______"
Mobilization means || calling the people together to do the work they must do || sending the people away from the work to be done || paying the people to go back to their houses || writing the names of the people in a big book
An example of community mobilization is || the clean-up exercise on the last Saturday of the month || the long sleep taken in the afternoon sun || the heavy rain that falls in the month of June || the closing of the market at high noon
The leaders who meet to plan for the people include the chief, the imam, the pastor and the || chairman of the local government area || driver of the bus at the park || trader that sells us food || pupil of the school near the market
Cooperation means people || working together to finish a job one person cannot finish alone || fighting one another to finish a job quickly || paying another person to do the whole job || waiting at home for the government to do everything
When a neighbour's house falls down, the community brings || sand, blocks and hands || a new television set || music and dancing shoes || a full pot of soup
When a neighbour's house falls down, the community brings || sand and blocks and working hands || a new television set for the family || music and dancing shoes for the street || a big pot of soup for the children
A strong community gives its people || safety, unity and progress || noise, dust and fear || homework and tests || hunger and thirst
A child's part in the community includes || not littering and keeping the gutters free || throwing papers on the wide road || blocking the gutter with soft mud || frightening the old people at the gate
The proverb of cooperation says || many hands make the work light || one head carries all the load || money finishes the good work || slow and slow wins the race
A responsibility is a thing we || must do, and not a thing we may choose to do || may forget when we are very busy || can buy for ourselves at the market || give to another child in the class
An example of responsibility at school is || coming on time and doing my homework || playing till the bell finishes || taking another pupil's book || hiding under the desk
If I lose or spoil a borrowed book, the consequence is that || it must be replaced or paid for || nothing at all happens || the book returns itself || the owner buys two
Respect for elders is shown by greeting them, listening, obeying and || using their respectful title, such as ma or sir || calling them by a nick name at the gate || answering them back with a loud voice || walking away without saying anything
People of all ages and backgrounds must be respected, including || a person with a disability || only the rich ones || only the old ones || only my own class mate
Polite words we use are please, thank you, sorry and || excuse me || over to you || mind the gap || good night
My country is Nigeria; it is in the || west part of Africa || east of Asia || north of Europe || south of America
Nigeria's neighbours include Benin Republic, Niger, Chad and || Cameroon || Egypt || Ghana || Kenya
The capital of Nigeria is || Abuja || Lagos || Kano || Ibadan
Our national flag has three vertical parts: || green, white and green || red, gold and green || white, blue and white || green, yellow and red
The white on the Nigerian flag stands for || peace and unity || water || milk || morning
The flag was designed by Michael Akinkunmi in || 1959 || 1914 || 1960 || 2000
Nigeria became independent on the || 1st of October 1960 || 29th of May 1999 || 1st of January 1960 || 27th of October 1958
The flag is flown at half a mast when || a great person dies || the market is closed || school begins in January || it is raining hard
The first stanza of our anthem begins "Arise, O || compatriots" || countrymen of Kano || children at school || leaders of the town"
The national pledge is said at school to || be faithful to the country and to honour Nigeria || greet the visitors that come to the school || sing before the match on Friday || open the market for the traders
The coat of arms has two horses, a shield with a black band in a Y-shape and || a red eagle on top || a green tree below || a white cap above || a golden bell behind
The Y on the shield stands for || the rivers Niger and Benue || the shape of the flag || the two horses || the capital city
