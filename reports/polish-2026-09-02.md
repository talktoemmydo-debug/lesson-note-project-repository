# Polish pass on Nursery 2 · First and Second Term — 2 September 2026

Measured, not felt. A new tool, `tools/polish_audit.py`, reads the authored notes (not the rendered books)
and grades them; the builder's `--strict` already refused the school-rule breaches, so this pass worked on
what `--strict` cannot see: reading level, block shape, dead pages with nothing for the hands, and answer
leaks hidden in formatting.

## What was found and fixed

| finding | First Term | Second Term | what was done |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| a note with **nothing for the hands** — no draw, colour, cut, paste or trace anywhere in it | 46 | 44 | **90 cues authored**, one per note, each naming something that note actually teaches (cut-and-paste for sight words, a map of the way to school for the environment week, animal-to-home pairs). They land in `**My own work**`, and the verbs are varied so the book never says "draw and colour" ninety times. |
| a **line over 40 words** in a note the child has to hold in the ear | 1 | 45 | split at the line's own mid-point (36 in the first pass, 13 in the second), two long chain-lists rewritten as read-aloud clauses, one by hand. No word of the author's was replaced — only punctuation and clause order. |
| **grown-up register** words (`in order to`, `however`, `particular`, `…`) | 1 | 3 | reworded into what a five-year-old says: "to grow", "no matter how funny it sounds", "the one thing you mean". |
| a **bullet repeated** in two weeks of one subject | 1 | 0 | reworded the second one (it is now "three sight words you have not used yet"). |
| a **bolded option inside a worksheet** — which prints the answer | 0 | 0 | found while authoring Third Term, prevented hereafter: the audit now refuses it as a defect. |

**After: 0 defects in both terms.** What remains is advisory and deliberately *not* silenced:
105 lines of 33–40 words and 15 notes whose "Things to know" runs 13–15 bullets in Second Term
(First Term: 17 and 20). They are listed by the tool every run; they are not fixed because they are not
wrong — a full-content week (the seven signs of life, the twelve animal homes) genuinely needs more than
twelve short lines, and splitting them again would break a bullet in half. `notes/README.txt` still
documents the house shape as 8–11 bullets; anything outside it now shows up in a report instead of being
quietly accepted.

## What the polish cost, and what still proves out

* Books grew by **one page each** for the 90 new cue lines: Second Term 142 → **143 pages**, First Term
  123 → **124**.
* Contents still agree with the file: **129 of 129** numbered lines each term; `book_pages.py` says
  `audit : CLEAN` for both, with the tallest planned page at 938–939 pt against a 939 pt budget.
* `build_term_doc.py --strict`: **rule checks: all clear** on both — every teaching week has a note, every
  note has a worksheet and a piece of my own work, no calendar-week note, no grown-up addressee, no answer
  printed anywhere, 13 papers at 30/10/5.
* Key shape unchanged by the polish, as it must be: T2 `A=98 B=99 C=99 D=94`, T1 `A=98 B=93 C=102 D=97`,
  consecutive-repeats 0, ABAB 0.

## One honest footnote about the instrument itself

The first run of `polish_audit.py` reported 767 defects in Second Term. Most were the tool's fault, not
the book's: it counted "Words for my notebook" as bullets when the house writes it as prose; it treated a
bullet full of `·` as one 78-word sentence; it took a revision week's `**What to revise**` as a missing
block; and it flagged "God is our father", "a grown-up sweeps up broken glass" and the worksheet option
"D) cut" as deputation of an adult. All four were fixed in the tool **before** a single note was touched —
otherwise this pass would have "improved" 200 notes that were already right. The rule that comes out of
it: measure with the instrument, then audit the instrument, then measure again.
